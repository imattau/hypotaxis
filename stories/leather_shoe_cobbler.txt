Worn leather soles lay stacked beside Osgood's workbench as he stitched a fresh sole onto a customer's beloved but heavily worn boots, extending the life of a pair that had clearly walked thousands of miles already. Good boots deserved proper repair, not replacement.

His apprentice, Cassandra, prepared the next pair waiting in the repair queue, examining a heel that had worn dangerously thin on one side, suggesting the owner's particular walking gait. "This heel's worn unevenly," she said. "Should we mention that when they pick these up?"

Osgood glanced over at the boots she'd indicated, nodding at her observation with the practiced eye of someone who'd seen thousands of worn shoes tell their owners' stories. "Definitely mention it," he said. "Uneven wear like that usually means they need better arch support too."

Cassandra made a note to discuss the finding with the customer and began preparing the replacement heel, cutting the leather to match the boot's original specifications precisely. "I'll flag it on the ticket," she said, reaching for her stitching awl.

Osgood finished his current repair and held the boots up for a final inspection, satisfied with how seamlessly the new sole had integrated with the boot's well-worn upper leather. "Good as new," he said. "Or better, honestly, this sole should outlast the original."
