Warm sugar syrup stretched between the taffy machine's rotating arms as Beatrix watched the candy shift from cloudy to glossy, the transformation that meant it was nearly ready for cutting. The shop's window display always drew a crowd during taffy-pulling hours.

Her shop assistant, Osgood, prepared the cutting table with parchment paper, arranging the wrapping station for the batch that would soon need packaging before the afternoon rush arrived. "Color's looking perfect," he said, watching through the machine's glass front.

Beatrix checked the taffy's consistency with a practiced eye, timing the pull against the sugar's temperature drop that determined the final texture's chewiness. "Almost there," she said. "We want it glossy but not too stiff, or it won't cut cleanly."

Osgood finished setting up the cutting table and moved to help Beatrix guide the finished taffy rope toward the automated cutter, watching the individual pieces drop steadily onto the waiting tray. "That batch looks perfect," he said, sampling a warm piece straight off the tray.

Beatrix smiled at his approval and began wrapping the first pieces while they were still warm enough to shape properly, the shop filling with the sweet smell of fresh candy. "Best batch this week," she said. "Let's get these wrapped before the after-school crowd arrives."
