Linen thread pulled taut as Cassandra sewed through the signature's fold, rebuilding the binding of a water-damaged family bible that had been passed down through five generations. The client had wept a little when dropping it off, and Cassandra understood why.

Her apprentice, Fitzgerald, prepared the new endpapers at the workbench nearby, matching the paper's texture as closely as possible to the original despite the impossibility of an exact replacement. "The marbled pattern's close," he said. "Not identical, but close enough that most people won't notice."

Cassandra glanced over at his work between stitches, nodding approvingly at the careful color matching he'd achieved with the new sheets. "That'll do nicely," she said. "Some imperfection is honest anyway, this book has earned a little visible history."

Fitzgerald trimmed the endpapers to size and set them aside, moving to help Cassandra hold the signatures steady as she continued the careful sewing process. "How many more signatures before you can start the spine?" he asked, watching her needle move with practiced rhythm.

Cassandra counted the remaining unsewn signatures stacked beside her, calculating the remaining hours of careful work before the book could be reassembled. "Six more," she said. "Should have the spine ready to shape by tomorrow afternoon if we keep this pace."
