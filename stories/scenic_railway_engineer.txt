Steam hissed from the vintage locomotive's valves as Engineer Cornelius checked the boiler pressure gauge one final time before the scenic railway's first departure of the day. Tourist season brought crowds eager to ride the century-old mountain route.

His fireman, Rosalind, shoveled another measure of coal into the firebox, maintaining the steady heat needed to keep steam pressure at the optimal running level throughout the morning's scheduled runs. "Pressure's holding at one-eighty," she called over the engine's rumble.

Cornelius checked his pocket watch against the departure schedule, satisfied they had a few more minutes before the conductor would signal for departure from the platform. "Good," he said. "Keep it steady through the first grade, that's where we need the most reserve."

Rosalind continued feeding the firebox at a measured pace, watching the pressure gauge for any sign it needed adjustment before their scheduled departure time arrived. "She's running smooth today," she said, wiping soot from her forehead.

Cornelius heard the conductor's whistle signal and released the brake, easing the throttle forward as the century-old locomotive began its familiar climb toward the mountain overlook. "Here we go," he said. "Another beautiful day for the mountain route."
