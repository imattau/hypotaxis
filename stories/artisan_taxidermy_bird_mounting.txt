Delicate feathers required Fitzgerald's careful attention as he positioned the pheasant's wings into a lifelike display pose, working the wire armature beneath the skin to capture the bird's natural mid-flight posture. Museum specimens demanded scientific accuracy.

His apprentice, Odalys, prepared the display base, carving foliage details that would match the pheasant's natural habitat according to the museum's reference photographs and scientific documentation. "Base is carved and ready for mounting," she said.

Odalys watched him continue adjusting the wing position, absorbing the subtle wire manipulation that created such a convincing illusion of a bird frozen mid-flight rather than simply posed stiffly. "How do you know when the pose looks natural?" she asked.
