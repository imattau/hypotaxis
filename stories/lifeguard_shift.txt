Sun glared off the pool's surface as Deacon scanned the swimmers from his elevated chair, whistle resting against his chest, sweat already trickling down his back despite the early hour. The community pool had opened just twenty minutes ago, but the deep end was already crowded with kids cannonballing off the edge.

His fellow lifeguard, Priscilla, walked the pool's perimeter with a first aid kit slung over one shoulder, occasionally blowing her whistle at a kid running too fast along the wet concrete. "Watch the running, guys," she called out, not unkindly. "Wet feet and concrete don't mix."

Deacon's eyes snapped to a small commotion near the diving board, a young boy struggling to keep his head above water after a rough entry. He was down from the chair and into the pool before the whistle even finished its shrill note, cutting through the water with quick, efficient strokes.

Priscilla was right behind him at the pool's edge, ready to help pull the coughing but otherwise fine boy up onto the concrete once Deacon reached him. "You're okay, you're okay," Deacon said steadily, keeping the boy calm as Priscilla checked him over for any signs of real distress.

Once the boy was settled with his relieved mother, breathing normally and already asking to get back in the water, Deacon climbed back into his chair, heart still hammering from the adrenaline. Priscilla gave him a small nod of acknowledgment before resuming her rounds along the pool's edge.
