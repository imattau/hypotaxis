Steam rose from the dye vat as Josefina lifted the soaked skeins with wooden tongs, checking the indigo's depth of color against the sample card pinned above her workstation. Natural dyeing took patience most modern dyers had forgotten, but the resulting blues were worth the extra care.

Her apprentice, Cassius, prepared the next batch of fiber, pre-soaking it in the mordant bath that would help the natural dye bind properly to the wool fibers. "This skein looks perfect," he said, holding up a sample from an earlier dip. "Exactly the shade you were describing yesterday."

Josefina examined the skein he held up, nodding in satisfaction at the rich, even blue that had developed through the careful oxidation process. "That's the one," she said. "Now we need eleven more skeins matching that exact depth of color."

Cassius lowered another batch of pre-soaked wool into the indigo vat, timing the dip carefully according to the schedule Josefina had taught him over the past several months. "Same timing as the last batch?" he asked, watching the vat's surface for the telltale color changes.

Josefina nodded and watched the submerged wool begin its transformation, the chemistry of oxidation slowly working its magic beneath the vat's dark surface. "Exactly the same," she said. "Consistency is everything when you're trying to match an existing commission."
