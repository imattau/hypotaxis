Vinegar-sharp scent filled the small brewery as Xiomara checked the SCOBY floating atop another batch of fermenting kombucha, testing the tea's acidity level before deciding whether this batch was ready for bottling. Commercial kombucha demanded precise fermentation timing.

Her assistant brewer, Ferdinand, prepared the flavor additions for today's bottling run, measuring precise amounts of ginger and fruit puree that would create the secondary fermentation's characteristic carbonation. "Ginger's measured for twelve bottles," he said.

Xiomara tested the current batch's pH one final time, satisfied with the reading that confirmed proper fermentation had occurred without the batch becoming either too sweet or too vinegary. "Perfect acidity," she said. "Let's get this into bottles before it develops further."

Ferdinand began filling bottles with the base kombucha, leaving appropriate headspace for the flavor additions and the pressure that would build during secondary fermentation. "Bottles are filling steadily," he reported, monitoring the process closely.

Xiomara added the measured ginger to each bottle before Ferdinand sealed them, working together through the practiced routine that had become second nature after years running this small commercial operation. "Two weeks to carbonate," she said. "Then these are ready for the market."
