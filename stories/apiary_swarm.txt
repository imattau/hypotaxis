A low roaring hum filled the orchard as Thaddea watched the swarm cluster thick and dark against the low branch of an old pear tree, thousands of bees clumped together around their queen. The call had come in an hour ago from a worried homeowner two streets over.

Her apprentice beekeeper, Cormac, set the empty swarm box down carefully beneath the branch, double-checking the latch mechanism before stepping back to give Thaddea room to work. "That's a big one," he said. "Must be twenty thousand bees at least, maybe more."

Thaddea nodded and positioned the box directly beneath the cluster, moving slowly and deliberately to avoid startling the colony before she was ready. "Hand me the branch clippers," she said. "I want to cut this branch clean and lower the whole cluster straight into the box."

Cormac passed the clippers over, watching nervously as Thaddea reached up toward the branch, the hum of the swarm rising slightly at the disturbance before settling again. She made the cut in one smooth motion and lowered the branch carefully into the waiting box below.

The bulk of the swarm dropped into the box with a soft, heavy thump, and within minutes the scattered stragglers began marching in to rejoin their queen inside. "That's the easy part done," Thaddea said, securing the lid. "Now we just need to get them home before dark."
