Cedar shavings curled from Bartholomew's carving knife as he shaped another fishing lure's body, working toward the precise profile that would give this design its distinctive swimming action once painted and rigged. Handmade lures commanded serious prices from collectors.

His grandson, Osei, visiting for the summer, painted the completed lure bodies with meticulous detail, replicating the natural minnow patterns that made Bartholomew's lures so effective on the water. "This one's scales look really realistic," he said.

Bartholomew examined the painted lure, admiring the detail his grandson had achieved despite the small scale requiring genuinely delicate brushwork. "Beautiful work," he said. "You've really developed an eye for this."

Osei set the finished lure aside to dry and reached for the next unpainted body Bartholomew had prepared, settling into the productive rhythm they'd established over the summer's shared workshop time. "How many more today?" he asked.

Bartholomew counted the remaining unpainted lure bodies stacked on his workbench, calculating how many they could realistically finish before dinner called them inside. "Maybe six more," he said. "Then we've earned a break, don't you think?"
