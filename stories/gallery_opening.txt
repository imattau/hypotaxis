Wine glasses clinked softly against the murmur of conversation as Camille adjusted the final painting's frame, stepping back to check it hung perfectly level under the gallery's track lighting. Opening night guests were already trickling through the front doors in small clusters.

Her gallery assistant, Idris, approached with a clipboard and an anxious expression. "The critic from the Herald just arrived," he murmured, glancing toward the entrance where a sharply dressed older man was already studying the first piece with a scrutinizing frown.

"Then let's hope he likes what he sees," Camille said, smoothing the front of her blazer. She took a steadying breath and moved to greet a cluster of guests who had paused before her largest canvas, a swirling abstract piece she'd spent the better part of a year completing.

"The color work is extraordinary," one guest said, tilting her head as she studied the layered brushstrokes. Camille smiled and began explaining her process, gesturing toward the canvas's lower corner where the composition had originated from a single accidental spill of paint.

Across the room, Idris watched the critic move from painting to painting, notebook in hand, his expression giving nothing away. When the man finally paused before the swirling abstract centerpiece for nearly a full minute, Idris caught Camille's eye and gave the smallest, most hopeful shrug he could manage without drawing attention.
