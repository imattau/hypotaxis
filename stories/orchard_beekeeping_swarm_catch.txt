Late spring sunlight filtered through the apple blossoms as Cordelia approached the low-hanging swarm cluster, bee suit zipped tight despite the calm, docile behavior swarms typically displayed before finding a new home. Swarm season kept beekeepers busy every warm afternoon.

Her neighbor, Fitzwilliam, watched from a respectful distance, having called her the moment he'd spotted the buzzing cluster in his own backyard tree that morning. "Never seen anything like it," he said. "Thought my tree was about to fall apart from the sound."

Cordelia positioned her collection box beneath the branch, moving with the slow, deliberate calm that swarm collection always demanded regardless of experience level. "They're actually pretty gentle right now," she said. "No hive to defend yet, just looking for a new home."

Fitzwilliam watched with fascinated nervousness as Cordelia gave the branch a firm, controlled shake, dropping the bulk of the cluster directly into her waiting box below. "That's incredible," he said, taking a small step backward despite his curiosity.

Cordelia secured the box's lid once most of the stragglers had marched inside to rejoin their queen, satisfied with another successful swarm capture. "Free bees," she said, grinning. "Thanks for the call, this is exactly how I like to grow my apiary."
