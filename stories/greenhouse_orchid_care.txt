Humid air clung to Theodosia's skin as she misted the rows of orchids lining the greenhouse's specialized growing benches, checking each plant's root health through the transparent pots that revealed their delicate system. The collection had taken fifteen years to build.

Her greenhouse assistant, Fitzgerald, prepared a fresh batch of orchid bark mix, measuring the specific ratio of components that different orchid varieties in the collection required for proper drainage. "The phalaenopsis need repotting soon," he said. "Roots are getting crowded."

Theodosia moved to examine the plants he'd mentioned, gently checking the root systems through the clear pots for the telltale signs of overcrowding that justified his assessment. "You're right," she said. "Let's repot the worst three today, the rest can wait another month."

Fitzgerald prepared the repotting station, laying out fresh bark mix and clean pots while Theodosia carefully removed the first orchid from its current container for inspection. "This one's roots look healthy despite the crowding," she observed, examining the plant closely.

Theodosia repotted the orchid into its new, larger container, settling the roots carefully into the fresh bark mix before watering it gently. "One down, two to go," she said. "These orchids have given us fifteen years of beautiful blooms, the least we can do is keep them comfortable."
