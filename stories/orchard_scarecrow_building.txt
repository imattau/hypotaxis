Straw dust drifted through the barn as Guillermina stuffed the scarecrow's burlap torso, packing it tightly enough to hold its shape against the season's expected winds across the cornfield. The birds had been getting bolder every week.

Her son, Balthazar, home from school for the afternoon, painted a weathered face onto the scarecrow's burlap head, adding details that he hoped would look properly menacing to the crop-raiding birds. "Should I make the eyes bigger?" he asked.

Guillermina glanced over at his work between handfuls of straw, considering the question with the patient attention she gave most of her son's creative projects. "Bigger eyes might help," she said. "Birds respond to that predator-like staring, apparently."

Balthazar enlarged the painted eyes as she'd suggested, stepping back periodically to judge the overall effect from a distance that approximated how the birds might actually view the finished scarecrow. "That's definitely more intimidating," he said.

Guillermina finished stuffing the torso and began attaching the limbs, working toward completing the scarecrow before evening when they planned to position it in the cornfield. "Let's get this out there before dark," she said. "Give those birds something to think about."
