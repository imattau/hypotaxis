Tiny gears glinted under the loupe as Henrietta positioned the escapement wheel with tweezers, her hands perfectly steady despite the hours already spent hunched over the antique pocket watch. The client had inherited it from his grandfather and wanted it running again before the man's upcoming ninetieth birthday.

Her apprentice, Fitzgerald, watched from the next bench over, his own practice movement disassembled in neat rows across a felt tray. "How do you keep your hands that steady after so many hours?" he asked, flexing his own fingers, which had started trembling an hour ago.

Henrietta didn't look up, focused entirely on seating the delicate wheel into its jewel bearing. "Practice, mostly," she said. "And knowing when to stop. Tired hands ruin more watches than difficult repairs ever do." She finally set down her tweezers and rolled her shoulders.

Fitzgerald took the hint and covered his own practice movement, deciding his trembling fingers had done enough damage for one afternoon. "I dropped a screw into the carpet twice today," he admitted. "Spent twenty minutes finding it both times."

Henrietta chuckled and reached for her cleaning cloth, wiping down her tools before setting the finished watch aside to test its timekeeping overnight. "Everyone loses screws into carpets," she said. "Buy a rubber mat for your bench, it'll save you hours over the years."
