Morning fog clung low to the paddock as Rosalind carried the feed bucket toward the emu enclosure, the large birds already crowding the fence line in anticipation of their breakfast. Five years of emu farming had taught her to respect their surprising speed.

Her farmhand, Cornelius, checked the water troughs along the fence, refilling the one that had run low overnight while keeping a careful eye on the nearest bird's curious pecking at his boots. "This one's getting braver every week," he said, stepping back slightly.

Rosalind distributed the feed along the trough, watching the emus jostle for position with their characteristic mix of clumsiness and surprising grace. "That's Clarence," she said. "He's always been the bold one, ever since he was a chick."

Cornelius finished refilling the water and moved to check the fence line for any damage from the previous night's wind, walking the perimeter with practiced thoroughness. "Fence looks solid," he called back. "No breaches to worry about today."

Rosalind finished distributing the feed and stepped back from the fence, watching the emus settle into their breakfast with visible satisfaction at another successful morning routine. "Good," she said. "Let's check on the incubator next, those eggs should hatch any day now."
