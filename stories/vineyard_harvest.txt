Morning fog still clung to the vine rows as Estefania moved down the line with practiced speed, clippers snapping through stems as clusters of dark grapes tumbled into the bucket strapped to her waist. The harvest window was narrow this year, and every hand on the property was out working before the sun fully cleared the ridge.

Her farmhand, Nikolai, worked the row beside her, dumping his filled bucket into the larger crate at the row's end before returning for another pass. "Sugar levels tested perfect this morning," he said. "Another day of waiting and we'd have risked losing this batch to the weather."

Estefania nodded without pausing her clipping, moving steadily down her row with the efficient rhythm of someone who'd done this every autumn for fifteen years. "Good thing we started early then," she said. "Keep the pace up, we want this whole block picked before that storm front moves in tonight."

Nikolai emptied another bucket and glanced up at the gray clouds building on the horizon, quickening his pace slightly despite the ache already settling into his lower back. "Think we'll make it?" he asked, snapping another cluster free and dropping it into his bucket.

Estefania straightened briefly to survey the remaining rows, calculating the math of daylight against workers against vines still waiting to be picked. "We'll make it," she said, bending back to her work. "We don't really have another option today."
