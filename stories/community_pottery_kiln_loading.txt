Bisqueware clinked softly as Persimmon arranged the community studio's glazed pieces onto the kiln shelves, spacing each piece carefully to prevent glaze drips from fusing neighboring work together. Monthly firing day always brought a flurry of last-minute submissions.

Her fellow studio member, Osgood, checked each piece's glaze application for any obvious problems before Persimmon placed it on the shelf, catching a few pieces that needed another coat before firing. "This bowl's glaze looks too thin," he said, holding it up to the light.

Persimmon examined the bowl he'd flagged, agreeing with his assessment after checking the coverage against the studio's usual standards for this particular glaze. "You're right," she said. "Set it aside, we'll fire it next month after another coat."

Osgood continued checking pieces as Persimmon loaded the shelves methodically, working to maximize the kiln's capacity while maintaining proper spacing between each piece. "That's the last of the acceptable pieces," he said, glancing at the nearly full shelves.

Persimmon closed the kiln's door and set the firing schedule, satisfied with how efficiently they'd loaded this month's substantial batch of community work. "Twelve hours to cone six," she said. "By tomorrow evening, everyone's pieces will finally be done."
