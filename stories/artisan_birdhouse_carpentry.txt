Cedar boards lay measured across Ferdinand's workbench as he cut the birdhouse's angled roof pieces, working toward the precise dimensions that would properly shed rain while fitting the specific bird species this particular design targeted. Backyard conservation demanded attention to detail.

His granddaughter, Xiomara, visiting for the weekend, sanded the completed body pieces smooth, removing any rough edges that might snag feathers or otherwise harm the birds who would eventually call this structure home. "These edges feel really smooth now," she said.

Ferdinand examined her sanding work, running his own weathered hand across the surface to confirm it met his exacting standards for the birds' safety and comfort. "Perfectly smooth," he said. "Birds deserve quality craftsmanship too."

Xiomara moved to help him assemble the roof pieces, holding them steady while Ferdinand secured the joints with weather-resistant glue and small brass screws. "How many birdhouses have you built total?" she asked, curious.

Ferdinand considered the question while finishing the current joint, genuinely uncertain of the exact number after so many years of this beloved hobby. "Lost count decades ago," he said. "Just know the birds around here have plenty of good homes."
