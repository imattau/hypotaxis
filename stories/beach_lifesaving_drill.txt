Whistle sharp against the wind, Fennimore watched the training swimmer struggle convincingly through the surf, timing his response before sprinting down the sand with the rescue tube trailing behind him. The lifesaving club's monthly drills kept everyone sharp for the real emergencies.

His drill partner, Seraphine, tracked the exercise from the tower with binoculars, radio ready to call adjustments if Fennimore's approach angle drifted off course. "Good line," she called down. "Current's pulling him further right than usual today."

Fennimore adjusted his angle slightly based on her call, cutting through the waves with strong, economical strokes toward the flailing training swimmer. He reached the swimmer within the target time, securing the rescue tube around him before beginning the tow back toward shore.

Seraphine timed the full exercise from the tower, noting the result in the club's training log alongside the dozens of other drills logged that season. "Forty-two seconds," she called out. "Best time this month, but the current's tricky today, don't get overconfident."

Fennimore helped the training swimmer to his feet on the sand, both of them breathing hard from the exercise despite it being a simulated rescue. "Current's definitely stronger than the forecast said," he agreed. "Good thing we drill for exactly this."
