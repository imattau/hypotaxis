Detective Mara Voss set her coffee cup down on the cluttered desk and studied the photograph pinned to the corkboard. Rain tapped steadily against the precinct window behind her, blurring the neon sign of the diner across the street.

Her partner, Owen, dropped a manila folder in front of her. "Witness says she saw a man in a grey coat leaving the warehouse around midnight," he said, loosening his tie. Mara flipped the folder open and scanned the first page, her brow furrowing.

"Grey coat narrows it down to half the city," she muttered. She stood, pulling her own coat from the back of her chair. "Let's talk to her ourselves."

They drove in silence through the wet streets until they reached a narrow apartment building. An elderly woman opened the door before they could knock, clutching a shawl around her shoulders. "I already told the other officer everything," she said, eyes darting between them.

"We just need five more minutes," Mara said gently, holding up her badge. The woman hesitated, then stepped aside to let them in. Owen glanced back at the street, where a parked car's headlights flicked on and then off again.

Inside, the woman sat by the window and pointed down at the alley below. "He was standing right there," she said. "Under that lamp. He looked up at my window, and I swear he smiled." Mara followed her gaze down to the empty, rain-soaked alley.
