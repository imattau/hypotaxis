Rubble crunched under Wilhelmina's boots as she followed her search dog through the collapsed building's debris field, watching the German shepherd's body language shift into the focused alert pattern that meant he'd caught a scent. Two days after the earthquake, hope was fading but not gone.

Her fellow handler, Cornelius, worked a parallel search lane with his own dog, radio crackling between them as they coordinated their overlapping search patterns through the unstable wreckage. "Any indication on your end?" he called over. "Rex just alerted near the north corner."

Wilhelmina watched her own dog paw insistently at a specific section of collapsed concrete, the trained behavior unmistakable to anyone who'd worked search and rescue dogs before. "Same here," she called back. "Getting a strong indication near this beam. Calling it in now."

Cornelius relayed her coordinates to the structural engineers assessing which sections were safe enough for the extraction team to approach, urgency clear in his voice despite the team's practiced calm. "Confirmed indication at Wilhelmina's location," he reported. "Requesting extraction team assessment."

Wilhelmina praised her dog with quiet, controlled enthusiasm, careful not to break his focus while they waited for the engineers to clear the area for careful excavation. "Good boy," she murmured. "Stay ready, we might need you again in a few minutes."
