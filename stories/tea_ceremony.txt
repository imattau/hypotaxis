Steam curled from the kettle as Michiyo knelt before the low table, whisking the powdered matcha into a smooth, frothy green with practiced circular motions of the bamboo whisk. Her student, visiting for the first formal lesson, watched every movement with careful attention.

Her student, Beauregard, sat stiffly across the tatami mat, unused to the traditional seated position and trying not to let his discomfort show. "How long have you been practicing this?" he asked quietly, careful not to interrupt the ceremony's measured rhythm.

Michiyo set down the whisk and turned the bowl precisely before offering it to him, her movements deliberate and unhurried despite his obvious nervousness. "Twenty-three years," she said. "And I still learn something new each time I prepare a bowl."

Beauregard accepted the bowl with both hands as she'd shown him earlier, turning it slightly before drinking as instructed, the tea's bitter warmth surprising him after the ceremony's quiet buildup. "It's stronger than I expected," he admitted, setting the bowl down carefully.

Michiyo smiled faintly and began the process of cleaning the utensils, each motion as deliberate as the preparation had been. "The strength is the point," she said. "Sit with it a moment before we begin again. There's no rushing this."
