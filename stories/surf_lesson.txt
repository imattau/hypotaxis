Salt spray misted over the board as Keturah paddled out past the breakers, glancing back to check that her student was keeping pace through the whitewash behind her. Morning light glinted off the swells rolling steadily toward the empty beach.

Her student, Alistair, struggled to keep his board pointed straight, wobbling with each small wave that pushed against him. "I keep getting turned sideways," he called out, spitting out a mouthful of seawater. "Is that normal for the first lesson?"

Keturah laughed and paddled back toward him, steadying his board with one hand while she adjusted his position slightly. "Completely normal," she said. "Point your nose straight at the horizon and paddle through it, don't fight the wave, just aim past it."

Alistair adjusted his angle and paddled harder, this time punching cleanly through the next wave's face instead of getting shoved backward by it. He grinned, water streaming down his face, clearly proud of the small victory as he caught up to where Keturah waited.

"Now for the fun part," Keturah said, turning her board back toward shore and pointing to a swell building behind them. "When I say go, paddle hard and don't look down. Trust the board, it knows what to do even if you don't yet."
