Tiny parts lay organized across Cyrus's felt mat as he disassembled the vintage fountain pen's filling mechanism, tracing the source of the ink leak that had frustrated the owner for months. This particular model dated back decades.

His workshop assistant, Marisol, cleaned the pen's nib assembly with a specialized solution, removing dried ink residue that had accumulated in the delicate feed channels over years of use. "Feed's looking much cleaner now," she said.

Cyrus examined the filling mechanism's sac closely, finding a small crack in the rubber that explained the persistent leak he'd been chasing through the pen's other components. "Found it," he said. "Old sac's finally given out. We'll need a replacement."

Marisol finished cleaning the nib and tested its flow against scrap paper, satisfied to find ink moving smoothly through the now-clear feed channels. "Nib's writing beautifully," she confirmed, examining the test strokes.

Cyrus carefully installed a new ink sac and reassembled the filling mechanism, testing its action several times to confirm the repair had resolved the leaking issue completely. "That's holding properly now," he said. "This old pen's ready to write again."
