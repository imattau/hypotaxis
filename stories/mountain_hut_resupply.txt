Heavy pack straps dug into Ottokar's shoulders as he climbed the final switchback toward the alpine hut, load of fresh supplies destined for the small kitchen that fed dozens of hikers each week. The resupply hike happened twice monthly, rain or shine.

His hut-keeper colleague, Marisol, met him at the trail's final approach, immediately reaching to help redistribute some of the heaviest items from his overloaded pack. "You brought the whole grocery list this time," she said, laughing at the pack's obvious weight.

Ottokar gratefully handed off a heavy bag of flour and rice, feeling his shoulders loosen slightly with the reduced load for the final stretch to the hut's door. "Hikers have been requesting more baking supplies," he said. "Apparently fresh bread is a big draw up here."

Marisol carried her portion toward the hut's kitchen storage, already planning how to organize the fresh delivery among the shelves that were running low after a busy week of visitors. "The propane's the real prize today," she said. "We were down to our last tank."

Ottokar followed her inside and set down the remaining supplies, satisfied to have completed another successful resupply despite his aching shoulders. "Same time in two weeks," he said. "Try not to run out of coffee before then, the hikers get cranky."
