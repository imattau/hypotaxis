Wax seals gleamed under the shop's warm lighting as Henrietta arranged the morning's fresh wheels along the display counter, each one labeled with its origin and aging time for the curious customers who'd soon fill the small shop. Saturday mornings always brought the biggest crowds.

Her shop assistant, Bartholomew, sliced samples from yesterday's opened wheels, arranging small squares on toothpicks for customers to taste before committing to a purchase. "The aged gouda's going fast," he said. "Might want to bring up another wheel from the back cooler."

Henrietta nodded and headed toward the walk-in cooler, mentally calculating how much stock they'd need to survive the weekend rush based on last month's sales patterns. "Bring up the manchego too," she called back. "People always want it once they taste the gouda."

Bartholomew arranged the fresh samples as the shop's first customers began filtering in, greeting a regular by name while directing a curious newcomer toward the tasting counter. "Try the aged gouda first," he suggested. "Then work your way toward the stronger blues."

Henrietta returned with the additional wheels just as the shop filled with its usual Saturday energy, customers clustering around the counter sampling and chatting about their favorites. "Busy one today," she said, already slicing into the fresh manchego for the growing crowd.
