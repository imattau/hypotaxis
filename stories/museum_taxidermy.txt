Glass eyes gleamed under the workshop lamp as Cornelius positioned the fox's form carefully, adjusting the ears to capture an alert, lifelike expression before the final stitching. The museum's natural history wing needed this specimen restored before the new exhibit opened next month.

His apprentice, Guinevere, mixed a small batch of clay to rebuild a damaged section of the nose, working the material carefully to match the surrounding texture. "The original mount is nearly a century old," she said. "Whoever did this work originally was remarkably skilled."

Cornelius nodded, examining the fox's pose from several angles before making a final adjustment to the wire armature beneath the fur. "They didn't have half the modern tools we do," he said. "Just patience and a deep understanding of anatomy."

Guinevere finished shaping the clay repair and set it aside to firm up, moving to help Cornelius with the final stitching along the fox's underside seam. "Should we match the original mounting pose exactly?" she asked. "Or adjust it slightly for the new display case?"

Cornelius considered the question while completing another careful stitch, weighing historical accuracy against the practical needs of the new exhibit space. "Match the original," he said. "This pose is part of the specimen's history now, we shouldn't change it just for convenience."
