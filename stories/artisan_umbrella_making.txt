Waxed cotton stretched across the wooden ribs as Percival worked the traditional umbrella frame, hand-stitching each panel with the same technique passed down through three generations of his family's small workshop. Machine-made umbrellas couldn't match this durability.

His apprentice, Rosalind, prepared the next panel section, cutting the waxed fabric to the precise pattern required for proper coverage once assembled onto the frame's remaining ribs. "This panel's cut and ready," she said.

Percival examined her cutting work, checking the edges against the pattern template before approving it for stitching onto the frame's next available section. "Perfect cut," he said. "Your precision keeps improving."

Rosalind threaded her needle and began stitching the panel into position, following the technique Percival had demonstrated countless times over her months of apprenticeship. "The stitches are staying even," she observed with quiet satisfaction.

Percival nodded approvingly and continued his own stitching on the neighboring panel, the workshop falling into comfortable silence broken only by their needles working through the waxed fabric. "Six more panels," he said. "Then this one's finally complete."
