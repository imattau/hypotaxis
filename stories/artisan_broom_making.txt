Broomcorn fibers stood bundled across Guinevere's workbench as she wound wire tightly around the handle's base, securing the material before the flattening and stitching process that would give the broom its distinctive fan shape. Traditional broom making required specific tools.

Her apprentice, Torbjorn, sorted broomcorn by length and quality, separating the finest material for the outer layer from the coarser fiber that would form the broom's sturdy inner core. "This bundle's perfect for the outer layer," he said.

Guinevere examined the sorted fiber he offered, confirming its quality matched the standard needed for a broom that would satisfy even her most discerning craft-fair customers. "Excellent selection," she said. "Add it to this handle's outer layer."

Torbjorn added the fiber as instructed, watching Guinevere's wire-winding technique closely to understand the tension needed for a secure, long-lasting bind between broomcorn and handle. "How tight should this wire actually be?" he asked.

Guinevere continued winding, demonstrating the technique while explaining the subtle feel that indicated proper tension without cutting into the broomcorn fibers themselves. "Tight enough to hold, gentle enough to preserve," she said. "You'll develop the feel."
