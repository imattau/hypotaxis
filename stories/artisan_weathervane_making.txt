Copper sheet gleamed under workshop lights as Philomena hammered the weathervane's rooster silhouette into shape, working the traditional repoussé technique that would give the finished piece its distinctive three-dimensional form. Custom weathervanes demanded artistic vision.

Her apprentice, Anselm, prepared the directional arms and cardinal letters that would complete the weathervane's functional components, cutting each piece to match the client's specific historical reference design. "Directionals are cut and ready," he said.

Philomena examined his cutting work, checking the proportions against her original sketch before approving the pieces for eventual assembly with the rooster's hammered form. "These match perfectly," she said. "Beautiful precision work."

Anselm watched her continue hammering the rooster's tail feathers, absorbing the subtle technique that transformed flat copper sheet into convincing three-dimensional feather texture through careful, repeated strikes. "How many strikes does each feather need?" he asked.

Philomena continued her rhythmic hammering, considering the question that depended entirely on the specific feather's size and desired depth of relief. "As many as it takes," she said. "You'll develop the eye for knowing when to stop."
