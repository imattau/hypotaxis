Cold morning air bit at Fennimore's ears as he climbed the ladder propped against the old apple tree, loppers in hand, breath fogging with each careful exhale. Winter pruning had to happen before the sap started rising, and this orchard had forty more trees waiting after this one.

His daughter, Marguerite, home from college for the weekend, held the ladder steady from below, occasionally pointing out branches he'd missed from her lower vantage point. "That one crossing through the middle," she called up. "You always say those are the first ones to go."

Fennimore squinted up into the tangled canopy and found the branch she meant, angling the loppers carefully before making the cut in one clean motion. "Good eye," he said, tossing the severed branch down into the growing pile beside the ladder's base. "You've been paying attention all these years after all."

Marguerite laughed and dragged the pile of branches toward the burn area at the orchard's edge, her breath visible in the cold air as she worked. "Hard not to, growing up climbing these same trees every summer," she said. "Though I never thought I'd miss the smell of cut branches this much."

By midafternoon they'd worked through six trees together, the rhythm of pruning and hauling settling into something almost meditative in the crisp winter light. Fennimore climbed down from the last tree of the day and surveyed their progress with quiet satisfaction, already looking forward to next weekend's continuation.
