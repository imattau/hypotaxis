Layers of old paint flaked away under Persimmon's careful scraping as she worked to uncover the carousel horse's original decorative details, hidden beneath decades of repainting by less careful hands. The century-old carnival piece deserved authentic restoration.

Her restoration partner, Bartholomew, mixed a small batch of paint to match a fragment of original color they'd discovered beneath the horse's saddle area, testing the shade against archival photographs. "This matches the old photo remarkably well," he said.

Persimmon paused her scraping to examine the color match herself, nodding in satisfaction at how closely Bartholomew had recreated the original vibrant hue. "Perfect," she said. "Now we just need to uncover the rest of the original design before repainting."

Bartholomew set the paint aside to test-dry and returned to help with the scraping, working on the horse's flowing mane where evidence suggested elaborate original detailing had been painted over. "There's gold leaf under here," he said, genuinely excited by the discovery.

Persimmon leaned over to examine his find, matching his excitement at the unexpected detail that would make this restoration even more historically significant than they'd initially hoped. "This horse just got a lot more interesting," she said, reaching for her own tools.
