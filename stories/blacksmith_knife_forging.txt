Orange heat radiated from the forge as Ambrose drew the glowing steel bar out with tongs, laying it against the anvil to begin drawing out the blade's initial profile with measured hammer strikes. The custom chef's knife commission needed to be finished before the client's visit next week.

His apprentice, Theodora, worked the bellows steadily, keeping the coal fire at the precise temperature Ambrose needed for this stage of the forging process. "How much longer on the rough shape?" she asked, watching sparks scatter with each hammer blow.

Ambrose examined the developing profile critically, checking the blade's proportions against a paper template pinned to the workbench nearby. "Few more heats," he said. "Want to get the profile close before we move to normalizing and the actual heat treatment."

Theodora pumped the bellows again as Ambrose returned the steel to the coals, watching the metal's color shift back toward the orange he needed for the next round of hammering. "The edge geometry's looking good already," she observed, studying the blade's developing bevel.

Ambrose pulled the glowing steel free once more and resumed hammering, the ring of metal on metal filling the small forge with its familiar, satisfying rhythm. "Getting there," he said. "This one's going to be a good knife, I can already tell."
