Flour dusted the air as Priya slid another tray of croissants into the oven, glancing at the clock with growing panic. Only forty minutes remained before the judges made their rounds, and her laminated dough had split twice already that morning.

Her competition partner, Desmond, hurried over with a fresh batch of pastry cream, nearly dropping the bowl as he skidded to a stop at her station. "The strawberries came in bruised," he said, wincing. "I picked through them but we lost almost half."

Priya wiped a floured hand across her forehead and exhaled slowly. "Use what's good, we'll make it work," she said, already reaching for a paring knife to help him salvage the fruit. "The judges care about flavor more than a perfect garnish."

Across the tent, a rival baker's timer buzzed loudly, and Priya flinched at the sound before forcing her focus back to the oven's small window. Desmond arranged the salvaged strawberries in careful rows, his hands steadier now that he had a task in front of him.

When the croissants finally emerged golden and impossibly flaky, Priya let out a breath she'd been holding for what felt like an hour. Desmond grinned and held up two fingers, mouthing "two minutes" as the head judge began making her way down the row of stations toward them.
