Announcements echoed off the station's high ceiling as Rosalind hurried through the crowd, scanning the departure boards for a platform number that kept eluding her. Her connecting train left in eight minutes, and she'd already circled the same corridor twice.

A station attendant, Emeric, noticed her frantic pace and stepped into her path with a calm, practiced smile. "You look like someone who needs platform nine," he said. "Everyone gets turned around here, the signage is terrible past the food court."

Rosalind exhaled with relief and fell into step beside him as he led her through a shortcut past the closed newsstand toward the correct platform. "I've never been so grateful for a stranger's sense of direction," she said, checking her watch nervously as they walked.

Emeric chuckled and pointed toward the platform entrance now visible ahead, the departure board finally confirming her train hadn't left yet. "You've got three minutes," he said. "Should be plenty if you don't stop to admire the architecture on the way down."

Rosalind thanked him quickly and broke into a jog toward the platform, glancing back once to wave before disappearing down the escalator. Emeric watched her go, then returned to his post near the information desk, already scanning the crowd for the next lost traveler.
