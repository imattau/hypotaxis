The overnight train rattled through the darkened countryside as Ines pulled her jacket tighter and studied the empty seat across from her, wondering if the conductor had made a mistake with the booking. Rain streaked sideways across the window, blurring the distant lights of a passing town.

A man ducked into the compartment just as the doors chimed shut, apologizing breathlessly as he stowed a battered suitcase overhead. "Sorry, cutting it close," he said, dropping into the seat across from her. "I'm Marcus. Looks like we're neighbors for the next six hours."

Ines offered a polite nod and returned to the book open in her lap, though she found herself glancing up every few pages as the train swayed through the dark. Marcus pulled out a worn paperback of his own, the spine cracked from repeated reading.

"Long trip?" Marcus asked eventually, folding down the corner of his page. "I'm heading to see my sister. Haven't visited in almost two years." Ines hesitated before answering, surprised by how easily the words came once she started talking about her own reasons for the journey.

By the time the train pulled into a brief stop somewhere past midnight, they had traded most of their life stories in hushed voices, the compartment's small reading light the only thing keeping the darkness outside at bay. Marcus offered her half of a chocolate bar from his bag, and Ines accepted it without hesitation this time.
