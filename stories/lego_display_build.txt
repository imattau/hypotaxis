Thousands of tiny bricks lay sorted across Percival's workbench as he consulted the custom design he'd spent weeks planning, snapping another section of the miniature cathedral's flying buttress into place. The model convention was only two weeks away.

His building partner, Wilhelmina, sorted through a bin of specialty pieces, searching for the specific curved elements the roof section still needed to look architecturally accurate. "Found six of the arch pieces," she said. "That should be enough to finish this section."

Percival took the pieces she'd found and began fitting them along the roof's curve, checking each connection point against his reference photos of the actual cathedral. "Perfect," he said. "The roofline's finally coming together the way I imagined it."

Wilhelmina returned to sorting, this time searching for the small stained-glass window pieces that would complete the cathedral's most distinctive feature. "These translucent pieces are scattered across three different bins," she said. "This might take a while to sort."

Percival continued building while she searched, the cathedral's silhouette growing more recognizable with each section he completed. "Worth it though," he said, stepping back to admire the model's growing scale. "This is going to be the best piece I've ever built."
