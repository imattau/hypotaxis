Spruce shavings curled from Ignatius's spokeshave as he shaped another oar's blade, working toward the precise thickness taper that would give this pair proper balance and flex for competitive rowing use. Custom oars demanded exact specifications.

His apprentice, Cordelia, sanded the completed oar's handle, smoothing it to the comfortable grip diameter that serious rowers specifically requested for their competition equipment. "This handle feels really comfortable already," she said.

Ignatius examined her sanding work, gripping the handle himself to confirm the diameter and smoothness matched what his rowing clients typically preferred for extended use. "Perfect grip," he said. "Rowers will appreciate that comfort."

Cordelia moved to help him check the blade's flex characteristics, bending it carefully to test whether the thickness taper had achieved the responsiveness this particular oar design was meant to provide. "Flex feels right to me," she observed.

Ignatius confirmed her assessment with his own test, satisfied that this oar pair was approaching the exacting standards his competitive rowing clients had come to expect from his small workshop. "One more oar to match," he said. "Then this pair's complete."
