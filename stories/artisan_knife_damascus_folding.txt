Layered steel billets glowed orange in the forge as Casimir prepared the next fold, the alternating bands of high and low carbon steel that would eventually reveal Damascus pattern beneath the finished blade's surface. Twelve folds remained before the pattern would be complex enough.

His apprentice, Wilhelmina, worked the power hammer's controls, providing the consistent striking force needed to weld each fresh layer into the billet under Casimir's careful guidance. "Ready for the next fold," she said, positioning the hammer.

Casimir positioned the glowing billet beneath the hammer and nodded, and Wilhelmina brought the mechanical hammer down in a series of controlled strikes that welded the layers together seamlessly. "Good weld," he said, examining the billet's edge for any signs of separation.

Wilhelmina helped reposition the billet for the next cut and fold, the pattern's complexity building with each repetition of this demanding, methodical process. "Eleven more folds," she confirmed, checking their count against the pattern chart.

Casimir returned the billet to the forge for reheating, satisfied with how the pattern was developing despite the many hours still required before this blade would be ready for final shaping. "Worth every fold," he said. "This is going to be a beautiful blade."
