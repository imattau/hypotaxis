The radio crackled to life just as Sana finished lacing her boots. "We've got a hiker stranded above the ridge line, possible broken ankle," the dispatcher said. "Weather's closing in fast." Sana was already moving before the message finished, grabbing her pack from the hook by the door.

Outside, her climbing partner Wolf was loading rope and a first aid kit into the truck. Clouds had gathered thick and grey over the peak, swallowing the summit whole. "Second call this month," Wolf said, tossing her a helmet. "People underestimate that trail every single time."

They hiked hard for an hour before the trail steepened into loose scree, wind picking up around them as the light began to dim early under the storm clouds. Sana spotted a flash of orange fabric wedged against a boulder ahead and quickened her pace, scrambling over the rocks.

The hiker, a young man with his ankle swollen badly inside his boot, looked up at them with obvious relief. "I didn't think anyone would make it up here before dark," he said through gritted teeth. Wolf knelt beside him, already unpacking a splint, while Sana scanned the darkening sky and radioed their position back to base.

"We're going to get you down," Sana said, crouching to help steady him. "Just hold still while we splint this." Thunder rolled somewhere beyond the ridge, and the first drops of rain began to fall as Wolf tightened the last strap.
