Wet clay spun steadily beneath Fitzgerald's palms as he demonstrated the centering technique for the third time that evening, the community center's small studio smelling of damp earth and old kilns. Beginner pottery classes always drew an eclectic mix of students.

His newest student, Marguerite, watched intently from the neighboring wheel, her own lump of clay wobbling badly off-center despite her best efforts to replicate his motion. "I don't understand what I'm doing differently," she said, frustration creeping into her voice.

Fitzgerald slowed his wheel and reached over to guide her hands gently, adjusting the angle of her palms against the spinning clay. "You're pushing down instead of inward," he said. "Feel how the clay wants to move, don't force it against its own momentum."

Marguerite tried again with his adjustment, and this time the clay began wobbling less, slowly settling toward something resembling a centered lump beneath her steadying hands. "It's actually working," she said, surprise evident in her voice.

Fitzgerald smiled and returned to his own wheel, letting her continue practicing the centering motion while he began shaping his demonstration piece into a simple bowl. "Once you've got centering down," he said, "everything else becomes so much easier."
