Clay slabs rolled evenly beneath Fabrizia's roller as she prepared another batch of tiles for the custom bathroom commission, checking each slab's thickness against her calibrated guide sticks. Consistent thickness prevented warping during the firing process.

Her apprentice, Cyrus, cut the rolled clay into precise squares using a template, stacking the cut tiles carefully between sheets of fabric to prevent them from sticking together while drying. "These are coming out perfectly uniform," he said.

Fabrizia examined his cutting work, checking the edges for the clean, straight lines that would ensure proper installation once the tiles reached their final glazed and fired state. "Nice work," she said. "Client specifically wants precise grout lines."

Cyrus continued cutting through the rolled clay, developing a steady rhythm that matched the efficiency Fabrizia had demonstrated during his training over the past several months. "How many tiles does this bathroom need total?" he asked.

Fabrizia calculated the coverage area against standard tile dimensions, arriving at a number that represented several more days of careful, repetitive work before the batch would be complete. "About two hundred more," she said. "Good thing we're both patient people."
