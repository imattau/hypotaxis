Melted tallow shimmered warm in the traditional dipping vat as Odalys lowered another rack of wicks in the slow, rhythmic motion that generations of candle makers had used before molded candles existed. Traditional dipped candles built up layer by patient layer.

Her apprentice, Fergus, monitored the tallow's temperature, adjusting the heat source slightly whenever the reading drifted from the precise range that produced proper layer adhesion during the repeated dipping process. "Temperature's holding steady," he said.

Odalys lifted the wick rack from the vat, checking the current layer's thickness against her mental count before beginning the next dip in the long, patient sequence toward finished candles. "Building up nicely," she said. "About twenty more dips."

Fergus watched her continue the repetitive dipping motion, absorbing the meditative rhythm that this ancient craft seemed to demand from anyone who practiced it seriously and consistently. "How many total dips does a candle need?" he asked.

Odalys continued her steady rhythm, considering the question that depended entirely on the desired final thickness and the specific taper shape this particular batch was meant to achieve. "Depends on the candle," she said. "This batch wants about forty total."
