The fluorescent lights in the office hummed overhead as Marcus stared at his monitor, the deadline countdown in the corner of the screen ticking toward zero. Empty coffee cups had accumulated in a small graveyard beside his keyboard.

His manager, Renata, stopped at his desk, arms full of printouts. "Tell me the client presentation is finished," she said, though her tone suggested she already knew the answer.

"Ninety percent," Marcus said, not looking away from the screen. "The numbers on slide twelve don't match what finance sent me, so I'm redoing the whole chart." Renata set the printouts down on the edge of his desk with a heavy sigh, pulling a spare chair over to sit beside him.

"Show me," she said. Marcus turned his monitor toward her, pointing at the mismatched figures highlighted in red. Renata leaned forward, scanning the numbers with narrowed eyes, then reached for her phone.

"I'll call finance myself," she said, already dialing. "You keep working on the layout." Marcus nodded gratefully and turned back to his screen, fingers moving quickly across the keyboard as the office around them slowly emptied for the evening, one desk lamp clicking off after another.

By the time Renata hung up the phone, the corrected numbers had been read out to her, and she relayed them to Marcus, who typed them in without missing a beat. Outside the window, the city lights had come on, and somewhere down the hall the cleaning cart squeaked past their door.
