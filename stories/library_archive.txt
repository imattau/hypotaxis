Dust rose in the narrow beam of light as Prudence carefully turned the brittle pages of the century-old ledger, cotton gloves protecting the fragile paper from the oils of her bare hands. The archive's basement stayed cool year-round, a small mercy for documents this old.

Her research assistant, Cornelius, photographed each page methodically before Prudence turned to the next, the camera's soft click punctuating the archive's otherwise complete silence. "This entry mentions the original property boundary," he said. "That's exactly what the historical society needed for the dispute."

Prudence paused on the page and leaned closer, squinting at the faded ink under her small desk lamp. "Read that measurement again," she said. "I want to make sure we're transcribing this correctly before we move on to the next volume."

Cornelius zoomed in on his camera's screen and read the figures aloud slowly, letting Prudence cross-check them against her own notes written in pencil on a legal pad beside the ledger. "That matches what I've got," she said, nodding and making a small check mark beside the entry.

By the time they'd finished the volume, both of them were stiff from hours hunched over the old desk, but satisfied with the afternoon's careful work. "Three more boxes after this one," Prudence said, stretching her back. "We might actually finish before the society's deadline after all."
