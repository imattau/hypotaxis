Thick saddle leather held its shape under Osborne's molding press as he formed another holster around the wooden gun blank, working the wet leather into the precise contours that would ensure both retention and smooth draw. Custom holsters demanded exact fit.

His apprentice, Guinevere, prepared the boning tool, ready to define the leather's fine details once it dried enough to hold crisp lines around the trigger guard and cylinder release areas. "Boning tool's ready when you need it," she said.

Osborne examined the leather's moisture level, confirming it had reached the ideal consistency for detailed boning work before beginning the careful definition process around the holster's critical retention points.

Guinevere watched him work the boning tool skillfully, absorbing the subtle pressure and angle that created such crisp, precise definition around the trigger guard without risking any damage to the leather. "That definition looks incredible," she observed.

Osborne finished the boning work and set the holster aside to fully dry, satisfied with how this particular piece was developing toward its eventual finishing and hardware installation. "Give it a day," he said. "Then we finish this one properly."
