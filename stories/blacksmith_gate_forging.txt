Orange sparks scattered as Ferdinand hammered the decorative scroll into shape, working the heated iron bar with the confident precision of thirty years shaping ornamental ironwork. The estate's new entrance gate needed twelve matching scrolls before installation.

His apprentice, Ottilie, worked the forge's bellows steadily, keeping the coal fire at the exact temperature Ferdinand needed for the intricate scrollwork's demanding heat requirements. "How many scrolls left after this one?" she asked, watching sparks fly with each hammer strike.

Ferdinand examined the developing scroll's curve against the template pinned to his workbench, satisfied with how closely it matched the pattern despite the material's stubborn resistance. "Four more after this," he said. "Then we start on the gate's central medallion piece."

Ottilie pumped the bellows again as Ferdinand returned the iron to the coals, watching the metal's color shift back toward the working temperature needed for the next round of shaping. "The medallion sounds like the hardest part," she observed.

Ferdinand pulled the glowing iron free and resumed hammering, his rhythm steady and unhurried despite the physical demands of the intricate work. "Hardest and most satisfying," he said. "That's usually how the best pieces turn out."
