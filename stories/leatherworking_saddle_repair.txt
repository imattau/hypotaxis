Heavy waxed thread pulled taut as Cornelius stitched the saddle's torn skirt back together, working the awl through the thick leather with the steady rhythm of forty years behind the craft. The ranch's oldest working saddle had earned this repair through decades of loyal service.

His apprentice, Rosalind, prepared a fresh batch of leather conditioner nearby, testing its consistency against a scrap piece before applying it to the saddle's worn sections. "This leather's held up remarkably well," she said, running a hand along the saddle's seat.

Cornelius glanced up from his stitching, nodding at her observation while continuing his steady rhythm through the thick material. "Good leather and regular conditioning," he said. "That's the whole secret, nothing complicated about it really."

Rosalind finished testing the conditioner and began working it into the saddle's flap, watching the leather visibly darken and soften as it absorbed the treatment. "Should I do the whole saddle or just the worn sections?" she asked, pausing her application.

Cornelius finished his final stitch and knotted the thread securely before answering, checking his repair's strength with a firm tug against the reinforced seam. "Whole saddle," he said. "Even's better than patchy when it comes to conditioning leather."
