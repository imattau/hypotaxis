Amber warning lights swept across the snow-covered highway as Yusuf guided the plow steadily down the empty lane, blade pushing a wall of fresh powder toward the shoulder in the predawn darkness. Six hours into his shift, and the storm showed no signs of letting up.

His radio crackled with an update from dispatch, Roswitha's voice cutting through the static with the next section assignment. "Route fourteen's still untouched," she said. "County wants it cleared before the morning commute starts in three hours."

Yusuf acknowledged the assignment and adjusted his course toward the connecting highway, watching his mirrors carefully for the rare vehicle brave enough to be out in these conditions. "Copy that," he radioed back. "Visibility's rough out here, tell the morning crew to expect drifting on the exposed sections."

Roswitha relayed his conditions report to the other plow drivers working the county's grid, coordinating the overnight effort to keep the roads passable before sunrise. "Copy your report," she said. "Truck three's having chain issues, might need backup on route nine."

Yusuf continued down the highway, plow blade throwing snow in a steady arc as the storm's intensity finally began to ease slightly with the approaching dawn. "I can swing to route nine after fourteen," he said. "Should still make it before the commute picks up."
