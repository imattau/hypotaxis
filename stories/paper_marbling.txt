Colored inks bloomed across the size bath's surface as Theodosia dropped another careful bead of pigment, watching the pattern spread and swirl before reaching for her comb tool. The commission needed twelve matching endpapers, each one slightly different but harmonious with the others.

Her studio assistant, Balthazar, prepared the next sheet of paper, checking its alum treatment had fully dried before handing it over for the marbling process. "This batch of paper took the alum evenly," he said. "Should give you a clean pull this time."

Theodosia dragged her comb through the swirling colors in a careful zigzag, creating the feathered pattern the client had specifically requested for this particular commission. "Beautiful," she murmured, mostly to herself, before reaching for the prepared sheet Balthazar held ready.

She lowered the paper onto the bath's surface in one smooth, practiced motion, letting it rest just long enough to capture the pattern before lifting it free and rinsing it gently. Balthazar took the finished sheet carefully, hanging it to dry alongside the others already completed that morning.

"Eleven more to go," Theodosia said, already skimming the bath's surface clean in preparation for the next pattern. Balthazar readied another sheet of alum-treated paper, settling into the rhythm they'd built over months of working together on this exact process.
