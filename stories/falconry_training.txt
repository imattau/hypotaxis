Morning mist still clung to the field as Persimmon raised her gloved fist, the young hawk shifting its weight and studying the open grass beyond with sharp, focused eyes. Months of patient training had brought them to this first real free flight, and her stomach was tighter than she wanted to admit.

Her mentor, Ezekiel, stood a short distance away with the lure ready, watching the hawk's body language for any sign of hesitation before nodding to Persimmon. "She's locked on," he said quietly. "Release when you're ready, and trust the training."

Persimmon took a slow breath and opened her fist, and the hawk launched into the misty air with a powerful downstroke, climbing before banking wide across the field toward Ezekiel's outstretched lure. She watched with her heart in her throat, half-certain the bird would simply keep flying and never look back.

The hawk closed the distance fast, talons extending as it struck the lure cleanly and settled onto the grass, feathers ruffling with satisfaction over the successful catch. Ezekiel knelt slowly, offering a small piece of meat as reward while keeping his movements calm and unhurried.

Persimmon jogged over, relief flooding through her as she watched the hawk feed contentedly under Ezekiel's steady hand. "She came back," she said, voice cracking slightly with emotion. "I really wasn't sure she would." Ezekiel smiled and nodded toward the glove. "Now you get to be the one she comes back to."
