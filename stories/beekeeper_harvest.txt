Smoke drifted gently from the small canister as Iolanthe pumped the bellows near the hive's entrance, calming the bees before she pried the lid free. Late summer sun warmed the orchard around her, the air thick with the low hum of thousands of wings.

Her nephew, Casper, stood a cautious distance away in his own oversized protective suit, holding an empty frame rack with visibly nervous hands. "Are you sure they can't sting through this thing?" he called out, voice muffled behind the mesh veil.

Iolanthe laughed and lifted the first frame free, heavy with capped honeycomb glistening gold in the sunlight. "They can, if you panic and give them a reason to," she said. "Move slow, stay calm, and they mostly ignore you." She carried the frame carefully to the rack Casper held out.

Casper flinched as a single bee landed on his sleeve, but forced himself to stay still the way she'd instructed, watching with wide eyes until it lazily flew off toward the hive again. "Okay," he said, breathing out slowly. "That wasn't as bad as I thought it'd be."

By the time they'd pulled six full frames, Iolanthe's forearms ached pleasantly from the careful work, and Casper had stopped flinching at every bee that drifted past his veil. "Next year you're doing the smoking," she told him, already looking forward to the honey extraction waiting back at the house.
