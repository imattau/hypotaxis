Paper lanterns swayed overhead as Ignatius arranged skewers of grilled meat across the stall's small grill, smoke curling up into the crowded night market's already thick air. Saturday nights brought the biggest crowds, and his stall had built a loyal following over three years of steady business.

His niece, Xiomara, worked the register beside him, calling out orders and making change with the speed of someone who'd grown up in this exact stall. "Two more orders of the spicy skewers," she said. "And someone wants to know if we're out of the peanut sauce yet."

Ignatius flipped the skewers with practiced timing, checking each one's char before pulling it from the heat at exactly the right moment. "Tell them one more batch of sauce," he said. "Should be ready in five minutes, ask them to wait by the noodle stall."

Xiomara relayed the message and returned to ringing up the growing line of customers, occasionally shouting updates to her uncle about which orders needed to move fastest. The stall's small space stayed in constant motion, a practiced choreography built from years of working together.

By the time the crowd finally thinned past midnight, both of them were exhausted but satisfied, counting the night's earnings by lantern light. "Best Saturday this month," Ignatius said, already thinking about tomorrow's prep list for another busy night ahead.
