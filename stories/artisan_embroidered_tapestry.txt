Silk threads glinted under the workshop's bright lamps as Guillermina worked another section of the elaborate wall tapestry, her needle carrying color into the historical scene the museum had commissioned for its new exhibit. Large-scale embroidery took years to complete.

Her apprentice, Fitzgerald, prepared the next color palette, sorting silk threads by shade to match the specific gradient this section of sky required for its dramatic sunset effect. "These oranges should work perfectly here," he said.

Guillermina examined the threads he'd selected, holding them against the completed section to confirm the color transition would flow naturally into the next area of sky she was about to begin. "Beautiful selection," she said. "Thread me a needle with the darkest one."

Fitzgerald threaded the needle as requested, watching Guillermina's stitching technique closely to understand the subtle tension and angle that produced such remarkably smooth color gradations across the fabric. "The blending looks so seamless," he observed.

Guillermina smiled and continued her careful stitching, the tapestry's sunset gradually taking shape after weeks of patient, methodical work toward the museum's ambitious commission deadline. "Six more months," she said. "Then this piece finally hangs."
