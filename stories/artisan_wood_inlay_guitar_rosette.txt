Tiny wood strips lay arranged across Emeric's workbench as he assembled another guitar rosette's intricate mosaic pattern, gluing individual pieces into the circular design that would eventually surround the instrument's soundhole. Rosette work demanded extraordinary patience.

His apprentice, Solange, sliced the assembled rosette log into thin cross-sections, each cut revealing the complete mosaic pattern that would be inlaid around a different guitar's soundhole. "This slice looks perfectly clean," she said.

Emeric examined the sliced cross-section, checking the pattern's clarity and symmetry before approving it for eventual inlay into one of his current guitar builds. "Beautiful clarity," he said. "That pattern really came together nicely."

Solange continued slicing additional cross-sections, absorbing the careful blade angle and pressure that prevented the delicate mosaic from chipping or fragmenting during the demanding cutting process. "How many slices does one log produce?" she asked.

Emeric calculated based on the log's length and each slice's required thickness, arriving at a number that would supply rosettes for several upcoming guitar builds. "About fifteen good slices," he said. "Worth every hour of assembly work."
