Maple shavings curled from Guinevere's carving knife as she shaped another wooden train car, working toward the smooth, child-safe edges that handmade toys specifically required before any finishing could begin. Traditional wooden toys demanded careful attention.

Her apprentice, Osei, sanded the completed pieces smooth, removing any rough spots that might splinter or otherwise pose a hazard to the small children who would eventually play with these handmade toys. "These edges feel really safe now," he said.

Guinevere examined his sanding work, running her own experienced hand across the surface to confirm it met her exacting standards for child safety and comfortable handling. "Perfectly smooth," she said. "Safety always comes before speed."

Osei moved to help her assemble the train set's remaining pieces, holding components steady while Guinevere secured the wooden axles with the non-toxic glue she exclusively used for children's toys. "How many pieces does this set need?" he asked.

Guinevere counted the remaining unassembled pieces, calculating the work still needed before this particular train set would be ready for its new young owner. "Eight more pieces," she said. "Then some lucky child gets a proper wooden train."
