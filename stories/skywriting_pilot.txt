Engine droning steady, Persimmon banked her biplane into the next turn, tracing white smoke across the clear blue sky as she began the second letter of today's marriage proposal commission. Skywriting demanded both flying skill and an artist's spatial sense.

Her ground coordinator, Fitzgerald, watched through binoculars from the beach below, radioing occasional adjustments as the letters began taking shape against the sky's blank canvas. "Letters are legible from here," he said. "Spacing looks good so far."

Persimmon acknowledged his report and began the trickiest letter of the sequence, requiring a tight loop that demanded precise timing to keep the smoke trail consistent throughout the maneuver. "Starting the loop now," she radioed back.

Fitzgerald watched the loop complete cleanly, the smoke trail holding steady despite the challenging maneuver's demands on both pilot and aircraft. "Beautiful loop," he confirmed. "The bride-to-be is going to lose it when she sees this."

Persimmon began the final letters, the message nearly complete as she worked through the remaining sequence with practiced precision despite the summer heat inside her small cockpit. "Almost done," she radioed. "Let's hope she looks up at the right moment."
