Clay spun steadily beneath Farrah's hands as she guided the wheel with a practiced foot, water dripping down her wrists as the vase's walls rose taller with each pass. Afternoon light slanted through the studio's tall windows, catching the dust suspended in the air.

Her mentor, an older potter named Bertrand, watched from a stool nearby, arms folded over his clay-stained apron. "Slower on the neck," he said, leaning forward slightly. "You're rushing it because you're excited, and excited hands make lopsided pots."

Farrah eased her pace, breathing out slowly as she let the clay guide her fingers instead of fighting it. The vase's neck narrowed gradually, evenly, and she felt a small thrill of satisfaction as the shape finally matched the one in her head.

"Better," Bertrand said, rising from his stool to inspect it more closely without touching the spinning wheel. "See how it wants to move now? You stopped forcing it." He gestured toward a shelf lined with finished pieces, several bearing the same graceful curve she was chasing.

Farrah slowed the wheel to a stop and sat back, studying her work with a critical eye of her own. A thin crack had begun forming near the base, barely visible, but Bertrand spotted it immediately and tapped the spot with one finger. "This one's for practice," he said gently. "The next one will be for keeping." Farrah nodded, already reaching for a fresh lump of clay to start again.
