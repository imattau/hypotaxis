Grease coated Malachi's knuckles as he worked the stubborn bolt free from the engine block, the old roadster's hood propped open under the garage's single hanging bulb. Three years of weekend work had brought the car this close to running again.

His neighbor, Philippa, leaned over the fender with a flashlight, reading part numbers off a stamped bracket while Malachi cross-referenced them against a stack of yellowed manuals. "This one matches what the parts catalog listed," she said. "Should be able to order it tomorrow if the supplier still stocks it."

Malachi wiped his hands on a rag and straightened up, rolling stiffness out of his shoulders after an hour hunched over the engine bay. "Good," he said. "That's the last piece we need before we can try starting her up properly for the first time in decades."

Philippa set the flashlight down and ran a hand along the roadster's faded paint, admiring the lines beneath the years of neglect and rust. "Hard to believe this was just sitting in a barn," she said. "Whoever owned it originally really loved this car."

Malachi nodded and closed the manual, already picturing the engine finally turning over under its own power. "We'll find out soon enough if she still remembers how to run," he said, reaching for the next tool on his makeshift workbench.
