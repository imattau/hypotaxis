Sedation still lingered in the tiger's system as Dr. Okonkwo-Reyes knelt beside the massive animal, checking her teeth and gums carefully while the anesthesia team monitored vital signs nearby. The annual checkup required this level of caution for everyone's safety.

Her veterinary technician, Percival, recorded each measurement as she called them out, tracking the tiger's weight and vital statistics against previous years' records. "Heart rate's steady," he said. "Temperature's normal too, she's handling the sedation well."

The veterinarian finished examining the tiger's teeth and moved to check her paws, looking for any signs of the arthritis that sometimes developed in older big cats under human care. "No swelling in the joints," she said. "She's aging remarkably well for her age bracket."

Percival prepared the blood draw equipment while the veterinarian positioned the tiger's leg carefully, working efficiently to minimize the time under sedation. "Blood draw's ready whenever you are," he said, double-checking the vial labels against the tiger's identification number.

The veterinarian completed the blood draw smoothly and stepped back to allow the anesthesia team to begin the reversal process, watching carefully as the massive animal began showing early signs of waking. "Good checkup overall," she said. "We'll have full bloodwork results by tomorrow afternoon."
