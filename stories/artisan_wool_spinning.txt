Raw fleece drifted through Wilhelmina's carding combs as she prepared the wool for spinning, working out tangles and aligning fibers before the material could be drawn onto her spinning wheel's bobbin. Handspun yarn required this patient preparation.

Her student, Ambrosine, practiced treadling the wheel's rhythm without yarn yet, building the muscle memory needed before attempting to actually draft fiber while maintaining consistent pedal pressure. "My rhythm keeps breaking," she said.

Wilhelmina watched her student's treadling with a practiced eye, noting where the rhythm faltered before offering the correction that had helped countless beginners before her. "You're thinking too hard about it," she said. "Let your foot find its own pace."

Ambrosine tried again, this time humming softly to establish a steady tempo, and her treadling smoothed into something approaching consistent rhythm for the first time that session. "That's helping," she said, surprised by the simple trick's effectiveness.

Wilhelmina smiled and handed her student a small amount of carded fiber, ready to introduce actual drafting now that the foundational rhythm had finally clicked into place. "Now the real challenge begins," she said. "But you're ready for it."
