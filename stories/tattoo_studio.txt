The needle's steady buzz filled the small studio as Marisela worked the linework across her client's forearm, wiping away excess ink with practiced rhythm between passes. Afternoon light slanted through the blinds, catching the dust motes drifting above the tattoo chair.

Her apprentice, Dashiell, watched from a stool nearby, sketchbook open to a half-finished flash sheet he'd been developing under her guidance for weeks. "Your line weight barely changes even on the curves," he said. "I keep overcorrecting and it looks shaky."

Marisela paused to check her client's arm before continuing, not looking up as she answered. "Slow down and let the machine do the work," she said. "You're still fighting it instead of guiding it, that's where the shake comes from."

Dashiell nodded and returned his attention to his sketchbook, tracing the same curve slowly across the practice skin taped to his workstation, trying to feel the difference she'd described. The buzz of Marisela's machine filled the silence between them as she finished the outline.

"There," Marisela said, wiping the finished linework clean and leaning back to check the proportions against her original stencil. "Color session next week, and then you'll have a healed piece worth being proud of." Her client examined the fresh outline in the mirror, grinning despite the redness still settling into the skin.
