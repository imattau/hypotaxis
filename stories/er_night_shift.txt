Fluorescent lights buzzed overhead as Dr. Osei jogged down the emergency room hallway, stethoscope bouncing against her chest, following the nurse who had just called her name. Monitors beeped in a dozen different rhythms from the curtained bays on either side.

"Bed six, chest pain, vitals dropping," the nurse said over her shoulder, not slowing down. Dr. Osei pushed through the curtain to find a middle-aged man clutching his chest, sweat beading across his forehead despite the room's chill.

"I need an EKG and a line in now," she said, already pressing two fingers to his wrist to check his pulse. A younger resident, Marcus, appeared at her side moments later, already unwinding the EKG leads with practiced speed.

"Sir, can you tell me when the pain started?" Dr. Osei asked, keeping her voice calm despite the alarm blaring softly from the monitor beside them. The man managed only a strained nod before wincing hard, one hand gripping the bed rail.

Marcus attached the last lead and stepped back as the monitor's jagged line resolved into a clearer pattern. "ST elevation," he said quietly, and Dr. Osei's expression sharpened immediately. "Call the cath lab," she said. "We need him up there in ten minutes." The nurse was already reaching for the phone before the sentence finished, and the small bay erupted into swift, coordinated motion around the patient.
