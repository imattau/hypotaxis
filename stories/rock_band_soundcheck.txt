Amp feedback squealed briefly as Desmond adjusted his guitar's output, nodding to the sound engineer positioned at the mixing board near the back of the empty venue. Doors opened in two hours, and the band's sound still needed dialing in.

The venue's sound engineer, Wilhelmina, called out adjustments through the stage monitors, her voice carrying the practiced patience of someone who'd run hundreds of these soundchecks. "Kick drum's too hot," she said. "Can you back off that gain a touch?"

The drummer adjusted his settings based on her call, testing a few measures of a fill to gauge whether the change had achieved the balance she wanted. Wilhelmina listened carefully through her headphones, comparing the new levels against her reference mix.

"Better," she called out. "Now let's check the vocals against the full band mix." Desmond stepped back from his amp and gestured to the singer, who moved to center stage and began a verse from the night's opening song.

Wilhelmina adjusted the vocal channel's levels as the singer worked through the verse, gradually finding the balance that would let every instrument breathe in tonight's mix. "That's the sound," she said. "You're ready for doors whenever the crowd shows up."
