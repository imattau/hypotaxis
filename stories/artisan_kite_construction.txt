Bamboo splits lay measured across Ottilie's workbench as she lashed the kite's frame together, working the traditional joinery techniques her grandfather had taught her for building competition-grade fighter kites. The regional competition was just weeks away.

Her nephew, Cassius, stretched the tissue paper covering across the completed frame sections, applying paste carefully to avoid wrinkles that would affect the kite's aerodynamic performance during flight. "This section's covering smoothly," he said.

Ottilie examined his covering work, checking the tension and smoothness that would determine how efficiently this particular kite cut through the air during competitive maneuvers. "Excellent tension," she said. "You've really gotten the hang of this."

Cassius continued covering the remaining frame sections, developing increasing confidence with each successful application of the delicate tissue paper across the bamboo structure. "Almost done with the covering," he reported, checking his progress.

Ottilie finished attaching the bridle lines, satisfied with how this particular kite had come together despite the demanding traditional techniques required for true competition-grade construction. "Ready for test flights," she said. "Let's see how she flies."
