Damp vegetable-tanned leather held Isolde's careful stamping tool as she worked an elaborate Celtic knot pattern into the journal cover, each mallet strike transferring the intricate design deeper into the moistened hide. Custom bindings demanded patient precision.

Her apprentice, Barnabus, prepared the next section of leather, dampening it to the exact moisture level that would hold stamped detail without tearing or losing the pattern's crisp lines. "This section's cased and ready," he said.

Isolde examined the leather's moisture level, pressing it gently to confirm consistency before beginning the detailed tooling work on this next journal cover in the batch. "Perfect casing," she said. "Let's transfer the pattern."

Barnabus traced the knotwork template onto the dampened leather, preparing guide lines that would direct Isolde's stamping tools through the elaborate interlocking pattern this commission specifically required. "Pattern's traced and ready," he confirmed.

Isolde began the careful stamping process, her mallet falling in steady rhythm as the Celtic knot gradually emerged from the leather's surface with each precisely placed strike. "This is always the best part," she said. "Watching the pattern come alive."
