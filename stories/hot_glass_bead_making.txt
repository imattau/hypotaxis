Molten glass glowed orange at the torch's flame as Persimmon wound the gather steadily around her mandrel, building the bead's layered core before adding the decorative surface elements the commission required. Custom lampwork beads took patience most jewelry buyers never appreciated.

Her studio assistant, Fitzwilliam, prepared the next color rod, heating its tip slightly so it would flow smoothly when Persimmon was ready to add the decorative dots to her base bead. "Base bead looks nice and round," he said, watching her work.

Persimmon rotated the mandrel steadily in the flame, maintaining the bead's symmetry while she reached for the prepared color rod Fitzwilliam had ready for the next decorative layer. "Ready for the dots," she said, taking the rod from him carefully.

Fitzwilliam watched as she applied the first dot, the contrasting color blooming against the base glass in a small, controlled bead of molten material that would harden into a permanent decoration. "That color's really striking against the base," he observed.

Persimmon continued adding dots in a careful pattern around the bead's circumference, working quickly before the glass cooled too much to accept the design properly. "Almost done with this one," she said. "Then it goes in the kiln to anneal overnight."
