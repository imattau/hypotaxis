Exotic wood dust coated Cassius's workbench as he shaped the knife handle's curve on the belt sander, checking the ergonomic fit against his own grip every few passes to ensure comfortable balance. The custom commission specified a particular hardwood the client had sourced himself.

His apprentice, Rosalind, prepared the brass pins that would secure the handle scales to the blade's tang, polishing each one to a bright shine before the final assembly process began. "These pins are ready," she said. "Should I start mixing the epoxy?"

Cassius held the shaped handle up to check its symmetry from multiple angles, satisfied with how the wood's natural grain pattern had aligned during the shaping process. "Mix it now," he said. "I want to get this assembled before the wood has any chance to shift with humidity."

Rosalind mixed the two-part epoxy carefully, checking the ratio twice against the manufacturer's instructions before applying it to the tang's surface. "Ready when you are," she said, watching Cassius position the handle scales precisely.

Cassius pressed the scales into place and inserted the brass pins, clamping the assembly securely while the epoxy began its curing process. "Twenty-four hours," he said. "Then we can shape the final profile and this knife finally comes together."
