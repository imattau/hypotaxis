Red safelight cast everything in a dim crimson glow as Ottoline slid the exposed paper into the developer tray, watching the image slowly bloom into visibility beneath the liquid's surface. The darkroom smelled of chemicals and old habit, a ritual she'd kept alive long after most had gone fully digital.

Her nephew, Barnaby, leaned over the next tray with a pair of tongs, waiting nervously for his own first print to develop. "Is it supposed to take this long?" he whispered, as if speaking too loudly might somehow ruin the process.

Ottoline smiled in the red light and used her own tongs to lift her print, letting the excess developer drip back into the tray. "Patience is half of this craft," she said. "Rush it and you'll either underdevelop the shadows or blow out everything trying to force it."

Barnaby's own print began emerging beneath the surface, faint shapes resolving slowly into the portrait he'd shot of his dog that morning. He gasped quietly and reached to pull it too early, but Ottoline caught his wrist gently before he could disturb the developing chemistry.

"Not yet," she said, timing it against the small clock ticking on the counter. "Thirty more seconds, then we stop it and fix it properly." Barnaby waited, practically vibrating with anticipation, until Ottoline finally nodded and helped him transfer the print to the stop bath.
