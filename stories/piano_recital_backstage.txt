Nerves fluttered through Ophelia's stomach as she waited in the wings, watching the current performer finish their piece while running through her own opening measures silently in her head. Six years of lessons had led to this exact recital hall.

Her piano teacher, Barnaby, crouched beside her with a reassuring hand on her shoulder, offering last-minute encouragement before her name would be called. "You know this piece better than anyone in that audience," he said. "Trust your hands, they know what to do."

Ophelia nodded, still watching the stage as the previous performer took their bow to polite applause and exited toward the opposite wing. "What if I freeze on the second movement?" she whispered, the same fear that had plagued her rehearsals all week.

Barnaby squeezed her shoulder gently and smiled with the calm confidence of someone who'd guided dozens of students through exactly this moment. "Then you take a breath and keep going," he said. "Nobody in that audience knows the piece as well as you do."

The announcer called Ophelia's name, and she walked onto the stage with her chin up despite the fear still fluttering in her chest, settling onto the bench and finding her position on the keys. Barnaby watched from the wings, mouthing the opening notes silently along with her as she began to play.
