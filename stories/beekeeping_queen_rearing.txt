Grafting tool trembled slightly as Philomena transferred another day-old larva into its prepared queen cup, the delicate procedure requiring steady hands and careful technique under the magnifying lamp's bright light. Queen rearing demanded precision most beekeepers never attempted.

Her assistant, Osborne, prepared the cell bar frame, ensuring each grafted cup was properly spaced and secured before it would go into the finisher colony's care for the crucial development period. "Twenty cups grafted so far," he said.

Philomena completed another successful graft and set the cup carefully into its designated slot, checking her count against the day's target before continuing the meticulous process. "Ten more should do it," she said, focus evident in her steady hands.

Osborne double-checked the frame's completed cups, confirming each larva remained properly positioned in its royal jelly bed before the frame would need transport to the awaiting finisher colony. "These all look properly placed," he confirmed.

Philomena finished her final graft and carefully closed up the frame, satisfied with the batch's quality despite the demanding precision the entire process required. "Thirty queens started," she said. "Let's get these into the finisher colony quickly."
