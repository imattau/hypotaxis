Pins clicked softly against the wooden worktable as Yusuf measured the sleeve length one more time, chalk marks already fading from the fabric under his careful hands. The wedding was in five days, and the groom's jacket still needed its final fitting.

His apprentice, Lila, stood nearby threading a needle by the window's afternoon light, occasionally glancing over to watch how Yusuf smoothed the fabric before pinning each fold. "You never rush this part," she said. "Even when there's barely any time left."

"Rushing is how seams pucker," Yusuf said without looking up, his voice carrying the patience of someone who had made this exact argument many times before. "A groom notices a crooked hem on his wedding day even if no one else does."

The shop door chimed, and the groom himself stepped in, looking anxious as he shrugged out of his coat for the fitting. Yusuf gestured him toward the raised platform in front of the tall mirrors, already reaching for the half-finished jacket draped carefully over a nearby chair.

Lila watched as Yusuf worked in near silence, adjusting the shoulders with small, deliberate tugs until the fabric sat perfectly. When the groom turned to study himself in the mirror, his nervous expression finally eased into something like relief, and Lila allowed herself a small, proud smile from her corner of the shop.
