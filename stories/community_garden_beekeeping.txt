Late afternoon sun warmed the community garden's small apiary as Theodosia lifted a frame from the hive, checking the brood pattern while curious garden members watched from a respectful distance behind the demonstration fence. Educational hive checks always drew a crowd.

Her beekeeping assistant, Balthazar, narrated the inspection for the gathered onlookers, explaining what Theodosia was checking for as she worked through the hive's stacked boxes methodically. "See that solid pattern?" he said. "That tells us the queen's healthy and productive."

Theodosia held the frame up higher so the garden members could see the capped brood cells clearly, pointing out the difference between drone and worker cells for the curious crowd. "This is exactly what we want to see," she said. "No signs of disease or problems."

Balthazar fielded a question from a nervous-looking gardener about whether the bees would sting anyone standing this close to the demonstration area. "Not unless you swat at them," he reassured. "They're actually pretty calm during an inspection like this."

Theodosia returned the frame to its position and closed up the hive carefully, satisfied with another successful educational session that hopefully eased a few more people's fear of these essential pollinators. "Same time next month," she said. "Thanks everyone for coming out."
