Fragrant powder mounded on Seraphina's workbench as she mixed the sandalwood base with precise measures of resins and essential oils, working toward the exact formula her grandmother had taught her decades earlier. Handmade incense demanded patience most buyers never considered.

Her apprentice, Radomir, prepared the bamboo core sticks, measuring and cutting them to the standard length before setting them aside for the coating process that would soon begin. "Cores are ready," he said, arranging them in neat rows.

Seraphina tested the paste's consistency between her fingers, adjusting the moisture level slightly before it would be ready for the traditional rolling technique passed down through her family's generations of incense makers. "Almost right," she said. "Little more binding agent needed."

Radomir handed her the requested ingredient, watching closely as she demonstrated the rolling technique on a test stick, coating it evenly with the fragrant paste mixture. "The coating looks so even," he observed, impressed by her practiced skill.

Seraphina smiled and began rolling the full batch, the workshop filling with sandalwood's rich fragrance as stick after stick emerged perfectly coated and ready for the lengthy drying process. "Practice," she said. "You'll get there too, given enough sticks."
