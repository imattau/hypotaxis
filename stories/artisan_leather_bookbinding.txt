Fine leather stretched taut across Cassiopeia's workbench as she pared the edges thin with a specialized knife, preparing the material for the elaborate binding commission's cover boards. Full leather binding demanded skills few modern bookbinders still practiced.

Her apprentice, Fitzgerald, prepared the binder's board, cutting it to the exact dimensions specified by the text block's measurements before the leather covering could begin. "Boards are cut and squared," he said, checking the corners.

Cassiopeia examined his cutting work, running a straightedge along each side to confirm the precise ninety-degree angles essential for a properly square finished book. "Perfectly square," she said. "Now we can start the leather application."

Fitzgerald prepared the paste that would adhere the leather to the boards, mixing it to the exact consistency Cassiopeia had taught him produced the strongest, most durable bond for this type of binding. "Paste is ready," he confirmed.

Cassiopeia began carefully applying the pared leather to the prepared boards, working with the patient precision that would eventually produce a binding capable of lasting centuries with proper care. "This is where the real artistry begins," she said.
