Fine sawdust settled across Balthazar's workbench as he applied the first coat of finish to the walnut dining table, brushing with the grain in long, even strokes that would eventually build toward a deep, protective sheen. Six months of careful joinery led to this final stage.

His finishing apprentice, Guinevere, sanded the table's legs between coats, working through progressively finer grits to prepare the surface for the finish's next application. "These legs are ready for their next coat," she said, running a hand along the smooth wood.

Balthazar examined her sanding work closely, checking for any remaining rough spots that might telegraph through the finish and mar the final appearance. "Nice and smooth," he said. "Let's get the next coat on before the dust settles again."

Guinevere prepared the finish for application, straining it through a fine mesh to remove any particles that might create imperfections in the final surface. "Strained and ready," she said, handing him the prepared container.

Balthazar applied the next coat with the same careful attention he'd given every previous layer, building the finish gradually toward the deep luster the client had specifically requested. "Four more coats," he said, "and this table will be ready for someone's family dinners for generations."
