Wind battered the lighthouse windows as old Silas climbed the spiral stairs with a fresh oil canister, each step groaning familiarly beneath his boots. Below, the sea churned dark and restless against the rocks, whipped into whitecaps by the gathering storm.

His granddaughter Wren followed close behind, clutching a logbook against her chest to keep the pages from fluttering in the draft. "Grandpa, the radio said this storm's supposed to be the worst one in years," she said, raising her voice over the wind's howl.

"Storms come, storms go," Silas said, not slowing his pace as he reached the lamp room at the top. He set the canister down and began checking the mechanism with careful, unhurried movements, his fingers finding each familiar part by memory alone. "This light's guided ships through worse."

Wren moved to the window, pressing her palm against the cold glass as she peered out at the churning water below. A faint light blinked far out on the horizon, rising and falling with the swells. "There's a boat out there," she said, urgency creeping into her voice. "It looks like it's struggling."

Silas joined her at the window, following her pointing finger until he spotted the distant light himself. Without a word, he moved to the radio in the corner, cranking the old hand generator to life before picking up the receiver. Wren watched him work, the beam of the great lamp beginning its steady rotation above them, cutting through the storm toward the struggling boat.
