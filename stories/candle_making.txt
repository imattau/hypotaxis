Melted wax shimmered gold in the double boiler as Henrietta checked the thermometer clipped to its edge, waiting for the temperature to climb the last few degrees before adding fragrance oil. The workshop smelled of soy wax and the faint sweetness of the cinnamon blend she'd been testing all week.

Her business partner, Cassius, lined up the glass jars in neat rows, wicks already centered and secured with small wooden clips balanced across each rim. "Forty containers ready," he said. "That should cover this weekend's market order with a few extra for the shop shelf."

Henrietta nodded and checked the thermometer again, satisfied with the reading before removing the wax from heat. "Perfect pour temperature," she said. "Hand me the fragrance oil, I want to get this poured before it starts cooling too fast."

Cassius passed the measured fragrance oil over, and Henrietta stirred it thoroughly into the melted wax before beginning the careful process of pouring each jar to the same consistent level. Steam rose faintly from the jars as the hot wax settled around each centered wick.

"That's the last one," Henrietta said, setting down the pouring pot and surveying the rows of cooling candles. Cassius began labeling the finished jars, already thinking ahead to which scent combination they should test next for the holiday season.
