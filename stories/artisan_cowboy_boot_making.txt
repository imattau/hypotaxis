Exotic leather stretched across the last as Duke worked the boot's upper into shape, hand-stitching the intricate pattern that would give this custom pair its distinctive Western styling. Handmade cowboy boots demanded generations-old techniques.

His apprentice, Consuela, prepared the sole leather, cutting and skiving it to the precise thickness that would provide both durability and the flexibility serious boot wearers specifically valued. "Soles are cut and ready," she said.

Duke examined her leather preparation, checking the skiving depth against his standards before approving it for attachment to the boot's already-formed upper. "Perfect thickness," he said. "You're really developing an eye for this."

Consuela watched him continue hand-stitching the decorative pattern, absorbing the subtle needle technique that produced such consistently even stitches across the boot's elaborately tooled surface. "How many stitches does this pattern need?" she asked.

Duke continued his careful stitching, genuinely uncertain of the exact count after so many years of producing these elaborate custom patterns for his discerning clientele. "Thousands, probably," he said. "I stopped counting decades ago."
