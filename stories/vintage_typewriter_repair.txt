Tiny springs scattered across Cordwainer's felt-lined tray as he disassembled the typewriter's carriage return mechanism, tracing the source of the sticking key that had frustrated the machine's owner for months. This particular model dated back to the 1940s.

His workshop assistant, Guinevere, cleaned individual type bars with a small brush, removing decades of accumulated ink and dust from the mechanism's delicate moving parts. "Most of these type bars move freely now," she said. "Just the E and R still feel slightly stiff."

Cordwainer examined the carriage return spring closely, finding a small bend that explained the sticking he'd been chasing through the mechanism's other components. "Found the culprit," he said. "This spring's slightly warped, probably from age more than misuse."

Guinevere finished cleaning the stubborn type bars and tested their movement again, satisfied to find them moving smoothly after the thorough cleaning session. "E and R are free now too," she said. "Just the carriage return left to fix."

Cordwainer carefully straightened the warped spring and reinstalled it, testing the carriage return's action several times to confirm the fix had resolved the sticking issue completely. "That's it," he said. "This old girl's ready to type again."
