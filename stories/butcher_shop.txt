Sawdust crunched underfoot as Radomir worked the boning knife along the joint with practiced precision, separating the cut cleanly while barely glancing down at his hands. Forty years behind this same counter had taught him exactly where every joint wanted to give.

His son, Anatol, home for the summer before starting culinary school, watched from beside him, taking mental notes on the angle of the blade through each motion. "You never seem to think about it," he said. "How do your hands just know where to cut?"

Radomir set the finished cut aside and reached for the next piece, considering the question while he worked. "Same way your hands will know eventually," he said. "Right now you're thinking through every step. One day you won't be thinking at all, you'll just be doing."

Anatol picked up his own knife and attempted the same cut on a practice piece, moving slower and more deliberately than his father, checking the angle twice before committing to the stroke. The cut came out slightly ragged along one edge, but structurally sound.

Radomir inspected his son's work and gave an approving nod, setting both cuts side by side on the counter for comparison. "Not bad for someone who's been at this three weeks," he said. "Keep at it and by the time you leave for school, these hands will know plenty too."
