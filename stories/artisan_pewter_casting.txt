Molten pewter shimmered silver in the small crucible as Radomir poured it carefully into the carved stone mold, filling the cavity that would produce another commemorative medallion for the historical society's commission. Casting demanded exact timing.

His apprentice, Beatrix, prepared the next mold, dusting it with fine talc that would help the finished casting release cleanly once the metal had cooled and solidified properly. "This mold's ready for the next pour," she said.

Radomir set aside the cooling first casting and lifted the crucible again, checking the metal's temperature before proceeding with the next careful pour into Beatrix's prepared mold. "Temperature's perfect," he said. "Steady hands now."

Beatrix watched him complete the pour with practiced precision, the silvery metal filling the mold's every carved detail before beginning its cooling process toward a finished medallion. "That poured beautifully," she observed.

Radomir set the crucible aside and carefully opened the first mold, revealing a perfectly formed medallion with crisp, detailed relief work that would satisfy even the historical society's exacting standards. "Twelve more to go," he said. "Good thing this never gets old for me."
