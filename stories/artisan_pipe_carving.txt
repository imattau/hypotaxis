Briarwood dust coated Ottokar's workbench as he hollowed the pipe's bowl with a specialized bit, working toward the precise wall thickness that would ensure both durability and proper heat dissipation during smoking. Handmade pipes demanded exacting craftsmanship.

His apprentice, Marguerite, sanded a completed pipe's stem, smoothing the vulcanite material to the comfortable bite profile that pipe smokers specifically valued in quality handmade pieces. "This stem feels really comfortable already," she said.

Ottokar examined her sanding work, testing the stem's profile himself to confirm it matched the standard his discerning pipe-smoking clientele had come to expect from his workshop. "Excellent profile," he said. "You're really developing a feel for this."

Marguerite watched him continue hollowing the bowl, absorbing the subtle technique that prevented burnout while achieving the thin walls serious pipe smokers specifically requested for optimal smoking characteristics. "How thin can you actually go?" she asked.

Ottokar continued his careful hollowing, considering the question that balanced structural integrity against the thinness that improved a pipe's performance characteristics. "Thin as the wood allows," he said. "Any thinner risks a burnout nobody wants."
