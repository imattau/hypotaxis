Diesel engines rumbled steadily as Captain Odalys eased the ferry toward the dock, judging the current's pull against the wheel with the practiced touch of someone who'd made this crossing thousands of times. Rush hour commuters crowded the lower deck, anxious to disembark.

Her first mate, Cornelius, stood ready at the dock line, watching her approach angle carefully before signaling when the timing looked right for the final approach. "Looking good," he called up. "Current's pulling us right on schedule today."

Odalys adjusted the throttle slightly, feathering the ferry's approach to compensate for a sudden gust pushing against the hull's broad side. "Wind's fighting us a bit," she said. "Give me another few seconds before you throw that line."

Cornelius waited, line coiled and ready, watching the gap between hull and dock narrow steadily as Odalys made her final corrections. "Now," she called, and Cornelius threw the line in one practiced motion, securing it around the dock cleat.

Odalys cut the engines once the ferry settled against the dock's rubber fenders, satisfied with another smooth docking despite the tricky wind conditions. "Nice catch," she said. "Let's get these commuters off before the next crossing's crowd arrives."
