Wet clay spun steadily beneath Akemi's palms as she centered another lump on the wheel, beginning the multi-piece throwing process that would eventually become a traditional teapot's body, spout, and handle. Precise teapots demanded exceptional throwing skill.

Her apprentice, Hiroshi, prepared the spout piece separately, throwing the small cylindrical form that would later attach to the teapot's body once both pieces reached the correct leather-hard consistency. "Spout's thrown and ready to firm up," he said.

Akemi continued centering the larger body piece, checking the wall thickness with practiced fingers before beginning to open and raise the clay into the teapot's rounded form. "This centering feels really solid," she said.

Hiroshi watched her throwing technique closely, absorbing the subtle hand pressure and wheel speed coordination that produced such consistently even walls throughout the developing vessel's curved form. "Your walls are so even," he observed.

Akemi finished shaping the teapot's body and reached for her needle tool to trim the base, satisfied with how this particular piece was developing toward its eventual assembly with the separately thrown components. "Now we wait for leather-hard," she said.
