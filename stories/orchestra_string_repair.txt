Fine wood shavings drifted from Percival's carving tool as he shaped a replacement bass bar for the damaged cello, working the spruce with the meticulous attention the instrument's owner's livelihood depended on. A touring musician couldn't afford weeks without his instrument.

His workshop partner, Beatrix, examined the cello's cracked top plate under bright light, tracing the fracture's full extent before Percival's repair could properly begin. "The crack runs further than it looked from outside," she said. "We'll need a cleat here too."

Percival set down his carving tool and leaned over to examine the crack she'd indicated, nodding at the additional reinforcement she'd correctly identified as necessary. "Good catch," he said. "Better to reinforce now than have it reopen during a performance."

Beatrix prepared the small wooden cleats that would reinforce the crack from inside the instrument, cutting them to the precise curve needed to match the cello's interior surface. "These should hold it permanently," she said, testing one cleat's fit against the crack.

Percival nodded and returned to shaping the bass bar, the workshop falling into focused silence as both craftspeople worked toward returning the touring musician's instrument to full playing condition. "Three more days," he said, "and this old cello sings again."
