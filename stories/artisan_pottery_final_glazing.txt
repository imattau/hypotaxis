Brushes lay arranged across Wendell's workbench as he applied the final glaze layer to a set of stoneware plates, working the brush in smooth, even strokes that would produce the rich, layered color this particular recipe was known for. Final glazing demanded steady hands.

His apprentice, Cassiopeia, prepared the kiln shelves, applying kiln wash to prevent any glaze drips from fusing the plates permanently to the shelving during the upcoming firing process. "Shelves are washed and ready," she said.

Wendell finished glazing the current plate and set it carefully on the drying rack, checking the coverage evenness before moving to the next piece waiting in his glazing queue. "This layer's looking really rich," he said.

Cassiopeia watched him continue brushing glaze onto the remaining plates, absorbing the subtle stroke technique that avoided streaking while still building up the color depth this particular glaze recipe required. "The color's building nicely," she observed.

Wendell finished the final plate and stepped back to survey the completed set, satisfied with how consistently the rich glaze had applied across every piece in this particular dinnerware commission. "Ready for the kiln," he said. "Tomorrow we see the magic happen."
