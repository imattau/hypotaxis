Test tiles lined the counter as Wilhelmina examined the previous firing's results, comparing each glazed square against her notebook of recorded mixtures. The copper red she'd been chasing for months still wasn't quite right, too brown in some spots and too pink in others.

Her studio assistant, Octavian, mixed a fresh batch according to her latest adjustment, weighing each component carefully on the small digital scale. "This one's got half a percent more tin oxide," he said. "Should help stabilize that color shift you keep getting."

Wilhelmina picked up a fresh test tile and dipped it into the newly mixed glaze, letting the excess drip back into the bucket before setting it on the drying rack. "Let's fire six more tiles with small variations," she said. "I want to bracket around this recipe until we find the sweet spot."

Octavian labeled each tile carefully with the exact recipe variation, arranging them in order on the kiln shelf for the next firing cycle. "Kiln's got room for all six plus your regular production pieces," he said, checking the shelf space one more time.

Wilhelmina nodded and made a final note in her recipe book before closing it, satisfied with the systematic approach even if the perfect red remained frustratingly elusive. "Fire it tonight," she said. "By tomorrow we'll finally know if this batch cracked the code."
