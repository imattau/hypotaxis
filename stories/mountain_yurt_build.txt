Frost coated the meadow grass as Bartholomew and his crew unloaded the yurt's lattice wall sections from the truck bed, breath clouding in the crisp morning air at nine thousand feet elevation. The remote campsite needed permanent shelter before winter fully arrived.

His construction partner, Ottavia, unrolled the canvas roof covering, checking each grommet for damage before they began the assembly process. "Lattice looks intact from the drive up here," she said. "Let's get the frame standing before the wind picks up."

Bartholomew and Ottavia worked together to expand the accordion-style lattice wall into a full circle, securing the connection points methodically as the structure's shape began taking form. "Door frame goes here," he said, positioning the wooden threshold at the circle's southern point.

Ottavia helped raise the central roof ring, the yurt's crown piece that would eventually support the radiating rafters connecting it to the lattice walls below. "Rafters next," she said, already sorting the numbered poles into the order they'd need for installation.

Bartholomew climbed a short ladder to help seat the first rafter into the roof ring's designated slot, working carefully in the thin mountain air that made every task feel slightly harder than it should. "Once these are up," he said, "the canvas goes on fast. We'll have shelter by sundown."
