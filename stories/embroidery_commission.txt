Silk threads glinted under the lamp as Persimmon worked the satin stitch along the commissioned piece's border, needle catching the light with each careful pass through the taut fabric. The client wanted the family crest finished before the anniversary dinner, now less than a week away.

Her student, Bartholomew, practiced a simpler pattern on a separate hoop nearby, occasionally glancing over to compare his tension against Persimmon's noticeably smoother stitches. "How do you keep the thread from bunching like mine does?" he asked, holding up his slightly puckered sample.

Persimmon paused her work to examine his hoop, gently pointing out where his stitches had pulled too tight against the fabric's weave. "You're pulling harder than the thread needs," she said. "Let the needle do the work, don't fight the tension so much."

Bartholomew loosened his grip and tried the next few stitches more gently, watching with satisfaction as the fabric lay flatter beneath the looser tension. "That's already better," he said, comparing the new stitches against his earlier, puckered attempts.

Persimmon nodded approvingly and returned to her own work, the crest's golden thread catching the lamplight as she worked steadily toward the deadline. "Keep practicing that tension," she said. "You'll have it fully internalized within another few projects."
