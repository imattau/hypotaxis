Steel rings clicked steadily as Beatrix wove another row into the chainmail shirt, closing each connection with a small pair of pliers before adding it to the growing garment's expanding pattern. Historical reenactment armor demanded authentic construction methods.

Her apprentice, Radomir, prepared additional rings by cutting coiled wire into precise segments, ensuring each piece matched the exact diameter needed for proper interlocking with the shirt's existing pattern. "These rings are cut and ready," he said.

Beatrix examined the rings he'd prepared, checking their consistency before incorporating them into her weaving pattern that was slowly transforming loose wire into functional protective garment. "Good consistency," she said. "Keep them coming at this rate."

Radomir continued cutting rings while Beatrix wove, the workshop filling with the rhythmic clicking of metal against metal as the chainmail shirt grew row by painstaking row. "How many rings does a full shirt need?" he asked.

Beatrix calculated based on the shirt's remaining coverage area, arriving at a number that represented many more hours of careful, repetitive work before this particular commission would be complete. "About eight thousand more," she said. "Job security, at least."
