Frost crept across the greenhouse panes as Solveig checked the thermometer for the third time that hour, worry creasing her forehead at how fast the temperature had dropped since sunset. An unexpected cold snap had rolled in early, threatening the seedlings she'd spent all spring nurturing.

Her farmhand, Percival, hauled in another armful of old blankets, draping them carefully over the most vulnerable rows of tomato starts near the door. "Heater's running full blast," he said, breath visible even inside the greenhouse now. "Not sure it's enough on its own tonight."

Solveig nodded grimly and grabbed a stack of empty buckets, filling them with water from the hose bib and setting them strategically between the rows. "Water holds heat longer than air," she said. "It'll radiate out slowly overnight and might buy us a few extra degrees where it matters most."

Percival finished draping the last blanket and grabbed a battery-powered lantern, checking the thermometer readings at the far end of the greenhouse where the cold seemed to be settling worst. "Corner's already at thirty-four," he called back. "We might lose the peppers over there no matter what we do."

Solveig hurried over with two more water buckets, setting them close to the struggling pepper starts before straightening up and surveying the dimly lit rows one final time. "Then we watch it through the night," she said. "I'm not losing this crop to one bad cold front without a fight."
