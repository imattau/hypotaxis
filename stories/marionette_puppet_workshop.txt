Thin control strings tangled slightly as Ottoline worked to restring the marionette's left arm, testing the joint's range of motion after replacing a worn wooden hinge. The theater company's oldest puppet needed to perform again by next weekend's show.

Her puppet-building apprentice, Balthazar, carved a replacement hand for a different marionette nearby, matching the proportions carefully against the character's established design sketches. "This hand's nearly done," he said. "Should I start painting it once it's sanded smooth?"

Ottoline glanced over at his carving between adjustments to her own strings, nodding approvingly at the proportions he'd achieved on the delicate wooden fingers. "Looks right to me," she said. "Prime it first though, raw wood won't take the paint evenly."

Balthazar set the carved hand aside to test his string tension on the marionette Ottoline had finished restringing, walking the puppet through a few basic movements to check its responsiveness. "Arm's moving smoothly now," he observed, manipulating the control bar carefully.

Ottoline took the puppet from him and ran it through a more complex sequence, satisfied with how naturally the restrung arm now moved through its full range. "That'll hold for the show," she said. "One more puppet fixed, only three more to check before Friday."
