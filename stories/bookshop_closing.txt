Rain streaked the front window as Delphine climbed the rolling ladder to reach the top shelf, pulling down a stack of dusty hardcovers for the final inventory count. The "Everything Must Go" sign taped to the door had been up for three weeks now, and the shelves were noticeably thinner than they used to be.

Her longtime employee, Ambrose, sat cross-legged on the floor sorting books into boxes labeled by genre, moving slower than usual as he handled each one. "I still can't believe we're actually doing this," he said, setting a worn poetry collection gently into its box. "Twenty-two years and it just ends on a Tuesday."

Delphine climbed back down the ladder, arms full, and set the stack beside him with a tired sigh. "It doesn't feel real to me either," she admitted. "But the lease is up and I can't keep fighting the numbers forever, not with the rent they're asking now."

Ambrose picked up an old customer's handwritten note tucked inside one of the returns, smiling faintly at whatever was written there before adding it to a small pile of keepsakes he'd been collecting all week. "At least we're closing on our own terms," he said. "Better than limping along until the landlord does it for us."

Delphine nodded and glanced around the half-empty shop, the afternoon light through the rain-streaked window casting long gray shadows across the remaining shelves. "Let's finish the poetry section," she said, "and then I think we've earned a drink somewhere that isn't this dusty."
