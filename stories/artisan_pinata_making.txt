Colorful tissue paper fringed Rosalind's fingers as she layered another section onto the papier-mache donkey's body, building up the traditional festive covering one careful strip at a time. The birthday party commission needed finishing by tomorrow.

Her nephew, Balthazar, prepared the next color's tissue strips, cutting the fringe pattern that would give the finished pinata its distinctive textured, colorful appearance once fully applied. "These pink strips are cut and ready," he said.

Rosalind took the offered strips and continued layering them onto the donkey's form, working to achieve the smooth color transitions the birthday child's parents had specifically requested for this custom piece. "These colors are blending beautifully," she said.

Balthazar watched her technique closely, absorbing the subtle overlap and layering approach that produced such professional-looking results compared to his own earlier, clumsier attempts at pinata decoration. "I think I'm finally getting this," he observed.

Rosalind smiled and continued her careful layering, the donkey's form gradually transforming from plain papier-mache into a vibrant, candy-filled celebration centerpiece. "Almost done," she said. "This kid's going to love smashing this tomorrow."
