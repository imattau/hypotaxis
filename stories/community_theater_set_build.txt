Sawdust drifted through the theater's backstage workshop as Reginald measured another length of lumber for the castle set's turret, checking his calculations twice against the director's approved design sketch. Opening night was ten days away, and the set was barely half finished.

His set-building volunteer, Philomena, painted a completed wall panel with weathered stone texture, working quickly to keep pace with the framing crew's rapid progress. "This panel's ready for the second coat," she said. "Should be dry enough by tomorrow to move into position."

Reginald finished his measurement and made the cut with the circular saw, the whine of the blade briefly drowning out the workshop's usual chatter. "Turret's coming together faster than I expected," he said. "We might actually hit the deadline this time."

Philomena set down her brush and stepped back to examine the wall panel's texture under the workshop's bright lights, satisfied with how convincingly the faux stone had turned out. "The lighting designer's going to love how this catches the spotlights," she said.

Reginald hefted the newly cut lumber toward the turret frame, already visualizing how the piece would fit into the growing structure. "Let's get this attached before we lose the afternoon," he said. "Ten days isn't as much time as it sounds."
