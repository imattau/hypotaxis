Cold mountain air bit at Farouk's fingers as he adjusted the telescope's focus, the observatory dome open to a sky thick with stars far from any city light. His research partner, Greta, hunched over a laptop nearby, cross-referencing coordinates against the night's target list.

"The seeing conditions are better than the forecast promised," Greta said, not looking up from the screen. "If this holds we might actually get clean data on that binary system tonight." Farouk grunted in agreement, still fine-tuning the alignment with careful, practiced turns of the focus knob.

A sudden gust rattled the dome's shutter, and Farouk swore under his breath as the telescope's tracking briefly lost the target star. "Recalibrating," he called out, fingers already moving across the control panel to reset the mount's position. Greta paused her typing to watch the readout, waiting for the numbers to settle.

"Got it back," Farouk said after a tense half-minute, the star sliding back into the crosshairs on the monitor. Greta exhaled and resumed her notes, occasionally glancing up at the dome's slow rotation as it tracked the sky's movement overhead.

By the time the first hint of dawn touched the eastern horizon, they had gathered nearly six hours of clean observational data, and Greta was already sketching preliminary conclusions in the margins of her notebook. Farouk closed the dome slowly, stretching stiff shoulders as the telescope powered down for the day.
