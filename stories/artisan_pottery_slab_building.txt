Rolled clay slabs lay smooth across Persimmon's canvas-covered table as she scored the edges for another rectangular planter box, working the traditional slab construction technique that produced clean, architectural forms. Slab building rewarded careful planning.

Her apprentice, Cassius, prepared the joining slip, mixing it to the specific consistency that would bond the scored edges together securely once the pieces were assembled into their final box form. "Slip's ready and mixed," he said.

Persimmon scored the second wall panel's edge, applying slip before pressing the two pieces together firmly to create a strong, seamless corner joint for the developing planter box. "This joint's looking solid already," she said.

Cassius watched her continue assembling the remaining walls, absorbing the subtle technique that kept each corner square and structurally sound despite the soft, malleable nature of the wet clay. "How do you keep everything so square?" he asked.

Persimmon continued her careful assembly, checking each new joint against a small level to ensure the box's walls remained perfectly vertical throughout construction. "Constant checking," she said. "Clay doesn't forgive assumptions."
