Early morning fog still clung to the grass as Odalys arranged wooden crates of tomatoes and peppers along her stall table, breath visible in the cool air. Around her, other vendors called greetings and hauled canopies upright against the pale sky.

Her nephew Teo trailed behind with a crate of fresh basil bundles, setting them down with exaggerated care beside the tomatoes. "Mrs. Halloway already asked if we'd have the yellow squash today," he said, brushing dirt from his hands onto his apron.

"Tell her it's coming next week," Odalys replied, straightening a hand-lettered sign advertising heirloom varieties. She glanced toward the market entrance, where the first customers were beginning to trickle in, coffee cups in hand and canvas bags slung over their shoulders.

A woman in a bright red scarf approached the stall first, picking up a tomato and turning it over in her palm, inspecting it with obvious appreciation. "These look wonderful," she said. "Are they the ones you had last week?" Odalys smiled, wiping her hands on her apron before answering.

"Same variety, fresh picked this morning," she said. Teo began bagging a selection of peppers the woman pointed to, weighing them carefully on the small scale beside the register. Behind them, the market slowly filled with the rising murmur of conversation and the clatter of crates, the smell of fresh bread drifting over from a stall two rows down.
