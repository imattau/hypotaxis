Cables snaked across the stage floor as Emerentia climbed the ladder toward the lighting bar, wrench in hand to adjust the angle of a stubborn spotlight that had been drifting out of position all week. Opening night for the theater's spring production was tomorrow, and the lighting design still needed final touches.

Her lighting assistant, Percival, stood at the control board below, calling out adjustments as he watched the beam's position on the empty stage. "A touch more to stage left," he said. "That should hit the mark where Juliet delivers her final line."

Emerentia adjusted the fixture slightly and locked it back into position, glancing down to check Percival's reaction to the new angle. "How's that?" she called down. "Better, or did I overcorrect?"

Percival checked the beam against the taped mark on the stage floor and gave a thumbs up, satisfied with the new positioning. "Perfect," he said. "That's the last one on this bar. Should we run the full cue sequence now?"

Emerentia climbed down from the ladder and dusted off her hands, already mentally reviewing the evening's remaining checklist before the final dress rehearsal began. "Let's run it," she said. "I want zero surprises when the actual audience shows up tomorrow night."
