Wood shavings curled onto the workbench as Anneliese carefully sanded the violin's cracked rib, magnifying loupe pushed up on her forehead between careful passes. The instrument had belonged to the same family for three generations, and its owner had been near tears when he'd brought it in the week before.

Her workshop assistant, Osgood, mixed a small batch of hide glue at the counter behind her, testing its consistency by dragging a brush through it slowly. "How bad was the crack once you got the top off?" he asked, glancing over at the exposed interior of the instrument.

"Worse than it looked from outside," Anneliese said, not looking up from her work. "But cleanly split, at least. Should glue back almost invisibly once I get the clamps positioned right." She set down the sandpaper and reached for a set of small wooden cauls shaped to match the violin's curve.

Osgood brought the warm glue over carefully, watching as Anneliese applied a thin, even line along the crack with a fine brush before pressing the cauls into place. "That's got to be nerve-wracking," he said. "One crooked clamp and you've ruined a hundred-year-old violin."

"That's why I only do this after coffee, never before," Anneliese said dryly, tightening the last clamp with practiced precision. She stepped back to examine her work under the bright lamp, satisfied with the nearly invisible seam already forming along the repaired rib.
