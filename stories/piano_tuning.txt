Felt hammers clicked softly against strings as Rosalind worked her way up the keyboard, tuning fork ringing faintly against her wrist between each careful adjustment. The old upright had sat untouched in the church basement for years, and half its strings had drifted badly out of tune.

Her apprentice, Callahan, sat nearby with a notepad, logging each string's measurement as she called it out, occasionally wincing at a particularly sour note before she corrected it. "That's the fourth string that's snapped this week," he said. "Are these just old, or is something wrong with the whole piano?"

Rosalind paused, resting her tuning lever against the pinblock, and considered the question seriously before answering. "Both, probably," she said. "Age makes strings brittle, and a piano left in a damp basement for a decade doesn't help matters much either." She resumed her slow climb up the register.

Callahan flipped to a fresh page and kept logging, occasionally plucking a string himself to compare its pitch against Rosalind's reference tone. "This one sounds right to me," he offered hesitantly, and Rosalind checked it against her fork before nodding in agreement, letting him log it without correction.

By the time she reached the top octave, the basement's single overhead light had dimmed twice from a flickering bulb, and Rosalind finally sat back to play a slow scale across the freshly tuned keys. "Not perfect," she said, "but it'll hold through Sunday's service, which is all we need right now."
