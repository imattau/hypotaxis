Cool damp air settled over Guillermina's shoulders as she descended the stone steps into the aging cave, headlamp cutting through the darkness toward the long rows of wheels resting on wooden shelves. The cave had held a steady fifty-two degrees for two hundred years, long before anyone thought to measure it.

Her cellar hand, Ottavia, followed close behind carrying a brushing kit, ready to tend to the wheels that needed their monthly rind maintenance. "Row three's looking a little dry again," she said, running gloved fingers along one wheel's crusted surface. "Might need another brine wipe this week."

Guillermina knelt to inspect the wheel Ottavia mentioned, turning it gently to check the rind's development on all sides before setting it back precisely where it had been resting. "Good catch," she said. "Humidity's been fighting us all month. Let's get the whole row brined before it spreads."

Ottavia set down her kit and began mixing the brine solution in a small bucket, the salty smell mixing with the cave's earthy must as she worked. Guillermina moved down the row methodically, flipping wheels and checking each one's progress with the practiced efficiency of someone who'd done this thousands of times.

By the time they'd finished the row, both women's hands were pruned and cold from the damp work, but satisfied with the wheels' steady progress toward readiness. "Another six weeks on this batch," Guillermina said, climbing back toward the stairs. "Then we finally get to taste if all this fuss was worth it."
