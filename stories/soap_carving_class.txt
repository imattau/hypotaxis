Soft shavings curled away from Marguerite's carving knife as she demonstrated the rose petal technique on a bar of soap, the beginner class watching closely from their own workstations. Saturday craft classes always drew an enthusiastic mix of skill levels.

Her newest student, Cyrus, attempted his own petal with visibly less confidence, the knife wobbling slightly as he tried to replicate the smooth curve she'd just shown. "Mine keeps looking lumpy," he said, holding up his uneven attempt for comparison.

Marguerite set down her own work and leaned over to examine his carving, gently repositioning his grip on the small knife before offering feedback. "You're holding it too rigid," she said. "Let your wrist rotate with the curve instead of forcing straight cuts."

Cyrus adjusted his grip and attempted the petal again, this time allowing his wrist more freedom of movement as she'd suggested, and the resulting curve looked noticeably smoother. "That's already better," he said, genuine surprise crossing his face.

Marguerite nodded approvingly and returned to her own rose, adding a few more petals while keeping an eye on her students' continued progress around the room. "Keep practicing that wrist motion," she said. "By the end of class you'll have a full rose of your own."
