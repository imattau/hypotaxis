Human hair strands lay bundled across Philomena's workbench as she hand-knotted individual strands into the lace foundation, building the theatrical wig strand by painstaking strand for the opera's upcoming production. This technique hadn't changed in over a century.

Her apprentice, Ottokar, sorted the hair by color and length, preparing bundles that matched the specific gradient the character's wig design required for its dramatic ombre effect. "This bundle matches the root color exactly," he said.

Philomena examined the bundle he offered, confirming the shade matched before continuing her methodical knotting work across the lace foundation's growing hairline. "Perfect match," she said. "Hand me the next section when you're ready."

Ottokar continued sorting while Philomena worked, occasionally offering encouragement as she progressed steadily across the demanding, detail-intensive knotting process required for a natural-looking hairline. "The density is looking really natural," he observed.

Philomena smiled and continued her careful work, knowing this particular wig needed to withstand nightly performances for the opera's entire run without losing its carefully crafted appearance. "Three more weeks," she said. "Then she'll finally take the stage."
