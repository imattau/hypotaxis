Milky glaze coated Theodosia's gloved fingers as she dipped another bisqueware bowl into the glaze bucket, timing the submersion precisely to achieve the coating thickness this particular recipe required for proper firing results. Glaze application demanded consistent technique.

Her apprentice, Ferdinand, wiped excess glaze from each dipped piece's foot ring, preventing the pieces from fusing to kiln shelves during the firing process that would transform the chalky coating into glossy finish. "Feet are clean and ready," he said.

Theodosia examined his wiping work, checking for any remaining glaze that might cause fusion problems before approving the pieces for their place in the kiln loading queue. "Perfectly clean," she said. "That attention to detail saves us headaches later."

Ferdinand watched her continue dipping pieces in steady rhythm, absorbing the subtle timing that determined coating thickness across the various bowl sizes waiting for their turn in the glaze bucket. "How do you know the right dip time?" he asked.

Theodosia continued her methodical dipping, explaining the visual cues and timing experience that guided her decisions throughout this crucial pre-firing stage of the ceramics process. "Count and watch," she said. "The glaze tells you when it's ready."
