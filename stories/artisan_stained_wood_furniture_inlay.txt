Contrasting wood veneers lay cut precisely as Ottavia fitted another piece into the tabletop's elaborate marquetry pattern, building the floral design one carefully shaped fragment at a time. Custom inlay work demanded extraordinary patience.

Her apprentice, Fitzwilliam, prepared the next veneer piece, cutting it against a paper template to match the exact shape needed for the pattern's next section according to Ottavia's detailed design. "This piece is cut and ready," he said.

Ottavia examined the piece he'd prepared, testing its fit against the developing pattern before applying adhesive to permanently secure it into position within the tabletop's surface. "Perfect fit," she said. "The grain direction really makes this pop."

Fitzwilliam watched her continue placing pieces, absorbing the subtle color and grain matching decisions that transformed simple wood fragments into a convincingly organic-looking floral composition. "How do you choose which wood for which petal?" he asked.

Ottavia continued her careful placement, explaining the color theory and grain considerations that guided her selections throughout this demanding, detail-intensive marquetry process. "Study the actual flower," she said. "Wood grain can mimic almost anything."
