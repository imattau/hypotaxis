Wind tugged at Nadia's sunhat as she knelt among the raised planter boxes, checking the drip irrigation lines that snaked between rows of tomatoes and climbing beans twelve stories above the city street. The rooftop garden had become her refuge after work, a patch of green stubbornly thriving above the concrete.

Her downstairs neighbor, Otto, pushed through the access door carrying two watering cans, water sloshing over the rims with each careful step. "The pepper plants look rough," he said, setting the cans down near the far corner. "I think that heat wave last week hit them harder than the tomatoes."

Nadia moved over to inspect the wilting leaves, frowning as she checked the soil moisture with two fingers pressed into the dirt. "They're not getting enough water through this line," she said. "The emitter's probably clogged again. Can you grab the toolkit from the shed?"

Otto jogged off toward the small storage shed in the corner of the roof, returning a minute later with a small pouch of tools that Nadia used to unclip and flush the stubborn emitter. Water sputtered out in an uneven spray before finally settling into a steady, even drip across the pepper bed.

"That should fix it," Nadia said, wiping her hands on her jeans and standing to survey the rest of the garden bathed in the last of the evening light. Otto nodded and picked up the watering cans again, already moving toward the row of beans that still needed their evening drink.
