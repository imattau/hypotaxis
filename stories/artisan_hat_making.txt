Steam rose from the felt cone as Odalys worked it over the wooden hat block, shaping the material into the crown's curved form using techniques her millinery training had taken years to fully master. Custom hats demanded patience most buyers never considered.

Her apprentice, Bartholomew, prepared the grosgrain ribbon that would eventually trim the finished hat, measuring and cutting it to the precise length the design called for. "Ribbon's ready when you need it," he said.

Odalys continued shaping the steamed felt, checking the crown's symmetry against her template before it would be ready to set and dry into its final permanent form. "This block gives such a clean line," she said, admiring the developing shape.

Bartholomew watched her blocking technique closely, absorbing the subtle hand pressure that guided the felt smoothly over the wooden form without creating unwanted wrinkles or distortions. "How long does it need to set?" he asked.

Odalys secured the shaped felt with pins to hold its form during drying, calculating the hours still needed before the hat would be ready for its ribbon trim and finishing touches. "Overnight," she said. "Good things take their proper time."
