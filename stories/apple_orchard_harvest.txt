Ladder braced against the branch, Casimir reached carefully into the canopy to twist another apple free, dropping it gently into the picking bag strapped across his chest. The orchard's oldest trees always ripened first, and this row needed clearing before the weekend's forecast rain.

His seasonal helper, Ottavia, worked the row beside him, filling her own bag steadily while occasionally calling out when she spotted a particularly impressive cluster higher up. "This tree's loaded," she said. "Might be our best producer this whole orchard."

Casimir climbed down to empty his full bag into the waiting bin, glancing up at the tree Ottavia had praised with a practiced, appraising eye. "Old Man Fitzwilliam's tree," he said, using the orchard's nickname for the ancient specimen. "Always has been the best producer, going back decades."

Ottavia climbed her own ladder to reach a higher cluster, twisting each apple carefully to avoid damaging the delicate spurs that would produce next year's fruit. "Should we start the next row after this one?" she asked, glancing at the sun's position to gauge remaining daylight.

Casimir nodded and hefted another full bag toward the bin, calculating how many more rows they could realistically finish before dark. "Two more rows if we keep this pace," he said. "Then we call it a day and finish the rest tomorrow."
