Bamboo frames stood in neat rows across Kavi's workshop as he stretched rice paper over another lantern's curved ribs, working the delicate material carefully to avoid tears that would ruin hours of preparation. The festival needed two hundred lanterns by week's end.

His daughter, Noor, painted traditional patterns onto the completed lanterns, her brush moving with practiced confidence across the translucent paper surface that would eventually glow warmly from within. "This design's coming together nicely," she said.

Kavi finished stretching his current lantern and examined her painted work, admiring the intricate detail she'd achieved despite the paper's delicate, unforgiving surface. "Beautiful work," he said. "Your patterns keep getting more elaborate."

Noor set her finished lantern aside to dry and reached for the next blank frame Kavi had prepared, settling into the rhythm they'd established over the week's intensive production schedule. "How many do we have left?" she asked.

Kavi counted the remaining frames stacked against the workshop wall, calculating the work still needed before the festival's opening ceremony would require the full lantern display. "Sixty more," he said. "We'll make it, working together like this."
