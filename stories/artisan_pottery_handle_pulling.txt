Wet clay stretched smoothly between Bartholomew's fingers as he pulled another mug handle, working the traditional technique that shaped a simple clay lump into a graceful, functional curve through repeated downward strokes. Handle pulling demanded consistent motion.

His apprentice, Xiomara, prepared the mugs waiting for handles, scoring and slipping the attachment points where each pulled handle would eventually be joined to its corresponding body. "These mugs are scored and ready," she said.

Bartholomew examined the scored attachment points, confirming their roughness would provide adequate grip for the slip before beginning the handle attachment process on this particular batch. "Good scoring," he said. "Let's get these handles attached."

Xiomara watched him attach the freshly pulled handle, absorbing the technique that balanced the handle's curve against the mug's proportions for both visual appeal and comfortable everyday use. "The proportions look perfect," she observed.

Bartholomew continued attaching handles to the remaining mugs, working steadily through the batch while explaining subtle adjustments he made for each mug's slightly different size and shape. "Every mug's a little different," he said. "The handle should feel like it belongs."
