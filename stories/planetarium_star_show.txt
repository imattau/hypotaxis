Dome lights dimmed slowly as Ignatius took his position at the projector controls, the evening's star show about to begin for a school group already settling into the tiered seating above him. Tuesday evenings always brought the elementary field trips.

His assistant, Wilhelmina, moved through the rows handing out star charts, answering a curious student's question about whether the projector could really show every star in the sky. "Not every single one," she said. "But close to nine thousand of the brightest."

Ignatius began the narration as the dome bloomed with simulated stars, his practiced voice guiding the audience through the winter constellations currently visible in the actual night sky outside. Wilhelmina found a seat near the control booth, watching the audience's reactions.

A bright meteor simulation streaked across the dome during the presentation's highlight moment, drawing gasps and scattered applause from the delighted students filling the tiered seats. Ignatius smiled slightly, having timed the effect perfectly for maximum impact.

Wilhelmina made a note about which parts of the show landed best with this younger audience, information that would help refine future presentations for similar school groups. "Great show," she told Ignatius afterward. "That meteor timing gets them every time."
