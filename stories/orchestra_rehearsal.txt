Baton raised, Conductor Wilhelmina signaled the strings section to begin again from measure forty, the symphony's difficult transition still not landing the way she needed for next week's premiere. The rehearsal hall's acoustics made every imperfection painfully audible.

Her concertmaster, Desmond, lowered his violin and caught her eye, sensing another stop was coming before she even raised her hand. "The tempo shift feels rushed," he said. "Maybe ease into it over two bars instead of one?"

Wilhelmina considered his suggestion, tapping her baton thoughtfully against her palm while the orchestra waited in patient silence for her decision. "Let's try it your way," she said. "From measure thirty-eight this time, give yourselves room to breathe into the shift."

Desmond nodded and relayed a quiet signal to the rest of the string section, adjusting his bow position in preparation for the passage's demanding technical requirements. The orchestra settled back into position, instruments raised and ready for Wilhelmina's downbeat.

This time the transition flowed smoothly, the tempo shift landing with the natural ease Wilhelmina had been chasing through three separate rehearsals. "Much better," she said, allowing herself a rare smile. "Let's take it from the top once more, just to be certain."
