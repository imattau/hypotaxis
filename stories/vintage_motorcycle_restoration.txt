Chrome gleamed under the garage's work light as Osborne carefully reassembled the vintage motorcycle's carburetor, checking each gasket against his parts diagram before tightening the final connections. This particular model had sat in a barn for thirty years.

His restoration partner, Guinevere, cleaned the fuel tank's interior with a specialized solvent, working to remove decades of accumulated rust and old gasoline residue from the tank's delicate metal surface. "Tank's looking clean," she said. "No pinholes that I can find."

Osborne finished the carburetor reassembly and set it aside carefully, satisfied with how the rebuild had gone despite the parts' considerable age and the scarcity of replacements. "Good news," he said. "Let's get this mounted before we lose the afternoon light."

Guinevere finished her tank inspection and began preparing it for reinstallation, double-checking the mounting hardware against the motorcycle's original specifications from the shop manual. "Tank's ready when you are," she said, hefting it carefully toward the frame.

Osborne helped guide the tank into position, both of them working together to secure the mounting bolts with the careful precision this rare motorcycle deserved. "Getting close now," he said. "This old girl might actually run again before the season's out."
