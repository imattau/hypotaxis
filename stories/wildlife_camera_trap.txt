Damp forest air clung to Rosalind's jacket as she knelt beside the camera trap strapped to an old cedar, checking the memory card for the past week's captures before resetting the device. The research grant depended on documenting the elusive lynx population in this valley.

Her field assistant, Torbjorn, marked the GPS coordinates in his rugged notebook, cross-referencing the location against the grid pattern they'd established for the survey area. "This is trap fourteen," he said. "We've got six more to check before we lose daylight."

Rosalind scrolled through the camera's captured images, her breath catching at a clear nighttime shot of a lynx passing directly in front of the lens. "We got one," she said, turning the screen so Torbjorn could see. "First confirmed sighting on this whole grid."

Torbjorn leaned in to examine the image closely, noting the animal's distinctive ear tufts and confirming it matched the species they'd been hoping to document. "Beautiful shot," he said. "This alone might justify the whole season's funding."

Rosalind carefully repositioned the camera and replaced its batteries, resetting it for another two weeks of silent monitoring before they moved on to the next trap location. "Let's hope trap fifteen has something just as good," she said, already hiking toward the next coordinate on Torbjorn's list.
