Copper sheet rang softly as Wendell raised the bowl's form with careful hammer strikes, working the metal against his stake in the traditional raising technique that shaped flat sheet into three-dimensional vessels. This kettle commission required exact proportions.

His apprentice, Anastasiya, annealed the next section of copper, heating it to the precise temperature that would soften the work-hardened metal before Wendell could continue shaping without risk of cracking. "This piece is ready for you," she said.

Wendell took the annealed copper and examined its color, confirming the proper temperature had been reached before continuing his raising work on the kettle's developing curved form. "Perfect anneal," he said. "You're really getting the timing down."

Anastasiya prepared the next section for annealing, watching Wendell's hammer work closely to absorb the subtle rhythm and pressure that seemed to guide the metal exactly where he wanted it to go. "The curve's looking really smooth," she observed.

Wendell finished another section of raising and checked the form against his template, satisfied with how the kettle's shape was developing toward the client's specific design requirements. "Getting there," he said. "This one's going to be beautiful."
