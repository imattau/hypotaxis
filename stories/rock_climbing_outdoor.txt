Granite dust coated Fitzwilliam's fingertips as he chalked up at the base of the sheer cliff face, craning his neck to trace the route's line up toward the anchor bolts glinting far above. Months of training had led to this exact route, one he'd failed twice already this season.

His belayer, Perpetua, double-checked the rope's figure-eight knot at his harness before feeding slack through her belay device. "Rope's good," she said. "Take your time on that crux move, you've got plenty of daylight left."

Fitzwilliam nodded and began climbing, moving methodically through the route's lower section where he'd never had trouble before, saving his energy for the difficult sequence higher up. The granite felt cool and solid beneath his fingers despite the afternoon sun.

Perpetua fed rope steadily as he climbed, calling up occasional encouragement while keeping her attention fixed on his movement in case of a sudden fall. "You're past your last high point," she called. "New territory now, you've got this."

Fitzwilliam reached the crux move and paused, studying the small crimp holds before committing to the sequence that had shut him down twice before. This time his fingers held, and he pulled through cleanly, a triumphant shout escaping him as he clipped the next bolt above.
