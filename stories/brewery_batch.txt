Steam rose thick off the mash tun as Aurelio stirred the grain bed slowly, checking the temperature gauge with a practiced glance before adjusting the burner underneath. The brewery's back room smelled of malt and boiling wort, warm and slightly sweet in the morning air.

His assistant brewer, Philomena, measured out the hop additions into small labeled containers, double-checking each amount against the recipe sheet taped to the wall above the workstation. "Bittering hops go in at the boil," she said. "Aroma addition's for the last five minutes, right?"

Aurelio nodded without looking up from the mash tun, focused on maintaining the steady temperature the recipe called for. "Right," he said. "And don't forget the whirlpool addition after flame-out, that's what's going to give this batch its citrus nose."

Philomena set the labeled hop bags in order near the kettle, ready for each addition at the correct time during the boil that would start once the mash was fully converted. "Gravity reading looks good," she said, checking the hydrometer sample she'd pulled a few minutes earlier.

Aurelio finally straightened up from the mash tun and wiped his hands on his apron, satisfied with the conversion progress. "Let's vorlauf and start running off to the kettle," he said. "Long day ahead of us before this batch is finally in the fermenter."
