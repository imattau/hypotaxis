Steam billowed thick from the evaporator as Guillermina monitored the boiling sap's progress, checking its density against a refractometer that would confirm when the liquid had concentrated enough to become finished syrup. Sugaring season lasted only a few precious weeks.

Her son, Torvald, home from college for spring break, hauled another batch of fresh sap from the collection tanks, pouring it carefully into the evaporator's feed pan to maintain the continuous boiling process. "Tanks are getting low again," he said.

Guillermina checked the current batch's sugar content, nodding at the reading that indicated they were approaching the critical point where syrup needed constant attention to prevent scorching. "Almost there," she said. "Keep that sap flowing steady."

Torvald continued feeding fresh sap into the system, watching steam rise in thick clouds through the sugarhouse's ventilation as the sweet, distinctive smell of boiling maple filled the small building. "This batch smells incredible," he observed.

Guillermina drew off the finished syrup at exactly the right density, filtering it carefully before bottling this batch of the season's liquid gold. "Perfect batch," she said. "Every year I forget how much work goes into something so simple."
