Grease coated Ferdinand's hands as he worked beneath the studio's oldest pottery wheel, tracing the source of a persistent wobble that had frustrated the ceramics department for the entire semester. This particular wheel had served three generations of students.

His workshop assistant, Cassandra, held a flashlight steady over the wheel's motor housing, occasionally passing tools as Ferdinand worked to diagnose the mechanical issue methodically. "Any luck finding the problem?" she asked.

Ferdinand traced the wobble to a worn bearing, finding the source of the vibration that had been affecting every student's centering attempts for weeks now. "Found it," he said. "Bearing's worn out completely. We'll need a replacement part."

Cassandra noted the part number from the wheel's manufacturer plate, already planning to order the replacement before the next semester's ceramics classes would need reliable equipment. "I'll order this today," she confirmed.

Ferdinand finished his inspection and began reassembling the accessible components, satisfied that they'd identified the exact fix needed before students returned for their next scheduled class. "Should have her spinning true again soon," he said.
