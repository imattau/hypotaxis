Cool earthy air greeted Rosalind as she descended into the root cellar, lantern light catching the rows of wax-coated wheels resting on wooden shelves that had aged cheese for three family generations. Winter was the best time for checking the older batches.

Her son, Percival, home from culinary school for the holidays, followed her down with a notebook, eager to document the traditional techniques she'd never fully written down. "How do you know when this one's ready?" he asked, pointing to a particularly dusty wheel.

Rosalind lifted the wheel carefully and tapped it, listening to the resulting sound with the practiced ear of someone who'd been doing this since childhood. "Listen to that resonance," she said. "Hollow and clear, that's an eighteen-month wheel telling you it's ready."

Percival tried tapping the next wheel over, straining to hear the difference she'd described, though the distinction remained elusive to his untrained ear. "I genuinely can't tell the difference," he admitted, slightly frustrated.

Rosalind laughed and handed him the wheel to try himself, patient with the learning process that had taken her years to fully internalize. "You will," she said. "Give it another few years of listening, and you'll wonder how you ever missed it."
