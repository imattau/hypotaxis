Wind roared past the open door as Thessaly knelt at the edge of the plane, altimeter strapped to her wrist and heart hammering despite the dozens of jumps already behind her. Below, the patchwork fields stretched out impossibly small under the afternoon sun.

Her tandem student, Reginald, gripped the harness straps tight behind her, his breathing audible even over the engine noise as the jumpmaster gave the final signal. "Remember what we practiced," Thessaly called back over her shoulder. "Arch hard the second we clear the door."

Reginald nodded rapidly, and together they tipped forward out of the plane into the rushing wind, the ground tumbling into view before Thessaly stabilized them into a smooth, controlled fall. His scream of terror shifted quickly into something closer to exhilaration as the freefall steadied.

Thessaly checked her altimeter and gave Reginald's shoulder a reassuring pat, both of them stable now in the rushing air, arms spread wide against the wind. At the planned altitude, she reached back and pulled the ripcord, the canopy deploying with a sharp jolt that yanked them upright.

"You did it," Thessaly said, laughing as the canopy settled into a gentle glide above the patchwork fields now growing larger below them. Reginald whooped loudly, gripping the straps with white knuckles and a grin that hadn't faded since the moment they'd left the plane.
