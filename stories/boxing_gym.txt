The gym smelled of sweat and old leather as Coach Ibrahim wrapped tape around Destiny's knuckles, his weathered hands moving with practiced efficiency. Fluorescent lights buzzed overhead, and somewhere behind them a heavy bag creaked rhythmically under another boxer's punches.

"Championship's in three weeks," Ibrahim said, not looking up from his work. "You still dropping your right hand every time you throw a jab." Destiny rolled her shoulders, wincing slightly as he pulled the tape tighter around her wrist.

"I know, I know," she muttered, flexing her fingers once he finished. She stepped toward the mirror lining the far wall, throwing a few practice jabs and studying her own form with a critical eye. Sure enough, her right hand drifted down each time, exactly as he'd said.

Ibrahim tossed her a pair of gloves and moved to hold the focus mitts up in front of himself, planting his feet firmly. "Again," he said. "Jab, jab, cross. Keep that hand up this time." Destiny snapped the gloves on and squared up, sweat already beading along her hairline from the earlier warmup.

She threw the combination slowly at first, then faster as Ibrahim called out corrections, his mitts snapping with each solid connection. By the tenth repetition, her right hand had started staying up on instinct, and Ibrahim allowed himself a small, satisfied nod behind the mitts. "Better," he said. "Now do it a hundred more times until you can't get it wrong."
