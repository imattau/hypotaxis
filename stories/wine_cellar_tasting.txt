Candlelight flickered against the stone walls as Athanasia drew a sample from the aging barrel, holding the glass up to check the wine's color before offering it to her cellar master for his assessment. This vintage had been resting for eighteen months, and the final blending decisions couldn't wait much longer.

Her cellar master, Bertrand, swirled the sample thoughtfully, inhaling deeply before taking a small taste and rolling it across his palate with practiced consideration. "Tannins are softening nicely," he said. "Another three months might round it out completely."

Athanasia nodded and made a note in her leather-bound journal, tracking the barrel's progress alongside dozens of other entries from this vintage's various lots. "Let's compare it against barrel twelve," she said. "I want to see how the different oak is affecting the development."

Bertrand drew a sample from the neighboring barrel and tasted it alongside the first, considering the subtle differences the two oak sources had imparted to otherwise identical juice. "More vanilla in this one," he said. "The French oak's coming through stronger than I expected."

Athanasia tasted both samples herself, weighing the characteristics against her vision for the final blend before recording her own impressions in the journal. "I think we blend these two," she said. "Sixty-forty, favoring the softer tannins. Let's trial that ratio tomorrow."
