Murky water lapped against Cassandra's kayak as she paddled toward another cluster of floating debris, netting bag already half-full of bottles and plastic scraps pulled from the river's surface. The annual cleanup event had drawn a decent crowd this year, kayaks scattered across the slow-moving water.

Her paddling partner, Octavius, maneuvered his own kayak toward a tangle of fishing line caught on a submerged branch, working carefully to free it without capsizing. "This line's really wrapped in there," he called over. "Might need the multi-tool to cut it loose."

Cassandra paddled closer and passed her multi-tool across the gap between their boats, steadying his kayak with one hand while he worked the tangled line free. "Careful of the hook still attached to that," she warned. "Don't want you snagging a finger out here."

Octavius worked the line loose slowly, finally freeing the tangled mess and stuffing it into his own collection bag before returning the multi-tool. "Got it," he said. "That's probably been sitting there since last season judging by how brittle the line was."

Cassandra checked her own bag's fullness and glanced toward the shore where the collection station waited, already calculating how many more passes they had before needing to unload. "One more sweep down this bank," she said, "and then let's call it a solid day's work."
