Water dripped steadily onto the whetstone as Barnabus drew the chef's knife across its surface in smooth, practiced strokes, maintaining the precise angle that produced his signature razor edge. The farmers market stall had built a loyal following of local chefs.

His apprentice, Philomena, tested a finished blade's sharpness against a sheet of paper, watching it slice cleanly through without any tearing or resistance along the cut. "This one's ready," she said, setting it carefully into the finished pile.

Barnabus finished his current blade and checked its edge under the bright work light, satisfied with the consistent bevel he'd achieved across the knife's full length. "Nice recovery on that chip," he said. "Whoever owns this abused it pretty badly before bringing it in."

Philomena picked up the next knife in the queue, a worn paring knife with an edge so dull it barely qualified as sharp anymore, and began the restoration process under Barnabus's occasional guidance. "This one needs serious work," she observed, examining the damaged edge closely.

Barnabus glanced over and nodded, recognizing the extensive repair the blade would require before returning to his own steady rhythm on the whetstone. "Start with the coarse stone," he said. "That edge needs to be completely reprofiled before we can even think about polishing."
