Mist clung low to the orchard rows as Cassiopeia guided the mechanical shaker toward the cider apple trees, watching the small, tart fruit tumble down onto the collection tarp spread beneath the branches. These particular apples were far too bitter for eating, perfect for pressing.

Her farmhand, Torvald, gathered the fallen apples into collection bins, sorting out any that showed signs of rot before they could spoil the rest of the batch destined for the cider press. "This variety's dropping earlier than usual," he said, examining a handful.

Cassiopeia adjusted the shaker's position for the next tree, checking the branches for any fruit that hadn't fallen from the initial vibration before moving to the collection process. "Good year for tannins," she said. "These bitter ones make the best cider, oddly enough."

Torvald finished sorting the current bin and hauled it toward the waiting truck, already calculating how many more trees they'd need to harvest before meeting this batch's target volume. "Six more trees should do it," he said, checking his estimate against the truck's capacity.

Cassiopeia nodded and moved the shaker to the next tree in line, satisfied with the harvest's pace despite the morning's persistent mist. "Six more," she agreed. "Then these bitter beauties get to become something much more interesting."
