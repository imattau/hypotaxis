Fine graver lines cut precisely into the silver plate's surface as Cordwainer worked the monogram's elaborate scrollwork, transferring the client's design through hundreds of careful, individual engraving strokes. Hand engraving demanded steady precision.

His apprentice, Persimmon, prepared the next plate with transfer paper, tracing the design pattern that would guide Cordwainer's graver once the current monogram reached completion. "This transfer looks nice and crisp," she said.

Cordwainer examined the transferred design, checking its clarity before setting the plate aside for his next engraving session once the current piece was finished. "Perfect transfer," he said. "That'll make the next one go smoothly."

Persimmon watched him continue the scrollwork, absorbing the subtle graver angle and pressure that produced such consistently elegant, flowing lines across the silver's reflective surface. "How do you keep the lines so smooth?" she asked.

Cordwainer continued his careful engraving, considering the question that combined decades of practice with an almost intuitive feel for how the graver interacted with different metal surfaces. "Steady pressure," he said. "And never rushing the curves."
