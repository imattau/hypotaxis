Goatskin stretched taut across the wooden shell as Ambrosine worked the lacing pattern that would hold the drumhead in tension, checking the pitch periodically by tapping the developing surface. Traditional djembe construction demanded weeks of careful work.

Her apprentice, Torvald, prepared the next section of rope, measuring and cutting it to the precise length needed for the complex diamond lacing pattern still requiring completion around the drum's circumference. "This section's measured and ready," he said.

Ambrosine took the prepared rope and continued the lacing pattern, working methodically around the drum while checking tension evenness at each connection point along the way. "Looking good," she said. "Tension's staying consistent."

Torvald watched her technique closely, absorbing the subtle hand movements that kept the complex pattern both structurally sound and visually striking across the drum's curved surface. "How many more sections?" he asked.

Ambrosine counted the remaining unlaced sections, calculating the work still ahead before this particular drum would be ready for its final tuning and finishing touches. "About a third left," she said. "This one's going to sound incredible."
