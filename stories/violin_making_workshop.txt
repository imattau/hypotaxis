Wood shavings curled from Bartholomew's carving knife as he shaped the violin's scroll, working the maple with the same patient precision he'd applied to hundreds of instruments over four decades of lutherie. Each scroll carried his personal signature, subtly different from every other maker's work.

His apprentice, Seraphina, worked at the neighboring bench, carving her own first scroll under his careful supervision, comparing her progress against his example whenever uncertainty crept into her movements. "My spirals never look as tight as yours," she said, examining her own attempt critically.

Bartholomew glanced over at her work, noting the slight inconsistency in her spiral's curve without judgment, just careful observation. "Your third attempt already beats my third attempt," he said. "Don't compare yourself to forty years of practice."

Seraphina returned to her carving with renewed focus, applying his feedback about blade angle to the next section of her developing spiral. "This feels more controlled," she said, checking the curve's consistency against his example once more.

Bartholomew nodded approvingly and returned to his own scroll, the workshop falling into comfortable, focused silence broken only by the soft scrape of two knives shaping maple. "That's the improvement," he said. "Keep that feeling in your hands."
