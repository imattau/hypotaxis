Tiny brass pins gleamed under the desk lamp as Cuthbert positioned another plank along the hull's curve, applying a thin bead of glue before pressing it carefully into place. The three-masted model had consumed most of his evenings for the past four months, plank by painstaking plank.

His granddaughter, Wilhelmina, watched from across the workbench, carefully threading rigging line through a tiny pulley block under his patient instruction. "This one keeps slipping out," she said, frowning at the stubborn thread. "How do you keep your hands so steady for this?"

Cuthbert set down his plank and reached over to help guide her fingers, showing her the trick of holding tension with her pinky while threading with her other fingers. "Lots of practice, and losing plenty of pieces to the floor," he said. "You should see how many pulleys I've lost under this desk over the years."

Wilhelmina tried again with his guidance, and this time the thread caught cleanly through the tiny block, pulling taut just as it should. "I did it," she said, grinning up at him with obvious pride at the small victory.

Cuthbert smiled and returned to his own work, watching from the corner of his eye as she moved on to the next pulley with newfound confidence. "Keep going like that," he said, "and you'll be doing the ratlines by yourself before this ship is finished."
