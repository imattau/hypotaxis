Unglazed clay took shape beneath Michiko's hands as she formed another shallow bonsai pot, working toward the precise proportions that would properly complement the specific tree style this particular vessel was intended to display. Bonsai pottery demanded restrained aesthetics.

Her apprentice, Torvald, prepared the drainage holes, carefully cutting them into the pot's base at the positions that would ensure proper water flow without compromising the vessel's structural integrity. "Drainage holes are cut and positioned," he said.

Michiko examined his hole placement, checking the spacing against traditional bonsai pottery standards before approving the pot for its final shaping and eventual firing process. "Perfect placement," she said. "Function matters as much as form here."

Torvald watched her continue refining the pot's rim, absorbing the subtle restraint that distinguished quality bonsai pottery from more decorative ceramic work meant to draw attention to itself. "Why keep everything so understated?" he asked.

Michiko continued her careful shaping, explaining the philosophy that the pot should support and complement the tree rather than compete with it for visual attention. "The tree is the art," she said. "The pot just quietly serves it."
