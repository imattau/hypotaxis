Dust motes drifted through the beam of Cornelius's flashlight as he crawled beneath the old star projector, tracing a bundle of wiring toward the source of the intermittent short that had plagued the machine for weeks. The museum's evening show started in two hours, and half the winter constellations still flickered unpredictably.

His colleague, Petronella, held a second flashlight steady from above, occasionally passing down tools as Cornelius called for them from beneath the massive machine. "Any luck?" she asked, crouching lower to see past the projector's housing. "The Andromeda cluster's still cutting out every few minutes."

Cornelius grunted and traced the wire further, finding a spot where the insulation had cracked and worn thin against a metal support bracket. "Found it," he said, voice muffled from beneath the machine. "Years of vibration wore right through the sheathing. Hand me the electrical tape and a wire nut."

Petronella passed the tools down carefully, watching the clock mounted on the far wall with growing awareness of the time slipping away before doors opened. Cornelius worked quickly but carefully, wrapping the exposed section and securing the connection with steady, practiced hands.

He slid out from beneath the projector just as Petronella finished testing the switch, both of them watching the star field steady into a clean, unwavering projection across the dome overhead. "Just in time," Petronella said, glancing at the clock. "Doors open in eleven minutes."
