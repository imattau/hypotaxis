Full-grain leather strip lay stretched across Cordelia's workbench as she beveled the edges with a specialized tool, working toward the smooth, rounded profile that distinguished handmade belts from mass-produced alternatives. Custom belts demanded careful finishing.

Her apprentice, Osgood, prepared the buckle hardware, polishing the solid brass to a bright shine before its eventual attachment to Cordelia's carefully finished leather strip. "Buckle's polished and ready," he said.

Cordelia examined the beveled edge closely, satisfied with the smooth, consistent profile before moving to burnish the edge with wax for a professional, finished appearance. "This edge is coming along beautifully," she said.

Osgood watched her burnishing technique, absorbing the friction and heat control that transformed a rough-cut edge into something glossy and refined without any visible tool marks. "How many passes does burnishing take?" he asked.

Cordelia continued her steady burnishing motion, considering the question that depended on leather thickness and the specific shine level this particular commission required. "Depends on the leather," she said. "This one wants about six passes."
