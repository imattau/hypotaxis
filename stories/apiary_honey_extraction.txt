Golden honey spun outward against the extractor's steel walls as Bertrand cranked the handle steadily, watching through the small viewing window as centrifugal force pulled the sweet liquid from the uncapped frames. Harvest day always filled the whole shed with honey's rich smell.

His daughter, Cordelia, home from college for the summer, uncapped the next batch of frames with a heated knife, slicing away the wax seal to expose the honey cells beneath. "This frame's incredibly heavy," she said. "Must be a really productive hive this year."

Bertrand paused his cranking to check the extractor's collection basin, satisfied with how much honey had already pooled at the bottom from just this first batch of frames. "Third hive's always our best producer," he said. "Something about that spot near the creek."

Cordelia finished uncapping her frames and loaded them into the extractor's empty slots, ready for the next round of spinning once Bertrand finished draining the current batch. "Should we filter this batch before bottling?" she asked, glancing at the waiting equipment.

Bertrand nodded and began draining the honey through the fine mesh filter, watching the golden liquid flow steadily into the waiting bucket below. "Always filter," he said. "Nobody wants wax flakes in their morning toast honey."
