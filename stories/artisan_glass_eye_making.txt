Colored glass rods softened under Anouk's torch flame as she worked the delicate prosthetic eye's iris pattern, layering precise streaks of color that would match her client's remaining natural eye exactly. Ocular prosthetics demanded extraordinary artistic precision.

Her apprentice, Radomir, prepared reference photographs of the client's healthy eye, organizing them by lighting condition so Anouk could cross-reference color accuracy throughout the demanding matching process. "Photos are organized by angle," he said.

Anouk compared her developing iris against the reference images, adjusting the color layering slightly to better match the subtle variations visible in the natural eye's authentic pigmentation pattern. "Getting closer," she said. "The outer ring needs more amber."

Radomir handed her the amber-toned glass rod she'd requested, watching the delicate torch work that transformed simple colored glass into something approaching genuine biological complexity. "The detail is honestly incredible," he observed.

Anouk continued her careful work, considering how many hours remained before this particular prosthetic would finally match closely enough to satisfy both her exacting standards and her client's understandable hopes. "Almost there," she said. "This has to be right."
