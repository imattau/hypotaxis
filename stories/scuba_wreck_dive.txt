Bubbles trailed upward past Bronwyn's mask as she descended along the anchor line, the wreck's rusted hull emerging slowly out of the hazy blue below. Twenty years underwater had softened the old cargo ship's silhouette, coral now claiming what had once been steel and rivets.

Her dive buddy, Anwar, signaled from a few feet away, checking his gauge before pointing toward the ship's broken bow section where a school of silver fish scattered at their approach. Bronwyn nodded through her regulator and followed, careful to keep her fins clear of the fragile coral growth along the hull.

They drifted along the deck's edge together, flashlight beams cutting through the gloom of the ship's open cargo holds, revealing shapes too dark to identify from the surface briefing. Anwar paused near an old ship's wheel, still miraculously intact, and gestured for Bronwyn to come take a closer look.

Bronwyn hovered beside him, adjusting her buoyancy carefully as she studied the corroded wheel now home to a small cluster of anemones swaying gently in the current. She checked her air gauge, tapping Anwar's shoulder and holding up ten fingers to signal their remaining bottom time before the ascent.

Anwar nodded and took one last photo of the wheel before they began working their way back toward the anchor line, ascending slowly to let their bodies adjust to the changing pressure. By the time they broke the surface, both were grinning behind their masks, already trading excited gestures about what they'd seen below.
