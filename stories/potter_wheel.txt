Clay spun beneath Odalys's steady hands as she coaxed a shapeless lump upward into the beginnings of a tall vase, water dripping from her fingertips onto the spinning wheel. The studio smelled of wet earth and the faint tang of the kiln firing in the next room.

Her student, Preston, watched from the neighboring wheel, his own attempt collapsing sideways for the third time that afternoon. "I don't understand how you make it look so easy," he said, scraping the ruined clay back into a ball with obvious frustration.

"It isn't easy," Odalys said without looking up, her thumbs pressing a slow, even groove into the vase's rising wall. "It just doesn't look hard once you've done it four thousand times." She glanced over and slowed her wheel to demonstrate the centering motion again, guiding his hands gently onto the clay.

Preston tried again, breathing slowly the way she'd shown him, and this time the clay stayed centered under his palms as it climbed upward in a wobbly but recognizable cylinder. "There," Odalys said, nodding. "Now stop thinking about your hands and just feel where the clay wants to go."

By the end of the session, Preston's cylinder had become something almost resembling a mug, lopsided but standing on its own. Odalys inspected it with a critical but approving eye before setting it carefully on the shelf beside her own far more polished vase to dry.
