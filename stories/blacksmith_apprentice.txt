Coals glowed white-hot in the forge as Ottokar pumped the bellows steadily, watching the steel bar deep in the fire shift from orange toward the color he needed for the next fold. Sweat ran freely down his back despite the open doors letting in the autumn air.

His apprentice, Seraphina, stood at the anvil with tongs ready, having spent the morning learning to read the metal's color the way Ottokar had drilled into her for weeks. "That looks close," she said. "Is it ready to pull?"

Ottokar squinted at the glowing steel and shook his head slightly. "Not quite, give it another thirty seconds," he said. "Pull it too cool and the fold won't weld properly, you'll just be hammering two pieces that never truly joined."

Seraphina nodded and watched the fire carefully, resisting the urge to reach for the tongs before he gave the signal. When Ottokar finally nodded, she pulled the bar free in one smooth motion and set it on the anvil for him to strike.

Ottokar's hammer rang out in steady, measured blows, folding the glowing steel back on itself with practiced precision as sparks scattered across the shop floor. "This is the part that actually makes the steel strong," he said between strikes. "Everything before this was just getting it ready."
