Sparks arced off the workbench as Zofia guided the torch along the seam, her welding mask tinting the world a deep amber as the metal glowed and fused beneath the flame. The shop smelled of hot steel and cutting oil, familiar after fifteen years behind this same bench.

Her apprentice, Rashid, watched from a safe distance, sketchbook open on his knee as he tried to capture the angle of her wrist through each pass. "You barely even look at the gauge anymore," he said once she flipped her mask up. "How do you know the heat's right just by the color?"

Zofia set the torch down and wiped her hands on a rag hanging from her belt. "You'll know it too, eventually," she said. "Right now it looks like magic because you're still translating it in your head instead of just seeing it." She nodded toward his own practice piece clamped in the vise nearby.

Rashid set the sketchbook aside and pulled his mask down, positioning the torch the way she'd shown him a dozen times already that week. His bead came out uneven, wavering along the seam, but noticeably straighter than his attempt from the day before.

Zofia leaned over his shoulder once he'd finished, inspecting the weld with a critical eye before nodding slowly. "Better," she said. "Grind it down and we'll test the joint's strength. That's how you'll really know if it held."
