Antenna gleamed under stage lights as Cordelia positioned herself before the theremin, hands hovering in the air to test the instrument's pitch and volume fields before tonight's unusual concert began. Few audiences had witnessed live theremin performance.

Her sound technician, Balthazar, adjusted the amplification levels from the mixing booth, calling out test tones as Cordelia moved her hands through the invisible fields that controlled the instrument's eerie, wavering sound. "Levels are balanced," he said.

Cordelia moved her right hand closer to the pitch antenna, the note rising in response before she pulled back to demonstrate the instrument's remarkable sensitivity to even small movements. "Sounds clean tonight," she said. "No interference from the lighting rig."

Balthazar made a final adjustment based on her feedback, satisfied that the theremin's notoriously finicky sound would translate properly through tonight's sound system for the curious audience. "You're all set," he confirmed. "Whenever you're ready to begin."

Cordelia took a steadying breath and positioned her hands for the opening piece, the theremin's otherworldly voice about to fill the concert hall for an audience mostly unfamiliar with this strange, hands-free instrument. "Let's give them something memorable," she said.
