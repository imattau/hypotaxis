Wet fur glistened as Marguerite carefully lifted the orphaned otter pup from its enclosure, checking the small animal's healing injury before the morning's scheduled swim therapy session. Wildlife rehabilitation demanded patience most people never fully appreciated.

Her fellow rehabilitator, Fitzgerald, prepared the therapy pool's temperature, checking it twice against the specific requirements for otter pups still building their waterproofing and swimming stamina. "Pool's at the right temperature," he said. "Ready whenever you bring her over."

Marguerite carried the pup toward the pool, murmuring soft reassurances despite the animal's obvious wariness after weeks in captivity following her mother's unfortunate accident. "Easy, sweetheart," she said. "Just a little swim today, nothing scary."

Fitzgerald stood ready at the pool's edge, prepared to assist if the pup showed any signs of struggling during this crucial stage of her rehabilitation and eventual release preparation. "She's looking stronger every session," he observed, watching Marguerite ease the pup into the water.

Marguerite guided the pup through a gentle swimming circuit, relieved to see her navigating the water with increasing confidence compared to just a week ago. "Almost ready for release," she said. "A few more weeks of this and she'll be back where she belongs."
