Vibrant powder mounds rose in neat cones across Farrukh's stall, cumin and turmeric and paprika arranged by color as customers wove through the market's narrow aisle sampling scents from open jars. Saturday mornings brought the week's biggest crowds.

His daughter, Zuleikha, helped a hesitant customer choose between two similar chili blends, describing the heat level and flavor profile with the practiced knowledge of someone who'd grown up around these spices. "This one's smokier," she said. "The other has more immediate heat."

Farrukh weighed out a customer's order of saffron threads with careful precision, the expensive spice requiring exact measurement given its considerable cost per gram. "Fifteen grams exactly," he said, sealing the small packet before handing it over.

Zuleikha finished helping her customer decide and rang up the purchase, already turning to greet the next person waiting patiently at the counter's edge. "The cardamom's especially fresh this week," she told them. "Just arrived yesterday from our usual supplier."

Farrukh watched his daughter work the counter with practiced ease, satisfied with how naturally she'd taken to the family business after years of helping on weekends. "Busy Saturday," he said. "Good thing you're here, I couldn't manage this alone."
