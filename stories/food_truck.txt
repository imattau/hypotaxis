Steam hissed from the griddle as Marisol flipped a row of sizzling plantains, sweat beading at her hairline despite the small fan clipped to the food truck's window. The lunch rush had hit early, and the line outside already stretched past the parking lot's painted curb.

Her cousin, Emiliano, worked the register with practiced speed, calling out orders over his shoulder without breaking rhythm. "Two more empanadas, one no cheese," he shouted, sliding the next customer's card back through the window. "We're almost out of rice, heads up."

Marisol swore under her breath and pulled a fresh bag of rice from beneath the counter, ripping it open one-handed while keeping the other on the spatula. "Start the backup batch," she said. "If we run out before two o'clock I'm never hearing the end of it from the regulars."

Emiliano laughed and ducked past her to grab the rice cooker, moving with the easy familiarity of two people who'd worked this cramped space together for years. Outside, the line shuffled forward as the aroma of charred plantain and roasting meat drifted across the lot, drawing even more people toward the truck's window.

By the time the rush finally slowed past one o'clock, both of them were leaning against the counter catching their breath, the griddle finally quiet for the first time in hours. "Best Tuesday we've had all month," Emiliano said, counting the register with a satisfied grin.
