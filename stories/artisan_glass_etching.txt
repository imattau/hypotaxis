Fine sand hissed through the blasting cabinet as Cassandra guided the nozzle across the stenciled wine glass, carving the delicate floral pattern into the surface through controlled abrasive etching. The wedding commission needed twelve matching glasses.

Her apprentice, Fitzwilliam, prepared the next glass with a fresh stencil, carefully positioning the adhesive pattern to match the exact placement Cassandra had established for consistency across the entire set. "This one's stenciled and ready," he said.

Cassandra finished her current glass and examined the etched pattern under bright light, checking the depth and clarity before removing the stencil to reveal the finished design. "Beautiful depth on this one," she said, holding it up.

Fitzwilliam passed her the next prepared glass, watching her technique closely to understand the subtle nozzle control that produced such consistent, professional-quality etching across each piece. "How do you keep the pressure so even?" he asked.

Cassandra smiled and began etching the new glass, the question familiar from her own early apprenticeship years learning this exact demanding technique. "Steady hands and about five hundred practice glasses," she said. "You're getting there."
