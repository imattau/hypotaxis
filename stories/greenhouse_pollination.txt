Warm humid air clung to Rosalind's skin as she moved between rows of tomato plants, small electric pollinator buzzing softly as she touched each cluster of yellow flowers in turn. The greenhouse's automated systems handled watering, but pollination still needed a human touch this early in the season.

Her research intern, Alaric, followed a few rows behind with a clipboard, recording which plants had been pollinated and noting any showing signs of stress or disease. "Row twelve's got some yellowing on the lower leaves," he said. "Might want to check the nutrient mix for that section."

Rosalind finished her current row and moved to inspect the leaves Alaric had flagged, examining the discoloration pattern with a practiced, critical eye. "Nitrogen deficiency, probably," she said. "Adjust the feed ratio for that section and we'll monitor if it clears up."

Alaric made a note on his clipboard and continued down the row behind her, occasionally reaching out to steady a heavy branch weighed down with developing fruit. "These early plants are looking strong otherwise," he said. "Should have a good yield if the pollination keeps pace."

Rosalind moved to the next row and resumed her methodical work with the pollinator, satisfied with the season's early progress despite the isolated nutrient issue. "Let's finish this section before lunch," she said. "Then we can tackle the nitrogen adjustment together."
