Apple pomace piled high near the press as Wendell fed another bucket of freshly ground fruit into the cloth-lined rack, building the layered cheese that would soon yield the season's first pressing. The orchard's oldest trees had produced an exceptional harvest this year.

His daughter, Marguerite, home from her first semester at college, worked the hydraulic press's controls under his careful instruction, watching the pressure gauge climb as juice began streaming into the collection trough. "Pressure's building steady," she said. "Should we go faster or keep this pace?"

Wendell checked the juice flow and shook his head slightly at her suggestion, preferring the slower, more thorough extraction his family had always favored. "Keep it slow," he said. "Rush the press and you leave juice in the pomace. Patience gets you every last drop."

Marguerite adjusted the pressure incrementally as instructed, watching the golden juice stream steadily into the collection vessel below the press. "Smells incredible already," she said, breathing in the sharp, sweet scent filling the small pressing barn.

Wendell smiled and dipped a cup into the fresh juice, offering the first taste to his daughter before sampling it himself, checking the sugar content against his experienced palate. "That's this year's harvest right there," he said. "Best batch we've had in a while, I think."
