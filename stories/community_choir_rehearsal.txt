Sheet music rustled as Marisol raised her hands to signal the choir's attention, the community center's rehearsal hall filled with the mixed voices of forty amateur singers preparing for next month's spring concert. Wednesday evenings brought together an eclectic group.

Her accompanist, Bartholomew, positioned his hands over the piano keys, watching Marisol for the downbeat that would begin their run-through of the concert's most challenging piece. "Ready whenever you are," he said quietly.

Marisol raised her hands higher and gave the preparatory beat, and Bartholomew's piano introduction filled the hall as the choir members found their opening pitches from memory. The sopranos entered first, followed by the other sections in careful sequence.

Marisol listened critically as the piece progressed, noting a few pitch issues in the alto section that would need addressing before they moved past this particular passage. She raised a hand to stop the choir mid-phrase, gesturing for the altos specifically.

"Altos, that entrance is flat," she said. "Let's isolate just that section and try it again." Bartholomew replayed the relevant measures on piano, and the altos worked through the passage until their pitch matched what Marisol needed for the full ensemble sound.
