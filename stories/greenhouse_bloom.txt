Humid air clung to Dr. Whitfield's skin as she moved between the rows of the botanical greenhouse, misting the leaves of a rare orchid with practiced, gentle sweeps of her spray bottle. Condensation beaded on the glass panes overhead, blurring the grey sky beyond.

Her assistant, a college intern named Rashid, hurried in from the adjoining lab, clipboard clutched against his chest. "The temperature sensors in section three are reading high again," he said, slightly out of breath. Whitfield set down her spray bottle and frowned toward the far end of the greenhouse.

"That's the third time this week," she said, already walking briskly toward section three, Rashid falling into step beside her. "If we lose the cooling system now, we lose the whole flowering cycle we've been tracking for months."

They reached the panel together, and Whitfield knelt to examine the humming unit while Rashid held the clipboard steady, ready to note down anything she found. She traced a finger along a loose wire connection, and her frown deepened into something more serious.

"Get maintenance on the phone," she said. "Tell them section three, now, not whenever they get around to it." Rashid was already dialing before she finished speaking. Whitfield stayed crouched beside the unit, one hand resting protectively on the nearest plant stand, watching the temperature display tick upward one degree at a time while she waited for a response on the other end of the line.
