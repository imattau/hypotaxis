Coal smoke drifted across the competition forge as Wendell hammered the glowing horseshoe blank into shape, working against the clock during the state fair's annual farrier competition. Three judges watched closely from behind the safety barrier.

His competition partner, Xiomara, called out the remaining time from their designated corner, tracking their progress against the other competing teams working at forges down the row. "Four minutes left," she said. "You're on pace for a solid finish."

Wendell adjusted his hammer strikes based on her timing update, working to complete the shoe's final shaping before the buzzer would signal the round's end regardless of completion status. "Almost there," he said, focused entirely on the glowing metal.

Xiomara watched the judges moving down the row of competitors, evaluating finished pieces with careful scrutiny that would determine this year's championship placement. "They're two stations away," she said. "Better hurry if you want it perfect."

Wendell finished his final strike just as the buzzer sounded, quenching the completed horseshoe with a satisfied exhale despite the competition's demanding time pressure. "Done," he said. "Now we just wait and see how the judges feel about it."
