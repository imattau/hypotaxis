Poplar wood chips flew from Henrietta's traditional hollowing tool as she carved another clog's interior, working the soft wood into the precise foot-shaped cavity that generations of her family had produced from this exact small workshop. Handmade clogs demanded specific technique.

Her apprentice, Fabrizia, sanded the completed clog's exterior, smoothing the wood toward the comfortable, rounded profile that made handmade clogs surprisingly pleasant for extended wear despite their rigid construction. "This exterior feels really smooth," she said.

Henrietta examined the sanded clog, checking its shape against her practiced eye before approving it for the final painting and finishing process that would complete this particular pair. "Beautiful shape," she said. "You're really getting the hang of this."

Fabrizia watched Henrietta continue hollowing the interior of the matching clog, absorbing the subtle hand movements that removed exactly the right amount of wood without weakening the structure. "How do you know when to stop hollowing?" she asked.

Henrietta continued her careful work, considering the question that balanced comfort against structural integrity in ways that took years to fully internalize. "You'll feel it," she said. "The wood tells you when it's ready."
