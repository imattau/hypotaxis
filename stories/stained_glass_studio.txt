Lead came bent easily under Hyacinth's fingers as she fitted another piece of cobalt glass into the window's growing pattern, the workshop's north-facing light ideal for judging color accuracy. The church commission needed six matching panels finished before the spring dedication ceremony.

Her apprentice, Roscoe, scored and snapped the next piece of glass according to the pattern template, checking the cut's edge for any rough spots that might need grinding before it could fit properly. "This piece keeps snapping short," he said. "Third attempt on this exact shape."

Hyacinth set down her leading knife and examined his cutting technique, noting where he was applying pressure unevenly along the score line. "You're hesitating partway through the snap," she said. "Commit fully once you start, or the glass fractures unpredictably."

Roscoe scored a fresh piece and attempted the snap again, applying steady pressure the way she'd demonstrated, and this time the glass separated cleanly along the intended line. "That's the one," he said, holding up the properly shaped piece for her inspection.

Hyacinth nodded approvingly and returned to her own leading work, fitting the newly cut piece into its designated spot within the growing panel. "Perfect fit," she said. "Five more panels to go before we can even think about soldering the joints."
