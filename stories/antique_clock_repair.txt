Brass gears lay scattered across the felt-lined tray as Peregrine examined the grandfather clock's movement under his magnifying lamp, searching for whatever had caused it to stop running three days ago. The client's family had owned this clock for four generations.

His workshop assistant, Marisol, sorted the disassembled parts into labeled trays, cross-referencing each piece against a hand-drawn diagram she'd sketched before taking the movement apart. "The mainspring looks intact," she said. "Might just be built-up dirt in the escapement mechanism."

Peregrine leaned closer with his loupe, examining the escapement's tiny teeth for the grime Marisol suspected was causing the stoppage. "You're right," he said. "Decades of old oil turned to sludge in here. We'll need to clean everything before reassembly."

Marisol prepared a small ultrasonic cleaning bath, carefully placing the smaller components into the mesh basket while Peregrine continued examining the larger gears by hand. "Bath should take about twenty minutes," she said. "Then we can start the reassembly process."

Peregrine nodded and began wiping down the mainspring housing with a soft cloth, methodical in his approach despite the clock's obvious age and delicate condition. "Four generations," he said. "No pressure, but let's make sure this one keeps running for a few more."
