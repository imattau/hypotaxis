Glass bottles clinked steadily along the small bottling line as Fitzgerald monitored the filler's calibration, watching each bottle receive its precise measure of the week's finished batch. The brewery's tasting room would open in three hours, and this batch needed to be ready.

His bottling assistant, Persimmon, capped each filled bottle as it emerged from the line, checking the seal's tightness with a practiced twist before setting it into the waiting crate. "Filler's running consistent," she said. "No foam-outs on this batch so far."

Fitzgerald nodded and adjusted the line's speed slightly, finding the sweet spot between efficiency and the careful attention each bottle needed during the capping process. "Good," he said. "Last batch had three bottles under-filled before we caught the calibration drift."

Persimmon continued capping steadily, occasionally glancing at the crate's fill level to track their progress against the total batch size waiting to be bottled. "Halfway through," she said. "Should have this whole batch done before the tasting room opens."

Fitzgerald checked the remaining kegs still waiting for their turn on the line, mentally calculating the schedule for the rest of the afternoon's bottling work. "We'll make it," he said. "Then we finally get to taste if this recipe adjustment actually worked."
