Elena knelt in the small garden behind the house, pressing seeds into the dark soil one at a time. It had been her grandmother's garden once, overrun with weeds until she'd spent the last three weekends clearing it out. The morning sun was still low, casting long shadows from the fence.

Her brother Tomas leaned against the porch railing, watching her without saying anything. He had flown in for the funeral and stayed an extra week, though neither of them had talked about why.

"She always said the tomatoes needed more sun than I gave them," Elena said, not looking up. "I moved the whole bed over by the fence this year."

Tomas walked down the steps and crouched beside her, picking up a stray seed packet. "You remember she used to make us shell peas out here every summer? I hated it."

"You loved it," Elena said, smiling faintly. "You just complained so she'd let you eat half of them raw."

He laughed quietly, turning the packet over in his hands. For a moment neither of them spoke, the only sound the distant hum of a lawnmower a few yards over. Elena brushed the dirt from her palms and sat back on her heels, looking at the empty chair by the garden's edge where their grandmother used to sit each morning with her tea.

"I keep expecting her to tell me I'm planting them too close together," Elena said. Tomas set the seed packet down gently in the soil beside her and said nothing, resting a hand on her shoulder instead.
