Attorney Zara Chen straightened the papers on her table one last time as the bailiff called the courtroom to order. Her client, a nervous young man named Deshawn, sat rigid beside her, hands clasped so tightly his knuckles had gone pale.

Judge Whitfield entered and took her seat at the bench, glasses perched low on her nose as she flipped through a folder of notes. "Has the jury reached a verdict?" she asked, her voice carrying easily through the hushed room.

The jury foreman, an older man in a grey cardigan, rose slowly from his seat in the front row. "We have, Your Honor," he said, unfolding a single sheet of paper with deliberate care. Across the aisle, the prosecutor leaned back in her chair, arms folded, watching the foreman with an unreadable expression.

Zara reached under the table and gave Deshawn's forearm a brief, steadying squeeze. He glanced at her, eyes wide with fear, and she gave him the smallest nod she could manage. The foreman cleared his throat, and the entire gallery seemed to lean forward at once.

"On the charge of grand theft, we find the defendant not guilty." A ragged breath escaped Deshawn all at once, and somewhere behind them, his mother let out a small, choked sob of relief. Judge Whitfield banged her gavel once to settle the murmur rippling through the gallery, though a faint smile tugged at the corner of her mouth as she did.
