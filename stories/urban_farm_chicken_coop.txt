Morning light filtered through the urban farm's chicken wire as Xiomara collected eggs from the nesting boxes, counting today's haul against the flock's usual productive average. The rooftop coop had become a neighborhood curiosity since its installation.

Her farm volunteer, Percival, refilled the feed and water stations, checking each hen for any signs of illness while he worked through the coop's morning maintenance routine. "Everyone looks healthy today," he said. "That new hen's finally settling into the pecking order."

Xiomara finished collecting eggs and moved to check the coop's ventilation, adjusting a panel that helped regulate temperature during the city's increasingly warm spring days. "Sixteen eggs today," she said. "Best morning this week, the girls must be enjoying this weather."

Percival finished his feed check and began the coop's routine cleaning, scraping the roosting bars and refreshing the bedding material that kept the space sanitary for the flock. "Compost bin's getting full," he observed. "Should schedule another pickup soon."

Xiomara nodded and made a mental note about the compost schedule, satisfied with another productive morning tending the urban farm's small but thriving flock. "I'll call about pickup today," she said. "Thanks for handling the coop, this place wouldn't run without you."
