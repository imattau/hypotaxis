Steam rose from the plate as Executive Chef Anouk plated the tasting menu's fourth course, adjusting a single microgreen with tweezers before nodding to the waiting server. Word had reached the kitchen an hour ago that tonight's anonymous diner was likely the city's most feared restaurant critic.

Her sous chef, Percival, worked the station beside her, expediting tickets with tight, controlled efficiency despite the obvious tension running through the entire kitchen. "Table twelve's course just went out," he said. "No visible reaction yet, but they're taking their time with each bite."

Anouk wiped down her station and reached for the next set of plates, refusing to let the pressure show in her movements despite her racing heart. "Focus on the food, not the guessing," she said. "We cook the same whether they're a critic or not."

Percival nodded and called out the next ticket, the kitchen's rhythm settling back into its usual controlled chaos despite the added weight of tonight's stakes. "Course five, two covers, table twelve," he confirmed, already reaching for the ingredients.

Anouk began plating the next course with the same meticulous care she'd given every dish that night, refusing to let a possible review change how she approached the work in front of her. "Send it," she said, stepping back to let the server take the finished plates toward the dining room.
