Wind buffeted the platform as Ignatius squeegeed a wide arc across the fortieth floor's window, suds streaking downward as the city sprawled dizzyingly below the swaying rig. Twenty years on the job had dulled the vertigo, mostly.

His partner, Cordelia, worked the opposite corner of the same platform, radio clipped to her harness relaying updates from the ground crew managing the rig's controls. "Wind's gusting a bit up here," she said. "Ground team wants to know if we should pause for ten minutes."

Ignatius glanced at the building's flag several floors below, gauging the wind's strength by its movement before answering her question. "We're fine," he said. "Nothing like that storm two summers ago. Let's finish this row before lunch."

Cordelia nodded and resumed her squeegee work, the platform swaying gently but predictably in the current breeze as they moved methodically along the row of windows. "Client wants the whole east face done by Friday," she said. "That's optimistic given the weather forecast."

Ignatius finished his section and signaled for the platform to shift laterally toward the next set of windows, already planning the remaining schedule in his head. "We'll manage," he said. "We always do, one window at a time."
