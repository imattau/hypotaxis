Waxed linen thread pulled taut as Guinevere hand-stitched another wallet's edge, working the traditional saddle stitch technique that produced stronger, more durable seams than any sewing machine could replicate. Custom leather goods demanded patient craftsmanship.

Her apprentice, Balthazar, prepared the next wallet's pieces, punching the stitch holes at precisely even intervals along the leather's edge using a specialized pricking iron and mallet. "Holes are punched and evenly spaced," he said.

Guinevere examined his punching work, checking the spacing consistency before approving the pieces for the demanding hand-stitching process that would join them permanently together. "Perfectly even," she said. "That consistency really matters for the finished look."

Balthazar watched her continue stitching, absorbing the subtle two-needle technique that created such remarkably strong and visually appealing seams along the wallet's carefully punched edges. "How long does one wallet actually take?" he asked.

Guinevere continued her rhythmic stitching, calculating the total hours this particular design typically required from initial cutting through final edge finishing. "About six hours total," she said. "Worth every minute for something built to last decades."
