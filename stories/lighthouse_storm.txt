Waves crashed hard against the rocks below as Isolde climbed the lighthouse's spiral stairs, storm winds rattling the old glass panes with each violent gust. The automated light had failed an hour ago, and the fishing boats still out past the point needed it working before the storm worsened further.

Her radio crackled with updates from her assistant, Percy, stationed below at the generator room, working to diagnose the electrical fault that had knocked out the primary system. "Backup circuit's fried too," his voice crackled through the static. "You'll need to run the lamp manually until I can get it fixed."

Isolde reached the lamp room, hands already moving to the manual crank mechanism she hadn't touched in years, muscle memory returning despite the disuse. She began turning it steadily, feeling the mechanism resist before slowly catching the familiar old rhythm.

"Light's turning," she said into her radio, breathing hard from the effort of maintaining the crank's pace. "How long until you can get the backup running?" Percy's reply crackled back, thin and strained. "Give me twenty minutes, maybe less if this splice holds."

Isolde kept cranking steadily, arms burning, watching through the rain-lashed glass as the beam swept its familiar arc across the churning black water. Somewhere out there, boats were counting on that light, and she wasn't about to let her arms give out before Percy finished his work.
