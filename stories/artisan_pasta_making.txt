Flour dust hung in the kitchen air as Fabrizia kneaded the pasta dough with practiced strength, working it toward the smooth, elastic consistency that decades of Sunday pasta-making had taught her to recognize by touch alone. Her grandchildren would arrive within the hour.

Her granddaughter, Wilhelmina, home from her first semester away, watched the kneading technique with renewed appreciation now that she understood how much skill the seemingly simple process actually required. "I never noticed how long you knead it," she said. "Ten minutes at least."

Fabrizia continued her rhythmic kneading, occasionally folding the dough back on itself with the same motion she'd learned from her own grandmother decades before. "Twelve minutes exactly," she said. "Rush it and the pasta won't hold its shape properly when cooked."

Wilhelmina reached out to test the dough's texture herself, feeling how it had transformed from a shaggy mass into something smooth and slightly springy beneath her fingers. "It feels different now," she said, genuinely surprised by the transformation.

Fabrizia smiled and finally set the dough aside to rest, covering it with a clean cloth before beginning to clean the flour-covered counter around them. "Now we wait thirty minutes," she said. "Then I'll teach you to roll it properly, just like my grandmother taught me."
