Sable hair bundled precisely in Marguerite's fingers as she shaped the next brush's tip, working toward the exact taper that would give this particular brush its signature fine point for detailed watercolor work. Handmade brushes demanded patient precision.

Her apprentice, Cyrus, prepared the ferrules that would secure each bundle of hair to its wooden handle, sorting them by size according to the batch's specific brush order requirements. "Ferrules are sorted and ready," he said.

Marguerite examined the shaped hair bundle under bright light, checking the taper's symmetry before it would be ready for insertion into its prepared ferrule and handle assembly. "This one's perfect," she said. "Hand me a size six ferrule."

Cyrus passed her the requested ferrule, watching as she inserted the hair bundle with practiced precision before securing it permanently into the brush's final assembled form. "That taper looks incredible," he observed, genuinely impressed.

Marguerite set the finished brush aside to join the growing collection ready for quality testing, satisfied with another piece that would eventually help some artist create their own detailed work. "Twenty more brushes," she said. "This batch is almost complete."
