Lye water hissed faintly as Bathsheba poured it slowly into the melted oils, stirring carefully behind her safety goggles while the mixture began to thicken toward trace. The workshop smelled of lavender and olive oil, warm despite the open window letting in the cool afternoon.

Her business partner, Cosmo, measured out essential oils with careful precision, checking the recipe card taped to the counter one more time before adding them to the batch. "Fragrance is ready when you are," he said. "I doubled the lavender like you wanted for this batch."

Bathsheba nodded without looking up, focused on the immersion blender as the mixture thickened visibly with each pulse. "Perfect timing," she said. "We're right at trace, pour it in now before it sets up too thick to blend evenly."

Cosmo poured the fragrance oil in a slow stream while Bathsheba worked the blender through it, the mixture turning glossy and uniform as the scent bloomed through the workshop. She poured the finished soap into the waiting mold, tapping it firmly against the counter to release trapped air bubbles.

"Four weeks to cure," Bathsheba said, setting the mold aside on the curing rack next to a dozen other batches in various stages of readiness. Cosmo began cleaning the equipment, already discussing what scent combination they should try for the next batch.
