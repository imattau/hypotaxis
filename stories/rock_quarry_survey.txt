Dust hung thick in the afternoon heat as Fitzwilliam walked the quarry's newest cut face, hammer in hand to sample rock at intervals along the exposed geological layers. The survey would determine whether this section justified the expense of further extraction.

His geological assistant, Marguerite, logged each sample's location with GPS coordinates, cross-referencing the readings against the quarry's existing extraction map for consistency. "This layer's showing good limestone purity," she said, examining a freshly broken sample.

Fitzwilliam struck another sample free and examined its texture and color against his reference chart, comparing the quality to what they'd already extracted from the adjacent section. "Consistent with what we're already pulling," he said. "This section looks worth developing."

Marguerite finished logging the sample and moved further along the cut face, continuing the systematic survey that would eventually cover the entire newly exposed area. "Three more sample points before we finish this face," she said, checking her survey plan.

Fitzwilliam followed her along the face, collecting the remaining samples methodically while the afternoon sun beat down on the exposed rock walls surrounding them. "Good data today," he said. "This should give management enough to approve the expansion."
