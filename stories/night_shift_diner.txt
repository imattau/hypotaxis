Neon light buzzed faintly over the counter as Consuela refilled a lone trucker's coffee at two in the morning, the diner otherwise empty except for the low hum of the kitchen refrigerator. Rain had been falling steadily since midnight, streaking the windows facing the near-empty highway.

Her coworker, Baptiste, wiped down the grill in the back, occasionally glancing toward the door whenever the bell above it jingled with a rare late-night customer. "Slowest Thursday I've seen in months," he called out. "Even the regulars aren't braving this rain."

Consuela leaned against the counter and stretched her back, glancing at the clock above the pie case that read half past two. "At least it gives us time to restock," she said, nodding toward the near-empty napkin dispensers scattered along the counter. "I'll do the ketchups if you handle the sugar packets."

The bell jingled again, and a soaked young woman hurried in, shaking rain from her umbrella before sliding into a booth near the window. Baptiste grabbed a menu and headed over while Consuela started a fresh pot of coffee, the rich smell slowly filling the otherwise quiet diner.

By sunrise, the rain had finally tapered off, and the first of the early morning regulars began trickling in for their usual orders. Consuela and Baptiste moved through the familiar rush with practiced ease, the long slow night already fading into routine as the diner came back to life.
