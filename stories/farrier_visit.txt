Metal rang against metal as Guinevere shaped the glowing horseshoe on her portable anvil, sparks scattering across the barn's packed dirt floor with each precise hammer strike. The old mare waited patiently in her stall nearby, already accustomed to the ritual after a decade of regular visits.

The barn's owner, Peregrine, held the mare's lead rope loosely, running a calming hand along her neck whenever she shifted her weight. "She's been favoring that front left again," he said. "Nothing dramatic, just a little hesitant on the turns lately."

Guinevere quenched the shoe in a bucket of water with a sharp hiss of steam and carried it over, checking its shape against the mare's hoof before making any final adjustments. "Let's take a look," she said, lifting the hoof gently and examining the frog and sole for any obvious signs of bruising or infection.

"Nothing serious here," Guinevere said after a careful inspection, setting the hoof back down and reaching for her rasp. "Might just be the angle wearing unevenly. I'll adjust the trim a bit this time and we'll see if that helps her turns." Peregrine nodded, visibly relieved at the reassurance.

Guinevere worked methodically through the trim and fitting, the mare standing calm and still under Peregrine's steady hand the entire time. When the final shoe was nailed and clinched, Guinevere straightened up and stepped back to check the mare's stance, satisfied with the level, even set of all four feet.
