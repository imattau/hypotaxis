Tiny screws lay organized across Bartholomew's felt mat as he disassembled the vintage rangefinder camera's shutter mechanism, tracing the source of the sticking curtain that had frustrated the owner for years. This particular model hadn't been manufactured since the 1960s.

His workshop assistant, Persimmon, cleaned individual shutter blades with a specialized solvent, removing decades of accumulated grime from the delicate mechanism's moving parts. "Most of the blades move freely now," she said. "Just two still feel sluggish."

Bartholomew examined the shutter's timing mechanism closely, finding a small amount of dried lubricant that had gummed up the spring's action over the decades of disuse. "Found the problem," he said. "Old lubricant's turned to varnish. We'll need to clean this thoroughly."

Persimmon finished cleaning the sluggish blades and tested their movement again, satisfied to find them moving smoothly after the careful solvent treatment. "These two are free now," she said. "Just the timing spring left to address."

Bartholomew carefully cleaned the gummed spring and applied fresh, appropriate lubricant, testing the shutter's action several times to confirm the timing had returned to specification. "That's it," he said. "This old girl's ready to capture images again."
