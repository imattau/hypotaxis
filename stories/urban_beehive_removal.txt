Wall cavity buzzed audibly as Percival cut carefully through the drywall, exposing the established honeybee colony that had taken up residence inside the old house's exterior wall over several seasons. The homeowners wanted the bees relocated, not exterminated.

His removal partner, Cassiopeia, prepared the vacuum system that would gently collect the bees without harming them, adjusting the suction to the specific level that wouldn't damage delicate wings. "Vacuum's ready," she said. "Let's see how big this colony really is."

Percival widened the opening carefully, revealing comb structure that suggested this colony had been established for at least two full seasons based on its extensive development. "This is a serious hive," he said. "Might take us most of the afternoon."

Cassiopeia began the careful process of vacuuming bees into the collection box, working methodically while Percival cut away comb sections for transfer into frames that would house the relocated colony. "Making good progress," she said, checking the collection box's fill level.

Percival finished removing the last section of comb and helped Cassiopeia complete the bee collection, satisfied with a successful removal that would give this colony a proper new home. "Good day's work," he said. "These girls get to keep living, just somewhere better for everyone."
