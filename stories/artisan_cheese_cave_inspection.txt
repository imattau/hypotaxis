Cool damp air greeted Fitzwilliam as he descended into the aging cave, headlamp cutting through darkness toward the long rows of wheels resting on ancient wooden shelves that had aged cheese for over a century. Monthly inspection day required careful attention.

His cellar assistant, Anouk, followed close behind carrying a brushing kit, ready to tend to any wheels showing signs of needing their regular rind maintenance. "Row five's looking a little dry," she said, running gloved fingers along one wheel's crusted surface.

Fitzwilliam knelt to inspect the wheel she'd indicated, turning it gently to check the rind's development on all sides before determining the appropriate treatment. "Needs another brine wipe," he said. "Humidity's been inconsistent this month, fighting us more than usual."

Anouk set down her kit and began mixing the brine solution in a small bucket, the salty smell mixing with the cave's earthy must as she prepared the treatment. Fitzwilliam moved down the row methodically, checking each wheel's progress with practiced efficiency.

Fitzwilliam finished his inspection of the row and glanced at his notes, satisfied with the wheels' steady progress despite this month's humidity challenges. "Another four weeks on this batch," he said. "Then we finally get to see if all this careful attention paid off."
