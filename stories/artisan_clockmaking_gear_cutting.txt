Brass swarf curled from Bartholomew's cutting tool as he shaped another gear's teeth on the small lathe, working toward the precise tooth count and profile the clock's movement design specifically required. Handmade clockwork demanded mathematical precision.

His apprentice, Seraphina, checked the completed gears against his technical drawings, confirming each tooth's dimensions matched the specifications before they could be approved for the movement's final assembly. "This gear matches perfectly," she said.

Bartholomew examined the gear she'd verified, spinning it between his fingers to check for any wobble or inconsistency that might affect the movement's eventual timekeeping accuracy. "Beautiful work," he said. "Add it to the assembly pile."

Seraphina set the approved gear aside and picked up the next one requiring verification, working through the growing collection of components that would eventually become a complete, functioning clock movement. "Twelve more to check," she reported.

Bartholomew continued cutting the next gear, the small lathe's steady hum filling the workshop as another component slowly took shape toward this ambitious clockwork commission's eventual completion. "This is my favorite part," he said. "Watching mathematics become motion."
