Vinyl crackled softly through the studio monitors as Theodora cued up the next record, checking the community station's overnight playlist against the request line's growing list of listener calls. Saturday night shifts always brought the most eclectic requests.

Her co-host, Balthazar, answered another incoming call, scribbling the caller's request onto a sticky note before adding it to the growing pile beside the mixing board. "Someone wants deep cuts only tonight," he said. "No singles, apparently they're feeling nostalgic."

Theodora smiled and flipped through her crate of vinyl, searching for something obscure enough to satisfy the specific request while still fitting the night's loose theme. "I've got just the thing," she said, pulling out a well-worn album from deep in the stack.

Balthazar answered another call while she cued the record, his easy banter with the listener audible even through his half of the conversation about the station's upcoming pledge drive. "We'll get that one queued up next," he told the caller, hanging up with a satisfied nod.

Theodora dropped the needle as her current song faded out, the studio filling with the warm crackle of vinyl before the requested deep cut began playing through the overnight airwaves. "That's for the nostalgic caller," she said into the microphone, "wherever you're listening tonight."
