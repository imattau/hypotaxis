Cold wind cut across the orchard as Ottokar wrapped insulation around the last hive in the row, preparing the colonies for winter's approaching harsh temperatures. Proper preparation now meant survival for these bees come spring.

His farmhand, Wilhelmina, checked each hive's food stores, lifting slightly to gauge weight and estimate whether supplemental feeding would be needed before the coldest months arrived. "This one feels light," she said, setting it back down carefully.

Ottokar finished wrapping his current hive and moved to examine the one she'd flagged, hefting it himself to confirm her assessment about the concerning weight. "You're right," he said. "Let's get sugar syrup on this one before we lose more warm days."

Wilhelmina prepared the feeding supplement while Ottokar continued wrapping the remaining hives, working efficiently to complete the winterization before the forecast's incoming cold front arrived. "Syrup's ready," she said, carrying it toward the light hive.

Ottokar finished the last hive's insulation and helped Wilhelmina position the feeder, satisfied with the preparation despite the light hive's earlier concerning weight. "That should get them through," he said. "Now we just wait and hope for a mild winter."
