Sawdust coated the workshop floor as Leopold ran the plane along the board's edge, checking the surface with a straightedge after every few passes to keep the joint perfectly flat. The commissioned table needed to ship by Friday, and the top still wasn't glued up.

His apprentice, Wilhelmina, sorted through the remaining boards for the tabletop, matching grain patterns as best she could before laying them out in the order they'd be joined. "This pair matches almost perfectly," she said, holding two boards edge to edge under the shop light.

Leopold set down his plane and walked over to inspect her arrangement, nodding approvingly at the grain continuity she'd found. "Good eye," he said. "The client's going to love this one. Let's get the biscuits cut before we lose the light in here."

Wilhelmina marked the joint locations with a pencil and carried the boards to the biscuit joiner, cutting careful slots at each mark while Leopold prepared the clamps and glue. Sawdust puffed out with each cut, settling into the afternoon light streaming through the shop's high windows.

By evening, the tabletop sat clamped and drying, glue squeezing out along the seams in thin, even beads that Leopold wiped away with a damp rag. "Should be ready to plane flat by morning," he said, stepping back to admire the nearly finished top glowing warm under the workshop lights.
