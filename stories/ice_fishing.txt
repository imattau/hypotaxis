Wind cut hard across the frozen lake as Torvald drilled through nearly two feet of ice, the auger's steady rhythm the only sound besides the occasional crack echoing across the frozen surface. His breath crystallized instantly in the subzero air.

His fishing partner, Marguerite, set up the small portable shelter a few feet away, anchoring it against the wind before ducking inside to escape the worst of the cold. "Depth finder says we're over twenty feet here," she called out. "Should be good structure for lake trout this time of year."

Torvald finished the hole and knelt to clear the slush with a skimmer, glancing toward the shelter where Marguerite was already threading bait onto her line. "Let's try jigging near the bottom first," he said. "They've been holding deep the last few trips out here."

Marguerite lowered her line through the hole and settled into the shelter's small folding chair, watching the rod tip for the faintest hint of a strike. Torvald joined her a moment later, setting up his own line in the second hole he'd drilled nearby.

They sat in comfortable silence for nearly twenty minutes before Marguerite's rod tip dipped sharply, and she set the hook with a practiced flick of her wrist. "Fish on," she said, already reeling steadily as Torvald grabbed the net and knelt by the hole.
