Metallic ringing filled the workshop as Ambrosine hammered another note into the steel drum's surface, the sinking and shaping process requiring hundreds of precise strikes to create each individual note's proper pitch and resonance. This particular pan needed twenty-three distinct notes.

Her apprentice, Casimir, tested the already-formed notes with a small mallet, checking each one's pitch against a tuning reference to catch any that had drifted during the shaping process. "The low notes are holding steady," he said. "Upper register's a bit sharp though."

Ambrosine paused her hammering to check the notes he'd flagged, striking each one herself to confirm his assessment before making the fine adjustments the correction required. "You're right," she said. "Let me true these up before we continue with the remaining notes."

Casimir watched her work, learning the subtle hammer technique that could shift a note's pitch by fractions of a tone without disturbing its neighbors on the drum's curved surface. "How do you know exactly how hard to strike?" he asked.

Ambrosine smiled and continued her careful adjustments, the question familiar from years of teaching this demanding craft to hopeful apprentices. "Thousands of hours of listening," she said. "Your ears will learn it before your hands do."
