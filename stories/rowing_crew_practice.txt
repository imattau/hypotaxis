Oars sliced cleanly through the still morning water as Persimmon called the stroke rate from the coxswain's seat, watching her crew's eight-person rhythm settle into the smooth cadence they'd been chasing all season. Regatta season started in three weeks.

Her coach, Bartholomew, followed alongside in the launch boat, megaphone ready to call corrections as he studied each rower's technique through the still, foggy water. "Bow pair, you're rushing the catch," he called. "Let the boat run beneath you."

Persimmon relayed the correction to her crew, adjusting the stroke rate slightly to give the bow pair room to implement the change without disrupting the boat's overall rhythm. "Settling to a thirty-two," she called. "Focus on the catch timing, not the power."

Bartholomew watched the adjustment take effect, nodding as the boat's set improved noticeably with the corrected timing from the bow pair's synchronized catches. "Much better," he called through the megaphone. "Hold that feeling for the next five hundred meters."

Persimmon called the crew through the next segment, the boat's rhythm now clean and powerful as they settled into race pace for the first time all practice. "That's what we need for Saturday," Bartholomew called, satisfaction clear in his voice.
