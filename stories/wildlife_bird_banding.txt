Mist nets stretched between poles caught the morning light as Anastasiya carefully extracted a warbler from the fine mesh, cradling the small bird gently before carrying it toward the banding station for processing. Migration season brought researchers out before dawn.

Her banding partner, Cornelius, prepared the appropriately sized band and recording sheet, ready to log the species and measurements the moment Anastasiya brought the bird to the station. "Looks like a yellow warbler," he said, examining her catch.

Anastasiya confirmed his identification and began the careful banding process, securing the numbered band around the bird's leg with practiced precision that minimized the animal's stress during handling. "Confirmed yellow warbler," she said. "Nice healthy weight too."

Cornelius recorded the measurements she called out, adding this individual to the season's growing dataset that would eventually inform migration pattern research across multiple monitoring stations. "That's our fourth warbler this morning," he noted.

Anastasiya released the bird gently, watching it fly off strong and healthy toward the surrounding trees before turning back to check the nets for any new arrivals. "Good morning for warblers," she said. "Let's see what else is waiting in the nets."
