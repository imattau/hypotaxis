Ranger Okafor crouched low behind a screen of brush, breath fogging faintly in the cold morning air as she raised her binoculars toward the den entrance across the ravine. Snow dusted the pine branches overhead, muffling every sound of the forest.

Beside her, wildlife photographer Petra adjusted her camera lens with slow, careful movements, careful not to let the equipment click or scrape against the rocks. "Any sign of the pups yet?" she whispered, eye pressed to the viewfinder.

"Not yet," Okafor murmured back. "The mother's been out hunting since before dawn. She should be back soon." As if summoned by the words, a grey shape emerged from the treeline below, moving with unhurried, deliberate steps toward the den, a limp hare hanging from her jaws.

Petra's camera shutter began clicking softly, rapid-fire, as the wolf paused at the den entrance and let out a low call. Three small shapes tumbled out from the darkness within, yipping and stumbling over each other in their eagerness to reach her. Okafor lowered her binoculars just slightly, unable to suppress a small smile at the sight.

The mother wolf dropped the hare and stood watch as the pups swarmed around it, snapping playfully at each other more than the food itself. Petra kept shooting, capturing frame after frame, while overhead a hawk circled once against the pale morning sky before wheeling away toward the ridge.
