Heat shimmered off the kiln's brick walls as Thaddeus checked the pyrometric cones through the small peephole, judging by their slow bending exactly how much longer the firing needed to run. The wood-fired kiln had been burning steadily for eighteen hours already, and everyone on the crew was running on fumes and strong coffee.

His firing partner, Wilhelmina, fed another split log into the firebox, sweat streaking through the ash already coating her forearms. "Cone six is starting to droop," she said, squinting through her own peephole on the opposite side. "Maybe another hour if we keep the temperature climbing steady."

Thaddeus nodded and adjusted the damper slightly, coaxing a bit more draft through the chamber without risking a temperature spike that could crack the pots inside. "Keep feeding it small and steady," he said. "We rush this now and three weeks of glazing work goes up in smoke."

Wilhelmina wiped her brow with the back of her wrist and reached for another log, stacking it carefully near the firebox door for the next feed. The two of them fell into the familiar rhythm they'd built over a dozen firings together, barely needing words anymore to coordinate the work.

By the time cone seven finally slumped fully at dawn, both of them were exhausted but grinning, watching the first hints of sunrise catch the kiln's shimmering heat above the chimney. "Told you we'd make it," Thaddeus said, already beginning the long process of sealing the kiln for its slow cooldown.
