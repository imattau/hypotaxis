Dust and grit whipped against the windshield as Rosalind gripped the wheel, eyes fixed on the churning wall cloud rotating slowly against the green-tinted sky ahead. The radio crackled with updated coordinates from the spotter network, each report sharpening the tension in the truck's cab.

Her research partner, Wendell, hunched over the laptop mounted to the dashboard, radar data scrolling faster than he could fully process. "Rotation's tightening," he said, voice tight with focus. "If this drops a funnel, it's going to happen in the next ten minutes."

Rosalind eased off the accelerator and pulled onto the shoulder, angling the truck for a clear view while staying close enough to the escape route they'd mapped that morning. "Get the camera ready," she said. "I want this one on record, not just the instruments."

Wendell fumbled with the tripod, hands cold despite the humid air, and had barely locked it into place when a thin gray funnel began stretching down from the cloud base toward the empty field ahead of them. Rosalind exhaled slowly, calling out wind speed readings as the funnel touched down and kicked up a churning cloud of dust and debris.

They tracked it for six more minutes before it dissipated as quickly as it had formed, leaving only a scoured line through the field to mark where it had been. Wendell packed away the equipment with hands that hadn't quite stopped shaking, while Rosalind was already reviewing the footage, grinning despite herself.
