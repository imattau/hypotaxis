Damp leaf litter crunched softly under Balthazar's boots as he crouched beside a cluster of mushrooms at the base of an old oak, examining the gills carefully before deciding whether to add them to his basket. Autumn rain the past week had brought out an exceptional flush across the forest floor.

His foraging student, Ottoline, hovered nearby with her own basket, holding up a mushroom she'd found for his inspection before touching it further. "Is this one safe?" she asked. "The cap color looks similar to the ones you showed me earlier."

Balthazar took the mushroom carefully and turned it over, checking the gill attachment and spore color before answering her question. "Close, but no," he said. "See how the gills run down the stem instead of staying free? That's a different family entirely. Leave that one."

Ottoline set the mushroom down carefully away from her basket, taking mental note of the distinguishing feature he'd pointed out for future identification. "Good thing I asked," she said, moving to examine another cluster a few feet away under a fallen log.

Balthazar continued working through the oak's cluster, adding several confirmed specimens to his own basket while keeping an eye on Ottoline's progress nearby. "When in doubt, always ask," he said. "A mushroom basket isn't worth a hospital visit."
