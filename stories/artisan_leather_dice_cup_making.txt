Thick vegetable-tanned leather curved around Fitzwilliam's wooden form as he shaped another traditional dice cup, working the wet material into the cylindrical shape that generations of gaming table craftsmen had produced before him. Custom dice cups demanded sturdy construction.

His apprentice, Marguerite, prepared the felt lining that would eventually muffle the dice sounds inside the finished cup, cutting it to the precise dimensions needed for a snug, secure fit. "Lining's cut and ready to install," she said.

Fitzwilliam examined the leather's shape after removing it from the forming mold, checking symmetry before it could be set aside to dry into its final permanent cylindrical form. "Beautiful shape," he said. "This one's going to feel great in hand."

Marguerite began installing the felt lining into the drying cup, working the material carefully to ensure it adhered smoothly without any wrinkles that might affect the dice-rolling sound quality inside. "The lining's going in smoothly," she observed.

Fitzwilliam checked her lining work, satisfied with how this particular cup was developing toward its eventual leather-stamping and finishing touches. "Perfect installation," he said. "One more cup and this custom set's complete."
