Dust motes drifted through the shaft of afternoon light in the university archive as Professor Adeyemi carefully turned the brittle page of a centuries-old ledger, cotton gloves muffling her fingertips. Rows of towering shelves stretched into shadow on either side of her narrow desk.

Her graduate student, Simon, hurried over with another stack of folders balanced precariously against his chest. "I think I found the shipping records you were looking for," he said, setting the stack down with visible relief before it could topple.

"Finally," Adeyemi murmured, pulling the top folder toward her and untying its frayed ribbon binding. She scanned the faded ink line by line, her brow furrowing deeper with each entry. "This doesn't match the date in the captain's log at all."

Simon leaned over her shoulder, squinting at the cramped handwriting. "Maybe it's a different shipment," he offered, though he didn't sound convinced himself. Adeyemi shook her head slowly, already reaching for a magnifying glass from the desk's cluttered surface.

"No, look here," she said, tapping the page with one gloved finger. "Same vessel name, same captain, but the cargo manifest is completely different from what the museum has on record." She sat back in her chair, exhaling slowly as the implications settled over her. Simon pulled out his phone to photograph the page, careful to angle the flash away from the fragile paper, while Adeyemi reached for her notebook to begin transcribing the discrepancy word for word.
