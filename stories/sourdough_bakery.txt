Flour dusted the wooden counter as Bernadette folded the sourdough gently, working the developing gluten with practiced patience before returning it to its proofing basket. The starter had taken three years to develop its exact flavor, and she guarded the routine religiously.

Her new hire, Percival, watched the folding technique closely, trying to memorize the exact hand movements she used to avoid deflating the dough's delicate structure. "It looks so gentle," he said. "I always tear mine a little when I try that fold."

Bernadette paused to demonstrate the motion again, slowing it down so Percival could see exactly how her fingers curved beneath the dough without gripping too tightly. "You're using your fingertips instead of your whole palm," she said. "Trust the dough's structure, don't fight it."

Percival attempted the fold on his own practice batch, moving more slowly this time and using his palm the way she'd shown him, watching with relief as the dough held together cleanly. "That felt completely different," he said, genuine surprise in his voice.

Bernadette nodded approvingly and returned to her own loaves, checking the proofing baskets lined up along the counter for the morning's full baking schedule. "Keep practicing that fold," she said. "By next month it'll be automatic, I promise."
