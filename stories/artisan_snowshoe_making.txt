Steamed ash wood bent slowly around Torbjorn's jig as he shaped another snowshoe frame, working the traditional bentwood technique that had outfitted trappers and hikers through generations of harsh northern winters. Custom snowshoes demanded exact proportions.

His apprentice, Cassiopeia, prepared the rawhide lacing that would fill the frame's interior, cutting long strips to the precise width needed for the traditional webbing pattern this particular design required. "Lacing's cut and ready," she said.

Torbjorn examined the frame's curve after removing it from the steaming jig, checking symmetry before it could be set aside to dry into its final permanent shape. "Beautiful bend," he said. "This frame's going to hold up for decades."

Cassiopeia began weaving the rawhide lacing through the drying frame's pre-drilled holes, following the traditional pattern that distributed weight properly across the snowshoe's surface. "The pattern's coming together nicely," she observed.

Torbjorn checked her weaving progress, satisfied with the tension and pattern accuracy that would determine how well this particular snowshoe performed in deep, powdery snow conditions. "Perfect tension," he said. "One more shoe and this pair's complete."
