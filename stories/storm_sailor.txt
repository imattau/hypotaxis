The sky over the harbor had gone the color of a bruise by the time Captain Rin ordered the sails reefed. Wind tore at the rigging, and the small fishing boat pitched hard over a swell twice its usual height.

"We should have turned back an hour ago!" shouted Dax, gripping the mast with both arms as spray drenched the deck. Rin ignored him, hauling on a rope with her whole weight, jaw set against the wind.

"The cove's closer than the harbor now," she called back. "We ride it out or we don't ride it out at all." A wave broke over the bow, and Dax stumbled, catching himself on a coil of rope just before he went over the side.

Below deck, young Finn clung to a support beam, seasick and pale, listening to the hull groan around him. He had begged to come along on this trip. Now he wished he'd stayed on shore with his mother, watching the boats from the safety of the pier.

The boat lurched sideways, and for a moment everything seemed to hang suspended before crashing back down into the trough of a wave. Rin's voice cut through the storm, steady despite the chaos. "Hold the line, Dax! We're almost past the point!"

Through the small porthole, Finn caught a glimpse of jagged rocks sliding past in the grey light, close enough to touch, and then the boat swung hard into calmer water beyond the point.
