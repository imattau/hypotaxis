Sweat stung Delphine's eyes as she pushed through the marathon's final mile, legs burning with a fatigue that had settled deep into her bones somewhere around mile twenty. The crowd's noise blurred into a single roaring wall of sound as the finish line arch came into view ahead.

Her running coach, Barnabus, stood near the final water station where he'd promised to be, cupping his hands to shout encouragement as she passed. "You've got this, Delphine! Strong finish, strong finish!" His voice cut through the noise, familiar even amid the chaos of the crowd.

Delphine found something extra in her legs at the sound of his voice, pushing her pace up slightly despite the screaming protest from every muscle in her body. She'd trained eight months for this exact moment, and she wasn't about to let the last four hundred meters slip away from her now.

The finish line arch grew larger with each stride, volunteers and spectators packed thick along the barriers on either side, all noise and color and motion blurring past her peripheral vision. Delphine crossed the timing mat with both arms raised, a raw sound of relief and triumph tearing from her throat.

Barnabus pushed through the finisher's area a few minutes later, catching her just as her legs finally gave out from the effort, wrapping an arm around her to keep her upright. "Personal best," he said, checking the watch on his own wrist. "By almost six minutes. I told you the hill work would pay off."
