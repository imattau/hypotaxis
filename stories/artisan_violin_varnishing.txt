Amber varnish caught the afternoon light as Anouk applied another thin coat to the violin's carved top plate, building the finish gradually toward the deep, glowing color that distinguished her instruments from mass-produced alternatives. Each coat needed days to properly cure.

Her apprentice, Desmond, prepared the next batch of varnish, mixing the specific resin combination according to the recipe Anouk had refined over twenty years of careful experimentation. "This batch looks the right consistency," he said, checking the varnish's viscosity.

Anouk finished her current coat and examined the instrument under bright light, checking for any drips or uneven application that might mar the finish's eventual appearance. "Nice and even," she said. "Let's let this cure before the next coat."

Desmond set the prepared varnish aside for later use and helped Anouk position the violin on its drying rack, ensuring proper airflow around the instrument during the curing process. "How many more coats until it's finished?" he asked.

Anouk considered the instrument's current color depth against her mental image of the finished piece, calculating the remaining work still required. "Maybe six more coats," she said. "This varnish is what makes these violins sing the way they do."
