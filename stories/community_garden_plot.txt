Morning dew soaked through Ezekiel's gloves as he turned the soil in his community garden plot, breaking up clumps before the season's first planting of the spring. The shared garden had become his weekly ritual since retiring last year.

His plot neighbor, Philomena, knelt at the adjacent bed transplanting tomato seedlings from their starter trays into the freshly warmed soil. "Your peas are already climbing," she said, nodding toward his trellis. "Mine barely poked through last week."

Ezekiel straightened up and stretched his back, glancing over at her seedlings with the easy familiarity of someone who'd shared this garden with her for three seasons now. "Started mine indoors a bit early," he said. "Risky with the frost, but it paid off this year."

Philomena finished transplanting another seedling and firmed the soil around its base, checking the plant's stability before moving to the next one in her tray. "Might steal that trick next year," she said. "Assuming you're willing to share your secrets."

Ezekiel laughed and returned to turning his own soil, the rhythmic work satisfying in a way his old office job never quite managed to be. "Happy to share," he said. "Gardens work better when everyone's plot does well, less competition for the water spigot."
