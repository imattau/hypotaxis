The attic stairs groaned under Callum's weight as he climbed up with a flashlight clenched between his teeth, cardboard box tucked under one arm. His landlord had asked him to clear out the last of the previous tenant's belongings before the weekend.

Dust hung thick in the beam of his flashlight, and cobwebs stretched between the rafters like torn lace. He set the box down near an old steamer trunk and knelt to check its contents, ignoring the cold draft that curled around his ankles despite the closed window at the far end.

Something scraped softly across the floorboards behind him. Callum froze, flashlight beam trembling as he turned toward the sound. Nothing there but shadows and a rocking chair, its runners still swaying faintly though no wind stirred the dusty air.

"Just settling," he muttered to himself, though his voice came out thinner than he intended. He turned back to the trunk, flipping the rusted latch open with some effort. Inside lay neatly folded blankets, yellowed photographs, and a child's music box tucked into one corner.

He lifted the music box out carefully, and the moment his fingers closed around it, a faint whisper drifted past his ear, too soft to make out words. Callum spun around, flashlight sweeping wildly across the empty attic, his heart hammering against his ribs. The rocking chair had stopped moving. The music box in his hands began, very faintly, to play.
