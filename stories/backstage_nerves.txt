Backstage, the bass line from the opening act thumped through the concrete walls as Lena tuned her guitar for the third time in ten minutes, fingers trembling slightly on the strings. Her bandmate Oz sprawled across a battered couch nearby, seemingly unbothered by the roar of the crowd beyond the curtain.

"You're going to snap a string if you keep doing that," Oz said without opening his eyes, one boot tapping idly against the couch's armrest. Lena shot him a look and set the guitar down, wiping her palms against her jeans.

"This is the biggest crowd we've ever played," she said. "I'm allowed to be nervous." The stage manager, a wiry woman with a headset clipped to her ear, poked her head through the doorway and held up five fingers before disappearing again.

Oz finally sat up, stretching his arms over his head with a groan. "Five minutes," he said, grinning at her. "You've played smaller rooms shaking worse than this. You'll walk out there and forget all about it the second the lights hit you." Lena picked the guitar back up, slinging the strap over her shoulder, and took a slow breath.

The rest of the band trickled in from the hallway, drummer Cass twirling a stick between her fingers, already nodding along to a rhythm only she could hear. Together they moved toward the stage entrance as the opening act's final chord rang out and the crowd's roar swelled even louder beyond the curtain.
