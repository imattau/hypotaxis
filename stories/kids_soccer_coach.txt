Whistle around her neck, Marisol jogged along the sideline as her team of eight-year-olds scrambled after the ball in a chaotic cluster, more enthusiasm than actual strategy on display this early in the season. Saturday morning practice had become the highlight of her week, unexpectedly.

Her assistant coach, Desmond, jogged over from the far side of the field, clipboard in hand with the drill rotation they'd planned for today's session. "They're bunching up again," he said. "Should we run the spacing drill now or let them tire out first?"

Marisol blew her whistle to pause the scrimmage, gathering the small cluster of kids around her with practiced patience despite their obvious eagerness to keep playing. "Spacing drill," she said. "Better now while they can still focus on the instructions."

Desmond set up the cones quickly while Marisol explained the drill in simple terms the kids could follow, demonstrating the spacing herself by jogging between two imaginary positions. "Everyone spread out like this," she said. "Give your teammates room to pass to you."

The kids attempted the drill with mixed success, some grasping the concept immediately while others drifted back toward the ball out of habit. Marisol laughed and gently redirected the stragglers, patient with the learning curve as Desmond called out encouragement from the sideline.
