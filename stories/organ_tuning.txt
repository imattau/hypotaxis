Dust motes drifted through afternoon light as Reginald climbed into the pipe organ's cramped chamber, tuning fork in hand to check each rank against the reference pitch before Sunday's service. The old cathedral organ had thousands of pipes, and the reed stops always drifted first in changing weather.

His assistant, Marceline, sat at the console below, holding notes at his instruction while he worked his way methodically through the chamber's rows of pipes. "Reed rank sounding now," she called up, pressing the key he'd indicated on the marked stop list.

Reginald listened carefully to the pipe's tone, comparing it against his tuning fork before making a small adjustment to the tuning slide with practiced fingers. "Little flat," he said. "Hold that note again, let me confirm the correction."

Marceline held the key steady while Reginald listened again, this time nodding in satisfaction as the pitch settled into perfect agreement with his fork. "That's it," he called down. "Next rank, the same note please."

Marceline moved to the next stop on his list and pressed the corresponding key, the chamber filling with a new pipe's tone as Reginald continued his patient work through the instrument's thousands of voices. "We've got maybe forty more pipes before we're done for today," he said, already reaching for the next tuning slide.
