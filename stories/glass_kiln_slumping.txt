Kiln heat shimmered against Beauregard's safety glasses as he checked the glass bowl's progress through the small viewing port, timing the slump against his notes from previous firings of this exact mold. The commissioned serving bowl needed precise, even curves.

His studio partner, Rosalind, monitored the digital controller's temperature readout, calling out updates as the kiln climbed steadily toward the critical slumping temperature. "We're at eleven hundred degrees," she said. "Should start seeing movement any minute now."

Beauregard peered through the viewing port again, watching the flat glass disc begin its slow, gravity-driven descent into the ceramic mold's curved shape beneath it. "There it goes," he said. "Nice and even so far, no bubbling or uneven stretching."

Rosalind adjusted the kiln's program slightly based on his observation, extending the hold time to ensure the glass fully conformed to the mold's precise curves. "Let's give it another fifteen minutes at this temperature," she said. "Better to be patient than rush the slump."

Beauregard checked the viewing port one final time before stepping back, satisfied with the bowl's developing shape and the smooth, professional curve taking form. "This is going to be a beautiful piece," he said. "Worth every minute of the wait."
