Deckled paper edges caught the light as Persimmon folded another signature for the custom journal commission, working toward the precise page count the client had specifically requested for their travel diary. Handbound journals demanded careful planning.

Her apprentice, Cornelius, prepared the leather cover material, cutting it to the exact dimensions that would accommodate the journal's specific spine width once all signatures were sewn together. "Cover's cut and ready," he said.

Persimmon finished folding her current signature and added it to the growing stack, checking the count against her planning notes before continuing the methodical folding process. "Halfway through the signatures," she said.

Cornelius watched her folding technique closely, absorbing the subtle hand movements that produced consistently sharp, even folds despite the paper's substantial weight and texture. "How do you keep the folds so crisp?" he asked.

Persimmon smiled and continued folding, the question familiar from previous apprentices who'd asked essentially the same thing during their own early training. "Bone folder, patience, and about a thousand practice folds," she said. "You're getting there."
