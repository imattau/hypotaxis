Colored lights flickered weakly as Ferdinand traced the vintage jukebox's wiring harness, searching for the short circuit that had left half its bulbs dark for months. This particular model had entertained diner customers since the 1950s.

His workshop partner, Xiomara, tested the record-changing mechanism, cycling through several selections to check the mechanical arm's alignment against the stack of vinyl singles. "Changer's working smoothly now," she said. "Just the lighting circuit left to fix."

Ferdinand traced the wiring to a corroded junction point, finding the source of the intermittent electrical fault that had plagued the machine's light display for the past several months. "Found it," he said. "This connector's completely corroded through."

Xiomara finished her mechanism tests and moved to help Ferdinand with the wiring repair, holding components steady while he worked to replace the failed connector. "Once this is fixed," she said, "we'll finally have this old girl back to full glory."

Ferdinand completed the connector replacement and powered up the jukebox, watching with satisfaction as the colored lights blazed to full brightness across the machine's ornate facade. "There she is," he said. "Ready to spin records again."
