Mosquitoes buzzed thick around Peregrine's ears as he hefted the canoe onto his shoulders, the portage trail stretching muddy and uneven through the dense forest ahead. Three lakes still separated them from tonight's campsite, and the afternoon light was already beginning to slant lower through the trees.

His paddling partner, Georgiana, followed behind carrying both packs, adjusting the straps as she navigated a particularly root-tangled section of trail. "This portage is longer than the map suggested," she called ahead. "Feels like we're past the halfway point though."

Peregrine grunted under the canoe's weight and paused briefly to reposition the yoke against his shoulders, catching his breath before continuing down the muddy path. "Should open up to the lake soon," he said. "I remember this section from the last trip, water's not much farther."

Georgiana caught up to him at a fallen log crossing the trail, steadying his elbow as he navigated the obstacle without setting the canoe down. "Careful," she said. "Last thing we need is a cracked hull three lakes from the car."

Peregrine cleared the log safely and continued forward, finally spotting glints of water through the trees ahead as the trail began sloping gently downward. "There it is," he said, quickening his pace despite the canoe's weight. "Almost there, then we can finally rest these shoulders."
