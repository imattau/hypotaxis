Blue-white flame hissed from the torch as Ottavia heated the glass tube's bend point, working the fragile material with careful, practiced rotation before shaping the precise curve the client's logo design required. Neon bending was a dying craft, and she guarded the skill fiercely.

Her apprentice, Fitzgerald, prepared the next section of tubing, measuring and marking the bend points according to the paper template pinned above the workbench. "This curve looks trickier than the last piece," he said, studying her technique closely.

Ottavia completed the bend and set the glass aside to cool slowly, checking its shape against the template before moving to the next section of the elaborate design. "Tighter curves need more patience," she said. "Rush the heat and the glass just collapses on you."

Fitzgerald handed her the next section of tubing, watching as she began heating it slowly, the flame's color shifting subtly as the glass approached the temperature needed for bending. "How many bends left on this piece?" he asked, glancing at the reference sketch.

Ottavia counted the remaining bends mentally while continuing her careful work, calculating how much longer the full sign would take to complete before pumping and testing. "Six more," she said. "Then we get to see if this whole thing actually glows the way the client imagined."
