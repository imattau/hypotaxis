Century-old dust rose in the afternoon light as Wilhelmina pried loose another rotted floorboard from the carriage house's interior, exposing the original beams beneath decades of accumulated debris. The historical society's grant depended on an accurate restoration.

Her restoration partner, Casimir, documented each discovered feature with careful photographs, building a record of the structure's original construction methods for the society's archives. "These joints are hand-cut," he said. "This confirms the 1890s construction date."

Wilhelmina set the rotted board aside and examined the exposed beam more closely, running her fingers along tool marks that told their own story about the building's original craftsmen. "Beautiful joinery," she said. "Whoever built this really knew their trade."

Casimir finished photographing the joint and moved to help her clear the remaining debris, working carefully to avoid damaging any other original features that might be hidden beneath the accumulated grime. "Grant inspector visits next week," he said. "We should have this section fully documented by then."

Wilhelmina nodded and continued the careful excavation, already anticipating what other original details might be waiting beneath the carriage house's neglected floor. "This building's earning its preservation," she said. "Every layer we uncover just proves it more."
