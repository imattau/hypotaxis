City noise drifted up faintly as Cassandra lifted another frame from the rooftop hive, checking the brood pattern against what she'd expect from a healthy queen this time of year. Twelve stories below, traffic continued its indifferent rush past the building's entrance.

Her building's facilities manager, Ignatius, stood a cautious distance away in borrowed protective gear, having volunteered to observe the inspection after months of curiosity about the hives he maintained access for. "Looks pretty healthy to my untrained eye," he said. "Lots of bees, anyway."

Cassandra held the frame up to better catch the afternoon light, pointing out the pattern of capped brood cells for Ignatius's benefit. "See this solid pattern here?" she said. "That tells me the queen's laying well, no gaps or scattered cells that would suggest problems."

Ignatius leaned in carefully to look, keeping a respectful distance from the frame despite his gradually increasing comfort around the buzzing insects. "The honey supers look heavy already," he observed. "Good year for the rooftop garden's pollination too, probably."

Cassandra smiled and returned the frame carefully to the hive, closing it up methodically before removing her own protective veil. "City bees do surprisingly well," she said. "All these rooftop gardens give them more variety than you'd expect."
