Dust motes swirled in the afternoon light as Evangelina carefully stripped decades of old varnish from the cabinet's carved door, revealing the rich walnut grain hidden beneath years of neglect. The piece had come to her shop in rough shape, but something in its craftsmanship had convinced her it was worth saving.

Her assistant, Balthazar, worked on the matching drawer nearby, sanding a water-damaged corner with slow, careful strokes to avoid removing more wood than necessary. "This dovetail joint is beautiful," he said, running a finger along the exposed joinery. "Whoever made this really knew what they were doing."

Evangelina paused her stripping to lean over and examine the joint herself, nodding in appreciation at the tight, precise craftsmanship. "Early nineteenth century, if I had to guess," she said. "They don't teach joinery like this anymore, not for furniture people actually intend to use."

Balthazar finished sanding and moved to test-fit the drawer back into its frame, pleased to find it slid smoothly despite the repairs. "Should we match the original finish or go lighter?" he asked, glancing over at the cabinet's newly stripped door glowing warm in the light.

Evangelina considered the wood grain thoughtfully before answering, already picturing the finished piece in her mind. "Original finish," she said. "This wood's earned the chance to look exactly like it did the day someone first built it."
