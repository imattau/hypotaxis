Cane fibers split precisely under Fergus's specialized knife as he shaped another bagpipe chanter reed, working toward the exact profile that would produce the specific tone quality his customers had come to expect. Reed making demanded acoustic knowledge alongside craft skill.

His apprentice, Seraphina, tested a completed reed's response on a practice chanter, listening carefully for the tonal characteristics that would indicate whether Fergus's latest batch had achieved proper calibration. "This one sounds a bit sharp," she said.

Fergus took the reed back and examined its profile, identifying where a slight adjustment might correct the tuning issue she'd correctly identified through careful listening. "Good ear," he said. "Let me shave just a touch more here."

Seraphina watched him make the minute adjustment, absorbing the subtle relationship between cane thickness and resulting pitch that seemed to come so naturally to Fergus after decades of practice. "How do you know exactly how much to remove?" she asked.

Fergus tested the adjusted reed himself, satisfied with the corrected tone before setting it aside with the other completed reeds awaiting final quality inspection. "Experience," he said. "And roughly ten thousand ruined reeds along the way."
