Pre-dawn darkness still hung over the barn as Josephina attached the milking machine's cups to the first cow in line, the rhythmic hum of the vacuum pump filling the otherwise quiet space. Four in the morning had become as natural to her as any other hour, after eleven years of dairy farming.

Her hired hand, Cornelius, moved the next cow into position, checking the animal's udder briefly for any signs of mastitis before securing her in the stanchion. "Bessie's milk looks a little off color today," he said. "Might want to check her separately before it goes in the main tank."

Josephina finished attaching the current cow's cups and moved over to examine Bessie more closely, checking for the telltale signs Cornelius had mentioned. "You're right, better safe than sorry," she said. "Milk her into the separate bucket, we'll test it before combining."

Cornelius nodded and prepared the separate collection bucket, working efficiently despite the early hour and the added complication to their usual routine. "Rest of the herd looks healthy," he said. "Just Bessie causing trouble this morning."

Josephina moved down the line to the next cow, the barn's rhythm settling into its familiar pre-dawn routine despite the small disruption. "One problem at a time," she said. "At least it's just the one cow and not the whole herd acting up."
