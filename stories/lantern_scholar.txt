The scriptorium was lit only by a single oil lantern when Brother Aldric finally set down his quill, ink-stained fingers trembling from hours of copying. Outside the narrow window, the monastery bell tolled for the midnight hour, its sound rolling out over the sleeping courtyard.

Sister Wren appeared in the doorway, a folded cloak over her shoulders and a fresh candle in hand. "The abbot will have your head if he finds you still working at this hour," she said, though she stepped inside anyway, setting the candle beside his.

"Two more pages," Aldric murmured, eyes fixed on the manuscript before him, its illuminated letters gleaming faintly in the lantern light. "The merchant's ship sails at dawn, and this copy must go with it."

Wren picked up one of the finished pages, studying the careful script and the small painted flourish of a bird in the margin. "You always did draw better than you wrote," she said with a faint smile, setting the page back down gently.

Aldric allowed himself a tired laugh, dipping his quill once more. "Brother Corwin says the same, usually right before he asks me to illuminate his own pages for him." A gust of wind rattled the window shutter, and the lantern flame guttered, throwing long shadows across the stone walls.

Wren moved to steady the shutter, latching it firmly against the night wind. When she turned back, Aldric had already bent low over the parchment again, quill moving in careful, steady strokes beneath the lantern's small circle of light.
