Chain grease coated Percival's fingers as he worked the derailleur back into alignment, the vintage road bike's owner waiting patiently while browsing the shop's wall of restored frames. This particular model dated back nearly fifty years, and finding compatible parts had taken weeks.

His shop partner, Anneliese, wheeled another restoration project out from the back room, checking its tire pressure before setting it up for a customer's test ride. "The Bianchi's ready for pickup," she said. "Rode it around the block myself this morning, shifts perfectly."

Percival finished the derailleur adjustment and spun the pedals to test the chain's smooth engagement across all the gears, satisfied with the crisp shifting he'd achieved. "This old girl's finally running like new again," he said. "Owner's going to be thrilled when he sees it."

Anneliese helped the waiting customer swing a leg over the newly repaired bike, offering a few adjustment tips about the seat height before sending him off for a test lap around the parking lot. "Let me know how it feels," she called after him.

Percival wiped his hands on a shop rag and moved to the next repair waiting on his stand, already assessing what the flat tire and bent spoke would require to fix properly. "Never a dull moment in this shop," he said, reaching for his tools once more.
