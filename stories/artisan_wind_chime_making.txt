Aluminum tubes hung suspended from Persimmon's workbench frame as she tested each one's pitch, cutting them to the precise lengths that would create the pentatonic scale her wind chime design specifically required. Tuned chimes demanded exact measurements.

Her apprentice, Balthazar, drilled the suspension holes into each tested tube, positioning them at the specific node points that would allow the tubes to resonate freely without dampening their carefully tuned pitch. "These holes are drilled and ready," he said.

Persimmon examined his drilling work, checking the hole placement against her calculations before approving the tubes for final assembly into the complete wind chime structure. "Perfect placement," she said. "That node point matters more than people realize."

Balthazar watched her continue testing and adjusting tube lengths, absorbing the subtle relationship between tube length and resulting pitch that governed this entire tuning process. "How precise does the cutting actually need to be?" he asked.

Persimmon continued her careful measurements, explaining how even small variations could throw off the pentatonic scale's carefully calibrated harmonic relationships. "Millimeters matter," she said. "That's the difference between music and noise."
