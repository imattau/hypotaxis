Buffalo horn shavings piled beneath Cassius's rasp as he shaped the powder flask's curved body, working the naturally dense material toward the smooth form traditional black powder shooters still sought for their historical firearms. Horn work demanded patience and specific tools.

His apprentice, Wilhelmina, prepared the brass fittings that would cap both ends of the flask, polishing them to a bright shine before their eventual attachment to Cassius's shaped horn body. "Fittings are polished and ready," she said.

Cassius examined the fittings she'd prepared, checking their fit against a test piece before approving them for permanent attachment to this particular flask's finished body. "Perfect fit," he said. "Let's get these mounted properly."

Wilhelmina helped position the brass cap while Cassius worked the attachment, both of them focused on achieving the secure, functional joint this flask would need for decades of practical use. "That's seated well," she observed.

Cassius finished securing the fitting and held the completed flask up to admire the natural horn's translucent amber glow against the workshop light. "Beautiful piece," he said. "This one's going to serve someone well for a very long time."
