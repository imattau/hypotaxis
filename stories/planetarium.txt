The dome overhead flickered to life with a slow sweep of simulated stars as Bertram adjusted the projector controls, double-checking the evening's show sequence one final time. School groups were already filing into the tiered seating, whispering excitedly in the dim blue light.

His assistant, Fatima, moved along the rows handing out worksheets, pausing to answer a curious kid's question about whether the sun was actually a star. "It is," she said, crouching to his eye level. "Just a lot closer than all the other ones you're about to see."

Bertram dimmed the house lights further and began the narration, his voice carrying easily through the dome's speakers as constellations bloomed slowly overhead. Fatima found a seat near the back, watching the rows of upturned faces rather than the show itself, gauging which parts were landing and which weren't.

Midway through the presentation, a technical glitch caused the projected sky to stutter and freeze, and a low murmur of disappointment rippled through the seats. Bertram kept talking smoothly through the pause, buying time while Fatima hurried up the side aisle toward the control booth.

By the time she reached the booth and reset the sequence, the sky resumed its slow rotation as if nothing had happened, and the murmur faded back into hushed attention. Bertram shot her a grateful glance across the dark auditorium before continuing the tour of the winter constellations.
