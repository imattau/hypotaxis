Fret wire pressed steadily into the guitar neck's slots as Balthazar worked methodically down the fingerboard, checking each fret's height and seating before moving to the next position along the instrument's neck. Custom guitar building demanded exacting tolerances.

His apprentice, Xiomara, filed the fret ends smooth, removing any sharp edges that might catch a player's hand during fast passages up and down the neck's full range. "These ends feel completely smooth now," she said.

Balthazar examined her filing work, running his own experienced fingers along the neck's edge to confirm no sharp spots remained that might compromise the instrument's playability. "Perfectly smooth," he said. "Players will really appreciate that attention."

Xiomara watched him continue pressing frets into position, absorbing the subtle technique that ensured each one seated perfectly level despite the neck's naturally curved fingerboard radius. "How do you keep them all so even?" she asked.

Balthazar continued his careful work, considering the familiar question from an apprentice genuinely trying to understand decades of accumulated technique. "Careful measurement and steady hands," he said. "Also checking twice before every single press."
